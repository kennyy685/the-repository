# Nocturne: final art direction for the AI hub (v27)

For `pages/crew-hq.html` + `pages/hub/scene.js`. Written 2026-09-28 from three competing directions (Nocturne, La
Maqueta, Calibre 01) and three judges. Nocturne scored highest (115). This file keeps its spine, grafts the best
of the other two, fixes every weakness the judges named, and folds in FilthE's design directive of 2026-09-28
(SPEC.md: "premium AI look, abstract generative forms, cinematic scroll, immersive WebGL, luxury type, dark
futuristic, feels alive").

Nothing FilthE approved goes away: every outfit, prop, the slide, the tube, the fun layer, the awareness scenes,
answer-in-the-hub, EN/ES, and the phone layout. This direction changes how it all looks, sounds and moves.

---

## 1. The idea

**A lit glass room in the Nebraska night.** Picture Hopper's *Nighthawks* and Shulman's photo of Case Study House #22:
a warm 3000K room is the only lit thing in a deep blue-graphite night (Whistler's *Nocturne*), and the tower under it
dissolves into black. Above the prairie horizon, behind the glass, the crew's **mind** drifts: a faint generative
weave of thread-thin light, like a slow aurora, whose speed, density and color come from the real work. It runs fast
when the crew is busy, goes calm when they sleep, and warms to ember when someone needs FilthE. The UI is museum wall
text: small, left-aligned, hairline and quiet, so the room carries the page. **Orange means one thing: you're
needed.** Brass is jewelry. Everything that moves is driven by real crew data. It is one screen on the MacBook, and
the trackpad moves the camera, not the page.

### The 3 signature moments

1. **The Call (one ember).** A robot needs FilthE, and the whole screen says so without a badge:
   - that robot's light strip turns knocker orange and breathes 12 times a minute (a 5 s cycle, like the PowerBook
     G4 sleep light);
   - the room's lamps ease down 12% over 1.2 s, leaving a warm pool of light on that robot, and it glides to "your
     spot";
   - the mind's threads warm toward ember above it;
   - at the top of the screen, the count window rolls to `01` and its dot breathes in the same rhythm.

   One click on the question, or one scroll, brings him to it.
2. **The Push-in (cinematic scroll).** On the trackpad over the room, scrolling is a dolly shot, not a page scroll:
   - Whole building, then the floor that needs him, then the robot itself (its wall label opens in the right
     column).
   - Keep scrolling and the **Ledger** (board, orders, memory, Right Hand, logs) rises over the room like a catalogue
     page, while the room behind dims to 40%: the lights go down as you leave the room.
   - Scroll back and it all reverses. The top band (Needs you, clock) never leaves the screen.
3. **Special Delivery (the answer).** He presses Yes:
   - the question note folds into a small brass capsule that rides the glass suction tube up to the King
     ("fwoop");
   - the robot that asked bows, its strip settles back to champagne, the pool fades and the lamps come back up;
   - a brass tick reads "Saved · the King has it / Guardado · el Rey ya lo tiene".

   Every later handoff between robots draws a streak of light through the mind from sender to receiver.

## 2. References

What we take from each, in one line. Grouped by world.

**Painting and light**
- Edward Hopper, *Nighthawks* (1942, Art Institute of Chicago): the warm room seen through plate glass against a
  dark street. This is the whole first screen, the lamp streaks on the glass, and view 5.
- James McNeill Whistler, *Nocturne: Blue and Gold, Old Battersea Bridge* (c.1872-75, Tate): the night palette,
  deep ink blue with one thin gold band at the horizon.
- Vilhelm Hammershøi, *Interior, Strandgade 30* (1901): quiet grey-blue daylight. The smoked-glass day sky and the
  calm of empty floor.
- Johannes Vermeer, *Officer and Laughing Girl* (c.1657): light from one side, never flat. The single raking key
  light.
- Giorgio Morandi, still lifes (1950s): few objects, grouped tight, muted. How each robot's job props sit on its desk.
- Agnes Martin, grid paintings (1960s-2000s): pale, slightly irregular marks. Town lights as brass pinpricks with
  2-4 px jitter.
- James Turrell, *Skyspaces*: the sky changes and the room stays constant. Robots and lamps never recolor with the
  hour; only the sky does.
- Hiroshi Sugimoto, *Seascapes* (1980-): one crisp, still horizon. Flat land, flat silhouettes, no detail.

**Architecture and interiors**
- Julius Shulman, *Case Study House #22* (1960): a glass box glowing at dusk above a dark city. Interior light
  balanced against the sky.
- Mies van der Rohe and Lilly Reich, *Barcelona Pavilion* (1929): honed travertine that glows. The upstairs floor and
  the plinth edge.
- Mies van der Rohe, *Farnsworth House* (1951): knife-edge joints and exact rhythm. Thin bronze mullions, one hover
  height for every robot, no z-fighting.
- Tadao Ando, *Church of the Light* (1989): light is the material. One low 3200K sun raking long shadows.
- John Pawson, *Minimum* (Phaidon, 1996): luxury is what you leave out. One linear pendant, three plants, 30% of each
  floor empty.
- Aman (Amangiri, 2009) and Aesop stores: calm arrival, one local material, small left-aligned labels. Our ink, our
  hairlines.
- Wes Anderson, *The Life Aquatic* (2004), the Belafonte cross-section: a building cut open with every room working.
  The poche cut faces with a brass reveal.

**Books and graphic design**
- Junichiro Tanizaki, *In Praise of Shadows* (1933): gold shows best in the dark. Shadow pockets between lamp pools;
  brass as jewelry.
- Dieter Rams, *Weniger, aber besser* and the ten principles: no new typefaces, honest motion, one thing at a time.
- Josef Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* (1981): the 12-column Ledger, the 4 px baseline, tabular
  numerals.
- MoMA / Tate tombstone wall labels: the robot card as a caption (catalogue number, name, medium line, facts).
- Richard Scarry, *What Do People Do All Day?* (1968): every figure doing one legible job, captioned on the picture.
  Name tags that add detail as you zoom, and one hero prop per desk.
- Stephen Biesty and Richard Platt, *Incredible Cross-Sections* (1992): leader-line callouts. The 1 px leader from a
  name tag to its robot.
- Otl Aicher, Munich 1972 pictograms: one stroke weight on one grid. The flat outfit avatars.

**Film**
- Roger Deakins, *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): every light is a practical you can see. Lamps are the key light on faces;
  no invisible fill.
- Spike Jonze and Hoyte van Hoytema, *Her* (2013): warmth inside a future. Warm graphite, never cold blue UI.
- Jacques Tati, *Playtime* (1967): an office as a grid of small simultaneous stories. Why the wide view stays calm and
  readable.

**Objects and instruments**
- Leica red dot, and Criterion's "one red mark, lots of quiet charcoal": knocker orange is reserved for "needs you".
- Apple PowerBook G4 breathing sleep light (US patent 6,658,577, about 12 breaths a minute): the ember breath.
- A. Lange & Söhne Lange 1 outsize date (1994): the needs count in a framed two-digit window whose digits roll.
- Braun ET 66 calculator (Lubs and Rams, 1987): one light key among quiet ones. The single ivory Send button.
- F1 broadcast timing tower (FOM): fixed order, a time-gap column in tabular figures. The crew list never reshuffles
  and shows time since each check-in.
- Hans Hilfiker, SBB railway clock (1944/1953): the seconds hand that waits at 12. A later extra for downstairs.
- Watch product photography with strip softboxes: the custom studio environment, so every ceramic head carries one
  clean long highlight.

**Generative and data art (for FilthE's "abstract generative forms")**
- Tyler Hobbs, *Fidenza* (2021): flow fields of curves that never cross. The mind's threads.
- Refik Anadol, *Unsupervised* (MoMA, 2022-23): data as pigment, moving in real time. The mind is the crew's actual
  activity, not decoration.
- The aurora seen across Nebraska on 10-11 May 2024 (NOAA G5 storm): a real Nebraska night sky that moved. Why the
  mind lives in the sky above the prairie.
- Olafur Eliasson, *The weather project* (Tate Modern, 2003): the sky as the installation.

**Motion and sound**
- Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, *The Illusion of Life* (1981): anticipation, follow-through, slow-in. The head leads
  the body.
- Pixar, *WALL-E* (2008), and Lucasfilm's BB-8: expression without a face. A visor tilt and a light strip carry
  emotion.
- Minute-repeater chimes and the felt-damped hotel desk bell: the optional "needs you" sound.

**Rendering**
- Khronos PBR Neutral tone mapper (2024; `THREE.NeutralToneMapping` in r169): keeps knocker orange and brass true
  where ACES pushes them to yellow.

**House bar**
- Aldaba landing page (`docs/design/product-brand/aldaba/index.html`): graphite `#111214`, one orange, Bricolage at
  opsz 96 with -0.03em tracking. Every token below starts there.

**The orange rule, stated once.** In the UI, `--ember` appears only on the count window, the needs chips, the waiting
glyph, the ask block in a card, a waiting bubble's edge and the mind's ember band. In the room, orange **light**
appears only on a waiting robot (strip, halo, the call pool's warm tint) and in the mind above it. The only orange
**object** is the Builder's matte hard hat (an approved outfit; it is never emissive). The slide inlay, the tube cap
ring, the "your spot" ring, the pod strips and the upstairs ledge light all turn brass or champagne.

**Top band (y 0-72, no box).**
- `--scrim-top` behind the whole width keeps text readable over any sky.
- Left, x 40: 28 px mark, "The Crew" (Bricolage 22) and a mono folio "HMP AI HUB · FREMONT, NE".
- Center, x 380-1040 (660 wide): the Needs strip.
  - The count window: two 18 x 32 cells with a 1 px `--brass` frame, digits in `--ember` when above 0, `--muted`
    `00` at zero.
  - The mono label "NEEDS YOU / TE NECESITAN".
  - Up to **two** question chips at once (44 tall, at most 300 wide), then `+n`.
- Right, ending at x 1400: live dot + clock + weather (mono 13), then the EN|ES segmented pair (6 px radius).

**Right column (x 1060-1400, y 88 to H-64).** No box and no blur. `--scrim-col` sits behind it. It has two states:
- **Crew list** (default): header 40, two floor subheads of 24, ten rows of 52 (46 when the viewport is under
  820 tall), and a 32 px scouts line.
- **Robot card open**: the list folds into the **crew rail**, one 40 px row of ten 28 px avatars in seat order, each
  with its status glyph. The card fills the rest of the column, about 560 px. All ten statuses stay visible, and a
  click on the rail switches robots.

**Room area** (the camera fit rect, `area()` in scene.js): `{x0:40, y0:88, x1:W-340-80, y1:H-64}`. At 1440 x 810 that
is 980 x 658, about 70% of the screen. It does not change size when the card opens, because the card lives in the
column. The camera reframes inside it.

## 6. 3D scene direction (`pages/hub/scene.js`)

### 6.1 Camera
- Keep the OrthographicCamera 3/4 cutaway.
  - `AZ0 = π/4 - .06` stays: an exact 45° flattens the cutaway into edge-on ambiguity.
  - `view.el = .60` stays: .55 hides more of the downstairs floor under the upper slab.
- Discrete view changes (view keys, Up/Down, card open, scroll detents) run a **750 ms easeInOutCubic tween** on the
  ortho rect, replacing the exponential lerp. Follow keeps its critically damped tracking (k = 5, no overshoot).
  Reduced motion cuts.
- Keys follow SPEC: 1 Dollhouse (Whole), 4 Ride-along (Follow), ↑/↓ floors. Keys 2, 3, 5-8 (Blueprint, Security cam,
  Through the window, Tilt-shift, Tour, Director) arrive with their builds.
- **Scroll dolly** (desktop, `pointer:fine`, the wheel over the stage only):
  - Detents: 0 Whole → 1 the focus robot's floor → 2 the focus robot (Follow framing + its card) → 3 Ledger up.
  - Focus robot: the first waiting, else the first stuck, else the selected robot, else the King.
  - Pinch (`ctrl+wheel`) maps the same way. Horizontal swipes are ignored.
  - Details are in 8.2.

### 6.2 Light rig (dusk and night values in section 3; `lightFromSky()` keeps lerping them)
- **Tone mapping:** `THREE.NeutralToneMapping`, exposure 1.0 (was ACES 1.3). Do this first and retune everything
  after. A/B against `AgXToneMapping` in one screenshot pair.
- **Key:** one DirectionalLight, the only shadow caster.
  - Dusk: a low 3200K sun raking in through the back glass, so shadows run 2-3x object height across the floor
    (Ando). Elevation is clamped at 8° or more (a 3-5° sun makes 10x shadows that the ±10 frustum clips).
  - Night: the key becomes a cool high moon at .25, so shadows never vanish.
  - Keep `PCFSoftShadowMap`, the 2048 map and the ±10 frustum. Delete `shadow.radius`; PCFSoft ignores it.
- **Hemisphere:** cut about 45% (dusk .50, night .30). Pools of lamp light make the room, with shadow pockets between
  them (Tanizaki). An evenly lit room reads as a render; chiaroscuro reads expensive.
- **Rim:** add a DirectionalLight from back-right, cool `#9fb4ff`, .22-.30, no shadow. It lifts the ceramic heads off
  the dark room, like the edge light on a watch bezel.
- **Practicals (Deakins):** every light has a visible fixture.
  - Replace the three white globe pendants over the Code lab (scene.js ~line 374) with **one linear brass pendant**:
    a 3.2 m bronze bar with an emissive opal underside (`MAT.opal`), backed by one PointLight (`#ffc38a`, 16, distance
    8, decay 2) under its middle. No RectAreaLight: it adds a per-fragment cost at DPR 2, and in r169 units it
    barely shows next to the existing PointLights.
  - Desk lamps become small brass task lamps with opal shades.
  - Keep the six lamp PointLights at the section 3 values.
- **The call pool:** one SpotLight created at init at intensity 0, so it never recompiles shaders.
  - When needs > 0 it moves above the first waiting robot (3.2 m up, angle .38, penumbra .7, `#ffc38a`) and rises to
    18 over 1.2 s, while the six lamps ease to 0.88x.
  - On answer it reverses over 1.2 s.
  - It is the only new light. Hover and selection use a CSS scrim instead (7.4).
- **Light budget:** hemi + key + rim + 6 lamps + call spot = 10. Never add a light at runtime.

### 6.3 Environment and materials
- **Studio environment:** replace `RoomEnvironment` with a custom PMREM studio built once at init via
  `pmrem.fromScene` (about 40 lines).
  - It is a black box with one long warm strip softbox overhead (8:1, `#ffe2c0`), one narrow cool strip on the window
    side (`#cfdcff`) and a dark bounce floor (`#2a2622`).
  - Every ceramic head, brass rail and the slide then carry one clean long highlight, the way watches are shot.
  - It costs nothing per frame. This is the single biggest "expensive" upgrade.
- **Materials:** use the section 3 values.
  - Travertine is honed. Its canvas texture gets about 400 deterministic 1-2 px pits and 6 faint veins.
  - Walnut: grain ±12% per plank, plus thin lighter "plane marks" at 60-100 px intervals.

  - Glass is neutral and nearly clear.
  - Sofa and armchair are cognac; the rug is plain oat.
- **Poche and precision (Farnsworth, the Belafonte section):**
  - Every cut face of the cutaway (slab edges, wall ends) is flat unlit `#141316` with a 12 mm brass reveal set 2 mm
    proud, with `polygonOffset` so nothing z-fights.
  - Mullions become bronze, .04 wide, evenly spaced, 2 mm off the glass. They cast no shadows: at 2048 over 20 m a
    texel is 10 cm, so a 4 cm mullion would shimmer.
  - Every robot hovers at exactly .20.

### 6.4 Post and "immersive WebGL", without a composer
The canvas is `alpha:true` over the CSS sky. An EffectComposer or UnrealBloomPass breaks that alpha (a black or
haloed sky edge), so there is none:
- **Bloom = sprites.** Additive `THREE.Sprite` halos (`glowTex`, `toneMapped:false`, `depthWrite:false`):
  - on every lamp fixture, `#ffd9ae` at .35;
  - on each robot strip, in its state color at .08-.30;
  - ember `.3 → .9` on the breath for a waiting robot.
- **Floor reflections = the polished downstairs stone** (real specular from the lamps and the studio strip), plus one
  vertically stretched additive sprite under each lamp and each downstairs robot (opacity .18, the wet-street neon
  of Hopper and Deakins). No Reflector pass.
- **Depth through the glass:**
  - `scene.fog = new THREE.Fog(land color, near, far)`, applied only to exterior materials (tower, fins, exterior
    slab; every interior material gets `fog:false`), so the tower dissolves.
  - `--scrim-foot` fades the bottom 22% of the stage to `--bg`.
  - A 4%-tall haze band at the CSS horizon.
- **Lamp streaks:** three vertical additive planes on the front glass with a warm gradient canvas, opacity .06,
  `renderOrder` above the glass: the Nighthawks reflection, with no reflection pass.
- **CSS finishing (over the canvas, under all UI):**
  - A static film grain: a 256 px SVG `feTurbulence` tile at opacity .035, `mix-blend-mode:soft-light`, no animation.
  - `.vig` strengthened to `rgba(5,6,10,.55)` at the edges.

### 6.5 The sky and the mind
- **Sky:** use the Whistler palette in section 3 and a flat Sugimoto horizon.

  - **Setup:**
    - It renders at 0.5x CSS size (720 x 405 at 1440) and runs at 30 fps.

    - needs > 0: a band of about 30% of the particles, centered on the waiting robot's screen x, warms to `--ember`

## 8. Motion, sound, and how live refresh feels

Tempo: weighted and calm, never bouncy. Everything that moves has a reason in the data (Rams: honest). Reduced motion
turns every glide, fly and fade into a cut, and the mind into a still.

### 8.1 Camera
- **View changes:** 750 ms easeInOutCubic on the ortho rect (the judges: 1.4 s is too slow for a tool clicked all
  day).
- **Card open:** a 750 ms glide that frames the robot inside the unchanged room area.
- **Follow:** critically damped (k = 5).
- **Director cuts** (when built): a 400 ms settle.

### 8.2 The scroll dolly
- **Input:** wheel deltas are normalized (`deltaMode` 1 → ×16) and accumulated.
  - 90 px of accumulated delta steps one detent.
  - Then input locks until the glide ends (750 ms) **and** the wheel has been quiet for 180 ms, so one trackpad
    swipe with inertia never skips two detents.
- **Scope:** only over the stage. Never inside the column, the card's Recent list or the Ledger. Inside the Ledger,
  scrolling scrolls the Ledger until its top, and then one more swipe up lowers it.
- **Detent 3 (Ledger):**
  - The sheet rises in 600 ms (`--ease-out`).
  - Its chapters fade in with an 8 px rise, 60 ms apart (the gentle parallax FilthE asked for).
  - The room drifts up 24 px and dims to 40%, and the renderer drops to 15 fps behind it.

### 8.3 Robots (Disney follow-through, BB-8's head leads)
- **Breathing:** hover bob ±6 mm on a 4.2 s sine, phase = seat × 1.37 rad, so no two robots breathe in sync.
- **Pose change:**
  - The strip brightens 150 ms early.
  - The head turns first (lerp dt × 4), the body follows 120 ms later (dt × 2.6), and props settle last.
  - Cape, tie and antenna trail on a spring (stiffness 120, damping .7).
- **Sleep:** a slow-in to the pod over the last 800 ms. Breathing slows to 6 s, and the strip fades to the pilot dot.
- **Roll call on arrival** (SPEC awareness): the robots look up at the camera in seat order, 70 ms apart, and the King
  nods last. It takes 1.2 s, once per visit.
- **Everything already approved stays at full charm:** idle habits, cursor-following heads, "your spot", the thank-you
  bow, double-click reactions, the coffee cup, the stand-up.

### 8.4 The Call (signature 1), timing
- **t = 0:** a robot enters `wait`.
- **0-1.2 s:** the six lamps ease to 0.88x, the call pool rises to 18, and the strip goes to ember and starts the
  5 s breath.
- **At the same time:** the count window rolls and its dot starts breathing on the same phase (`HUB.breath`), and the
  mind's ember band fades in.
- **0.4 s:** the robot turns its visor to the camera, then glides to "your spot" (existing path).
- **On answer:** everything reverses over 1.2 s, and Special Delivery runs.
- **Two or more waiting:** the pool sits on the first; the others breathe with no pool.

### 8.5 Special Delivery (signature 3), timing
- **0-240 ms:** the answered chip or card ask folds into a 12 px brass capsule.
- **The capsule's path:** it flies to the tube base over 500 ms on a bezier, rides up in 900 ms ("fwoop") and lands
  at the King's desk. The page calls `HUB.onAnswer(id)`; the scene plays it.
- **The asker:** it bows (400 ms), and its strip returns to champagne.
- **The receipt:** a brass ✓ plus "Saved · the King has it".
- **If the asker is hidden** (upper floor lifted in the Down view): the capsule rides anyway, and the bow is skipped.
- **Unchanged:** the answer still saves to `answers/<id>` + a handoff event and still wakes the King instantly, as
  today.

### 8.6 UI
- **Card:** enters in 280 ms (opacity 0 → 1, translateX 8 → 0, `--ease`) and leaves in 160 ms. The list folding into
  the crew rail takes 240 ms.
- **Needs strip:** a new chip drops in 6 px over 320 ms, and the chip plate opens in 200 ms.
- **Micro:** row hover 160 ms; buttons press to scale .98, no bounce.
- **Never:** badge pops, confetti in the UI, or orange flashes.

### 8.7 Live refresh
- Changed text cross-fades over 300 ms, and nothing reflows: rows are fixed and times tick in place.
- A new log row gets a 1 px brass left edge that fades over 1.2 s (no orange flash).
- Board rows that change group slide with FLIP (300 ms).
- The connection state is the live dot before the clock: sage when live, oxide when lost (steady, no blinking).
- New data never moves the camera by itself. Only The Call pulls attention, and it does so with light, not motion
  of the view.

### 8.8 Sound
Off by default, one switch. Web Audio synthesis, no files. At most one sound every 3 s, and silent when the tab is
hidden.
- **Needs you:** a felt-damped bell. Sine partials C5 523 Hz + G5 784 Hz, 8 ms attack, 1.1 s exponential decay,
  lowpass 3 kHz, -26 dBFS.
- **Answer sent:** a soft "fwoop", a sine sweeping 300 → 90 Hz over 250 ms, -30 dBFS.
- **Task done:** one C6 1047 Hz strike, 400 ms, -30 dBFS. No 1320 Hz chimes: they are shrill on MacBook speakers.

### 8.9 Frame budget
- **Targets:** 60 fps on an M1 Air at DPR 2; Intel Iris gets the existing DPR auto-drop.
- **Idle cap:** 30 fps when there is no camera tween, flight, hover or data change for 10 s (breathing is still smooth
  at 30).
- **Pauses:** the renderer stops when the tab is hidden and runs at 15 fps behind the Ledger.

## 10. Risks and guards

1. **Quiet reads as empty; FilthE loves the cute ("AWWW").**
   - Every outfit, prop, slide, tube and fun scene stays. Idle habits stay at full amplitude. Name tags are always
     on. The ceramic robots stay the brightest objects in the frame.
   - Before publishing, put v26 and v27 side by side at 1440 x 900 (night, blue hour, card open, ES).
2. **The strips change from orange to champagne when working.** This is the biggest taste call.
   - Guard: one flag, `PAL.stripMode = 'ember-only' | 'brand'`.
   - `'brand'` keeps every strip full orange (never dimmed). "Needs you" then rides on the breath, the halo, the
     pool, the mind and the walk to "your spot".
   - Show FilthE one screenshot of each; default `'ember-only'`.
3. **Too dark to read.**
   - `--scrim-top` and `--scrim-col` stay under all text. Muted text is 5.5:1 or better, and name tags carry a text
     shadow.
   - The Day sky is smoked, so the Needs chips hold 4.5:1.
   - The design gate's contrast check must pass in every sky state. Test at the dimmest MacBook brightness.
4. **The tone-mapping swap shifts every material.**
   - Do it first (step 1) and retune.
   - Target: the waiting strip pixel within ΔE 5 of `#f5883a` at every sky state; brass reads as brass, not yellow.
5. **MacBook GPU (M1 Air, Intel Iris at 2880 x 1800).**
   - No composer, no RectAreaLight, no transmission glass, no Reflector.
   - At most 10 lights, all created at init; the call pool starts at 0.
   - The only blur on desktop is the card.
   - The mind is a half-res 2D canvas at 30 fps. The grain is static.
   - Plus the 30 fps idle cap and `.lowfx`.
6. **Scroll-jacking annoys.**
   - Only over the stage, only with a fine pointer at 900 px or wider.
   - Detents with a lock, so it never skips. Fully reversible; Esc always returns to the room.
   - It never captures scroll inside the column or the Ledger. Horizontal swipes are ignored.
   - Reduced motion cuts. A one-time hint explains it.
7. **The mind reads as a screensaver.**
   - Alpha .28 at most, 0.6 px threads, ivory and brass only.
   - Only in the upper sky, masked clear of the horizon and never under UI text.
   - Every visible change maps to a real number (working share, sleep, stuck, needs, handoffs), so it means something.
   - If it still reads cheap in review, halve its alpha before cutting it.
8. **Sorting and flicker** (a transparent canvas, thin geometry, additive sprites):
   - glass `depthWrite:false` with `renderOrder`;
   - 2 mm offsets on mullions and reveals, `polygonOffset` on poche;
   - no mullion shadows;
   - overlay positions rounded to device pixels.
9. **Orange creep over time.** Add a design-gate grep that fails if `#f5883a` or `--ember` is used outside the
   allow-list:
   - page: `.count`, `.need*`, `.st-wait`, `.ask`, `.bub.waiting`, `#mind`;
   - scene: the strip, halo and call pool (the matte Builder hard hat is excepted).
10. **Keys inside the claude.ai frame.**
    - Keys work only after the page has focus and never while typing; read `e.key`.
    - No key sends an answer: A only focuses Yes, and ⏎ confirms the focused button.
11. **Spanish runs about 25% longer.**
    - The display name clamps from 34 to 28 with `text-wrap:balance`.
    - Chips and rows ellipsize, with the full text in `title` and `aria-label`.
    - No codes to translate.
12. **The Ledger hides things.**
    - The top band (Needs you) is always visible above it.
    - The sheet is real DOM (focusable, and ⌘F finds text while it is open).
    - The phone keeps the plain scrolling page.
13. **Data.** Days 1-2 need no contract change. The lap-time extra needs a King pickup timestamp. Sample and
    placeholder text stays clean: "registered", never "licensed"; nothing about deductibles; never "we handle your
    claim".

**Rejected on purpose** (from the three directions and the research):
- Tilt-shift blur bands on the default view, and the lighten/darken film grade: GPU cost, Safari mask bugs, lifted
  navy blacks.
- Pastel props, a rose blocked color, lilac sleep.
- The letterbox "Screening" auto-starting after 3 minutes idle: it hijacks a dashboard left open all day.
- "Whee!" sound-words.
- The ⌘K palette, three-letter crew codes and single-key answers.
- A true-isometric camera and the 20 s snap-back.
- UnrealBloomPass, BokehPass and a Reflector pass on the alpha canvas.
- The 24-hour scrub timeline (it needs an event-history engine).
- A saturated F1 green and red, or any blue "in progress" family.
- Bounce easing on numbers.
- Ghosted 40% props.
- A 1320 Hz chime.
- The orange SBB seconds hand.

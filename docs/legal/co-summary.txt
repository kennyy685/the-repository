1. Deductible waiver/rebate - C.R.S. 6-22-105 (from SB 12-038, 2012)
   Paraphrase: "A roofing contractor that performs roofing work, the payment for which will be made
   from the proceeds of a property and casualty insurance policy...shall not advertise or promise to
   pay, waive, or rebate all or part of any insurance deductible applicable to the claim." If
   violated, the insurer need not consider that contractor's estimate, and the property owner or
   insurer can sue the contractor for damages.
   Source: law.justia.com CRS 6-22-105; coloradoroofing.org; redhawkroofing.com.

2. Home-solicitation cancel right - C.R.S. 5-3-402 (Consumer Credit Code, Home Solicitation Sales
   part)
   Paraphrase: buyer may cancel by midnight of the 3rd business day after signing. Notice by mail is
   effective on deposit. Emergency-services exception requires a separate signed statement AND the
   seller must have made a "substantial beginning of performance" before cancellation notice.
   NOTE: Colorado's roofing deductible law lives in Title 6, Article 22 (a roofing-specific act,
   separate from the general home-solicitation Title 5 cancel-right) - two different titles to keep
   straight if HMP ever expands there. No storm-specific extension found for the general cancel
   right.
   Source: colorado.public.law CRS 5-3-402; hollingtonlawfirm.com.

3. Contractor registration/licensing - Colorado Roofing Contractor Registration Act, C.R.S.
   12-150-101 et seq. STATEWIDE REGISTRATION with DORA (not a trade exam/license) - minimum
   liability insurance + surety bond, no trade exam. No statewide roofing license; local
   municipalities (e.g. Denver) layer on their own license/certificate requirements. Use
   "registered," not "licensed," for the statewide piece.
   Source: coloradoroofing.org "Do Contractors Need a Colorado Roofing License?"; procore.com.

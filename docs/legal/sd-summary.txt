1. Deductible waiver/rebate - SDCL Sec. 58-33-66
   Paraphrase: "No contractor providing residential roofing goods and services to any person or
   entity may advertise or promise to pay or rebate all or part of any applicable insurance
   deductible." UNIQUE REMEDY vs. other states: violation makes the CONTRACT ITSELF null and void,
   not just a fine/misdemeanor or a right for the insurer to disregard the estimate. Worth flagging
   to FilthE as the strictest downside of any state on this list, if HMP ever expands there.
   Source: propertyinsurancecoveragelaw blog (Colorado SB 12-038 companion doc referencing similar
   language); direct SDCL search summary.

2. Storm-specific cancel extension - SDCL Sec. 58-33-67 (UNIQUE TO SD ON THIS LIST)
   Paraphrase: a homeowner who signed a written storm-damage repair contract for residential roofing
   may cancel within 72 HOURS after being notified that the property insurer has denied coverage, in
   whole or in part - a claim-outcome-triggered cancel right layered on top of the general 3-day
   cooling-off period below. No other state in this batch had a comparable rule surface in this
   round's searches - double check this is real and current before repeating it anywhere.
   Source: search-summary citing SDCL 58-33-67 directly.

3. General home-solicitation ("door to door") cancel right - SDCL 37-24-5.1 to 37-24-5.7
   Paraphrase: buyer may cancel any time before midnight of the 3rd business day after the
   transaction; seller must furnish a duplicate "NOTICE OF CANCELLATION" form; on cancellation the
   seller must return trade-ins/payments/negotiable instruments within 10 business days.
   Source: SD Dept. of Consumer Protection "Door to Door Sales" statute compilation (PDF, not
   opened directly - egress-blocked); FindLaw/kirschenbaumesq.com summary of 37-24-5.3.

4. Contractor registration/licensing - NO statewide contractor license for roofers in South Dakota.
   The state requires a contractor's EXCISE TAX license (2% gross-receipts tax on construction, all
   trades) from the Dept. of Revenue - a tax registration, not a trade credential. Only electricians
   and plumbers carry state-issued trade licenses. Local city/county licenses may apply (e.g. Rapid
   City has its own roofing-contractor ordinance).
   Source: contractorsliability.com SD general contractor guide; procore.com SD contractor license
   guide; Rapid City Municipal Code 15.04.140 (roofing-specific local ordinance, referenced not read).

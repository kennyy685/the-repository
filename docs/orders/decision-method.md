# How the crew decides (FilthE, 2026-09-27)

FilthE: "You can create scenarios, check perspectives, find ways to think better to make decisions better." So every
real decision (what to build next, a legal or money call, what to tell FilthE to do) runs through these steps before
it reaches him. Small, reversible calls get steps 1 and 6 only. He gets the result, never the homework.

1. **Look it up first.** If the web or the repo can answer it, don't ask a person (CLAUDE.md rule). Primary sources for
   law (nebraskalegislature.gov, NE DOI), `data/benchmarks.json` for sales numbers, `docs/research/` for past rounds.
2. **Outside view (the numbers from people who already did it).** Before guessing "how long / how many / will it
   work", find how it went for similar roofers and start from that average (reference class forecasting: Kahneman,
   Flyvbjerg). Our own plans are always too optimistic.
3. **Walk around the table.** Say what each person would think, in one line each: the homeowner at the door (tired of
   storm chasers?), the adjuster, a competing roofer, the boss (crews, cash, materials), FilthE at 5 PM, tired, in the
   sun, and a Nebraska AG investigator reading our script. A choice that only works from one seat is a bad choice.
4. **Pre-mortem.** "It's 3 months from now and this failed badly. Why?" List the reasons in past tense, then fix
   the top 2 now (Gary Klein, HBR 2007: imagining it already failed surfaces about 30% more risks).
5. **Two scenarios, side by side.** Do it now vs. later (or A vs. B): what happens in each, with the numbers from
   step 2. Name the one thing that expires (a fresh storm, the season, a deadline).
6. **One-way or two-way door?** (Bezos, 2015 letter.) Reversible calls (a screen layout, a knock day, a script line):
   decide fast, try it, adjust. One-way calls (signing, money, anything legal, deleting data, promises HMP must keep):
   slow down, and only these go to FilthE or the boss, as a single yes/no with our recommendation.

Output to FilthE: the decision, the one reason, and what would change our mind. Five lines or fewer.

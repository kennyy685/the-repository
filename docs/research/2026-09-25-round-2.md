## Claims 101 (read this first, before the steps/tracker/checklist)
**Glossary:** Claim = homeowner asks their insurer to pay for storm damage. Adjuster = the insurer's
inspector; decides what they'll pay. Deductible = the fixed amount the homeowner always pays - HMP
can never cover, waive or discount it. ACV = the first check (repair cost minus depreciation). RCV =
full cost of a new roof, no age discount. Depreciation = value held back for the roof's age until the
job is done. Supplement = a follow-up request, with photos, for items the scope missed. Scope = the
insurer's written list of what they'll pay for. Public adjuster = a separately-licensed pro who can
negotiate a claim - HMP is not one and never negotiates.
**Steps:** 1) Storm hits; HMP inspects, shows the homeowner photos. 2) Homeowner calls their own
insurer and files (their job, not HMP's). 3) Insurer sends an adjuster. 4) HMP meets the adjuster,
points out damage, shares photos/measurements - never argues price. 5) Insurer sends the scope + ACV
check. 6) HMP signs the job contract; if the scope missed something, sends a written supplement
request with photos. 7) HMP orders materials, does the roof. 8) HMP sends proof of completion; insurer
releases the depreciation check. Done. Full cycle is usually 45-90 days.
**Money:** both checks come from the insurer (sometimes jointly with the mortgage company, who signs
first). The deductible always comes from the homeowner - HMP never touches it.
**HMP can:** inspect, document, meet the adjuster, request supplements in writing, do the work.
**HMP cannot:** cover/waive/discount the deductible, promise what insurance will pay, or negotiate the
settlement (that's public-adjuster work).

## Claim + money tracker fields (for T34)
Claim #; insurer + adjuster name/phone; date of loss; adjuster meeting date; scope received; RCV
total; ACV amount + received + deposited; depreciation held; mortgage co + check sent/returned;
supplements (date asked, approved); code items (permit #); material order date/supplier/cost; install
start/end; completion cert sent; depreciation check amount/date; total collected vs. contract;
deductible (owed by the homeowner, never covered by HMP); status.

## Adjuster-meeting checklist (for T40)
1. Claim #, date of loss, declarations page. 2. Date-stamped photos/video (all 4 slopes, gutters,
soft metals, interior stains). 3. Roof measurements/diagram. 4. Xactimate-style line-item scope.
5. NOAA/NWS storm-date proof (HailHunter's own hail report for the address). 6. Likely-missed code
items (drip edge, ice & water shield, starter strip, permit #) - flag them, don't demand. 7. Notepad
for every item the adjuster calls out. 8. Swap contacts; the script is "we document the damage, we
don't set the settlement."

## Nebraska law notes - VERIFY before contract use
Search-summarized from the Insured Homeowners Protection Act (44-8601 to 44-8608) and the Public
Adjuster Licensing Act - not opened at nebraskalegislature.gov this round:
- 44-8604: never rebate, waive or discount the deductible.
- 44-8607: any contract/estimate paid from insurance needs a large-print (14-pt, all-caps) deductible
  notice, signed by the homeowner.
- 44-8603: homeowner has 3 business days to cancel; refund within 10 days.
- 44-8608: violating the Act voids the contract.
- Public Adjuster Licensing Act: no negotiating or settling a claim without that separate license.

## EN/ES door opener + objections (ready for the practice-door tool and for when FilthE starts)
**EN opener:** "Hi, I'm Kenny with HMP Siding & Roofing - we're local, out of Fremont. We've been
checking roofs on this street for damage from the [date] hailstorm. Mind if I take a quick free look
at yours? No obligation, takes about 10 minutes."
**ES opener:** "Hola, soy Kenny de HMP Siding & Roofing, somos de Fremont. Hemos estado revisando
techos en esta calle por danos de la tormenta de granizo del [fecha]. Le parece si reviso el suyo
rapido? Es gratis, sin compromiso, toma unos 10 minutos."
**Objections:** Spouse not home - "Let me look now so I have real photos to show you both tonight. No
pressure to decide today." Insurance will drop me - "I can't speak for your policy - I'm just here to
show you what's on your roof, free, so you have the facts either way." Someone already came by -
"Good, more eyes never hurt - did they get on the roof or just look from the ground? Happy to give a
free second look." Renting - "Can I get your landlord's or property manager's name and number?" Not
interested - "No worries - mind if I leave this bilingual card?" then leave and move on.
**Numbers (industry blogs, unaudited):** beginners ~40 doors/day, 30-40% answer, knock-to-inspection
~1% for beginners (2-5% for top reps), 3 passes reach ~90% of a block, inspection-to-signed 15-20%
average. **First week:** days 1-2 role-play only (EN and ES, boss as skeptical homeowner, or the
practice-door tool); day 3, 5-10 doors with a partner; day 4, 10-15 solo on an easy street; days 5-7,
build to 30-40/day with a 5-minute debrief each night.

## Watch out
- More features can still replace selling - these are picked because each one teaches sales/claims or
  needs zero knocking, not because building is free of that risk.
- Skip "No Soliciting" doors when he does start; never say insurance will cover it; never rebate/waive
  a deductible; never negotiate a claim.
- Reviews: never pay for or filter them (FTC rule). No cash referral fees to agents/realtors without
  checking the rules first (rebating/fee-splitting risk).
- No cold texts, no bought lists, no mailers - hand-delivered notes and QR-to-text (homeowner-initiated
  after they take the hanger) are fine; unsolicited texting is not.
- The claims statute citations above are unverified - don't ship the contract template until someone
  checks 44-8601 to 44-8608 at nebraskalegislature.gov.

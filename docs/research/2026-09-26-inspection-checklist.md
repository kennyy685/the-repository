# Inspection + photo checklist for a new inspector (2026-09-26)
Documentation only: never state a verdict and never negotiate. Sources: haagglobal.com test square method; hrti.com; gorillaroofing.com; rooftechnologies.com; myroofingpal.com; advantage-construction.com; vanguardroofingcompany.com; hinarratives.com (blisters vs hail); embrysroofing.com; nachi.org; inspectorproinsurance.com.

## Walking order (each one becomes a photo slot in the app)
1. Ground: the house number, then a wide shot of each of the 4 sides.
2. Window screens + AC fins (dents or tears show hail and its direction).
3. Soft metals: vents, flashing, gutter straps.
4. Gutters/downspouts: round dings; granules inside the gutter.
5. Siding, storm side first. Vinyl: cracks, chips, round holes. Fiber cement: chipped paint and hairline cracks (look closely).
6. Chalk-circle every hit BEFORE the photo.
7. Ladder: level ground, 3-point contact, extending ~3 ft above the roofline.
8. Roof: check pitch and wetness first. Harness above ~6:12; skip if wet, icy, mossy or windy.
9. One 10×10 ft test square per slope, away from trees; circle and count the hits (~8+ per square on 2+ slopes is often treated as damage, but it varies by insurer: record the count, not a verdict).
10. Photo each square: wide (with slope label) → medium → close-up with a coin or tape for scale.

## Hail vs not hail
- Hail: random round hits, soft bruise, granules knocked off in a crater.
- Blistering: small bubbles spread evenly, no bruise, from heat/age.
- Wear/mechanical: linear scuffs near valleys, vents and foot paths.
- Vinyl needs ~1"+ hail; the cracks are sharp, unlike wavy age cracks.
- Fiber cement needs ~1.5"+ hail; match it with dents on metal on the same side.

## Safety
3-point contact; a harness above ~6:12; never wet, icy or windy roofs; tell someone before you climb and when you're down.

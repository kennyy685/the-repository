# Off-season plan, Oct-Mar (2026-09-26)
Hail in Nebraska: June ~31%, May ~21%, July ~19% of reports; the Omaha peak is May 1 - June 15. Claims are usually allowed about 12 months (some policies 24) after the storm: it's a POLICY term, so never promise ("check your policy, usually about a year").
- **Oct:** re-knock every storm from the last ~10-11 months that hasn't been worked; the route prefers storm walks, then everyday.
- **Nov:** daylight is the limit (sunset ~5 PM): knock 3:30-5:30 PM weekdays + Saturday late morning; never after dark. Storm first, then more everyday; end by dusk.
- **Dec:** light knocking; phone follow-ups on warm leads and estimates that didn't close; ask Nastase/VTR for winter referral work.
- **Jan-Feb:** estimates and relationships; commercial/leasing-office business calls; old-house siding estimates for spring. Material cold-weather floors: see the table below.
- **Mar:** convert winter estimates into signed spring jobs; everyday first, switching to storm-first at the first spring hail report.
Tip: track each claim's "file-by" date (about date of loss + 12 months) in Money.

## Cold-weather build limits (added 2026-09-27, round 57)

| Material | Floor | Why | Verified? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asphalt shingles | ~40°F+ to self-seal | Below that, the self-seal strip may not activate right away - hand-seal each tab with a dab of roofing cement instead | Partly. NRCA's own trade-press article (professionalroofing.net, PDF fetched directly this round) confirms sealing strips "may not immediately activate if installed in cold weather" and recommends hand-tabbing [10]. The exact "40°F" number is the industry-standard figure everywhere (ARMA, shingle makers) but ARMA's own page (asphaltroofing.org) was **blocked to direct fetch again this round** - same gap as prior rounds, unverified against the manufacturer-association's own page. |
| Vinyl siding | Gets brittle starting ~35-40°F; most makers won't warrant work below ~20°F | Cold vinyl needs wider expansion gaps at the nail slots and slower, sharper cuts, or it cracks | **Unverified.** vinylsiding.org (Vinyl Siding Institute) was blocked to direct fetch this round. The figure is repeated consistently across independent contractor sites in both round 52 and this round, but never confirmed on VSI's own installation manual (a paid document, not a free page). |
| James Hardie ColorPlus / pre-finished siding | Not below ~30°F | Caulk and touch-up paint won't bond right at or below 30°F, even if the boards themselves install fine | **Unverified against James Hardie's own guide**, same gap as round 52. jameshardie.com's root, blog and FAQ pages are directly reachable this round, but the actual "Best Practices" installation PDF is not (its technical-docs page needs JavaScript to list documents, and direct PDF-mirror URLs on other sites were all blocked). The 30°F figure is consistent across several independent contractor sites that say they're citing Hardie's guide - moderate confidence, not primary-source-verified. |
| Plain (unfinished) fiber cement | Can go colder, roughly 20°F, with the right cold-rated caulk and an experienced crew | Siding stored outside overnight can be colder than the air temperature even on a "warm enough" day - check the material with a thermometer, not just the forecast | Same sourcing and confidence as the ColorPlus row above. |

**One-line rule for FilthE and the boss:** if the forecast for every work day of the install won't hold above the coldest material on that job's floor (30°F for Hardie ColorPlus, otherwise ~35-40°F), **book the contract and deposit now, but schedule the actual build for the first stretch of spring weather above that floor** - never promise a winter build date the weather can't back up.

Sources: mapscaping.com Nebraska storm reports; asphaltroofing.org cold-weather install; uphelp.org Nebraska deadlines; propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com; howbump.blog best times; hailprotector.com hail season guide; jobnimbus.com off-season marketing; [10] NRCA "Cold Weather Application," Professional Roofing, Dec. 2012 (professionalroofing.net/nrca.net, PDF fetched directly, round 57) - see `docs/research/2026-09-27-round-57.md` for the full round-57 sourcing on the table above.

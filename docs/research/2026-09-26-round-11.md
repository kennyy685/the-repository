# Research round 11 (2026-09-26): tab structure + home screen, learned from field-sales apps

Sources: help centers/app stores for SalesRabbit, RepCard, CompanyCam, Jobber, Housecall Pro (search summaries with some
opened help pages); Apple HIG and Material Design pages opened directly for the nav rules; field-service-app design
articles (glove/sun/offline). AccuLynx, JobNimbus, Roofr mobile nav specifics were **not confirmed** - general reviews only,
no opened screenshots - marked unproven below.

## What the best apps actually do
- **SalesRabbit**: home screen toggles between "My Stats" (leads created today/30 days) and "My Appointments" (calendar).
  Swipe between tabs on a lead's detail screen - fewer taps to move through a lead. [confirmed, help center]
- **RepCard**: gamification (leaderboards, "doors knocked") lives inside the app, mobile-first; competitions are
  configured on the web, played on mobile. [confirmed, vendor]
- **CompanyCam**: recent redesign explicitly "streamlined home and project screens... grouped features," camera opens
  fast, a dedicated Map tab. [confirmed, vendor]
- **Jobber**: Home tab opens to a **map of today's appointments**, first job pinned and centered - not a list, not a
  dashboard. Bottom bar: Home, Schedule, then icons for activity/messages, and a "More" catch-all. [confirmed, help center]
- **Housecall Pro**: bottom bar includes Inbox and Customers as first-class tabs, plus "More". [confirmed, help center]
- **Apple HIG**: use **3-5 tabs** on iPhone; every extra tab adds complexity; avoid an "overflow/More" tab if you can,
  because people rarely dig into it. [confirmed, opened]
- **Material Design**: bottom nav destinations should be of **equal importance** and top-level; **never more than 5**;
  each icon must go straight to a screen, never open a menu. Bottom placement chosen specifically for one-thumb reach.
  [confirmed, opened]
- **Field-app design writing** (multiple practitioner sources, consistent with each other): common tasks should take
  **2-3 taps, not 7**; touch targets 44-56px for glove use; high contrast + 16-18px+ text for sun glare; **offline-first**
  - capture now, sync later, never lock the worker out for no signal. [consistent across sources, treated as good practice
  though not a single controlled study]

## Recommended HMP App structure (4 tabs + the King chat)
**Today | Leads | Money | Crew** is already the plan (per CLAUDE.md). This research says: keep it at 4, and get the
*home screen inside "Today"* right - that's where the apps above put all their effort.

**Today tab home screen, in this order (matches Jobber's "map + today's list" pattern, adapted to a walk not a truck route):**
1. One line: where to knock right now (today's turf/area) - already O0's job.
2. The walk list, **house-only, in order** - already built (`todaywalk`).
3. One tap per door (Not home / No / Interested / Booked) - already the plan; keep it exactly 4 buttons, no sub-menu
   (Material's "each icon goes straight to a screen" rule applies to buttons too: no picking a reason on a second screen
   mid-walk - collect detail later, in Leads).
4. The King chat box, always reachable from Today, not a separate tab - the "log it by talking" behavior already planned.

**Leads tab**: the 9-stage pipeline + hot leads, one list, filterable, not multiple screens deep. Follow-up reminders
(round 9) surface here, not buried in a settings screen.

**Money tab**: Claim Tracker + quick estimate + invoices/paid status. Anything with a number a homeowner or adjuster will
ask about lives here.

**Crew tab**: the team board (already decided, "HMP Pro Dark," a card per AI).

## Move / merge / cut rules for the designer's audit
1. **Move** anything that's "for later, rarely tapped" (settings, registration #, print kit links, help/about) into one
   **"More" screen off Crew or a profile icon** - never its own bottom tab. Matches HIG/Material: bottom tabs are only for
   equal-importance, frequent things.
2. **Merge** any screen that only shows a number FilthE already sees elsewhere (e.g., a duplicate lead count on a
   dashboard vs. the Leads tab itself) - one source of truth per number, shown in one place.
3. **Cut** any confirmation screen or "are you sure" between a door tap and the next door. Door taps must be instant -
   log first, allow undo, never block the walk.
4. **Cut** nested menus deeper than 2 taps for anything used *during* a knock (door result, calling a business line).
   Reserve deeper nesting for things done at the office/at night (reviewing a full claim history, editing prices).
5. **Move** the King/chat box so it's reachable from every tab (a floating button or a persistent strip), not something
   you have to leave your task to find - it's the one thing meant to be used constantly, mid-sale.
6. **Merge** "Storms" and "Everyday" walk info into one "why this house" line already inside Today, instead of a separate
   tab or screen to check before heading out (per round 8's "why this house" idea - this round confirms it belongs on
   the home screen, not one tap deeper).
7. Keep every screen used mid-knock at **44px+ touch targets and high-contrast text** - test it outdoors, in gloves, in
   October/November when knocking happens 3:30-5:30 PM with the sun low.
8. Keep the walk **available offline**: if a screen needs live internet to show the next door, that's a bug per this
   round's field-app research, not a nice-to-have.

## Watch out
- AccuLynx/JobNimbus/Roofr specific mobile nav layouts are **unproven this round** - reviews mention "easy tabs" and
  "limited mobile app" but no opened screenshots. If the app team wants to copy a specific detail from them, verify it
  by installing the trial app first.
- Leaderboards/gamification (RepCard) are confirmed to exist and to be liked by some users, but round 8 already flagged
  they backfire on a team of one salesman - don't add just because a competitor has it.
- Don't chase "more tabs = more features" - HIG and Material both treat 5 as a hard ceiling for good reason (cramped taps,
  overflow menus nobody opens).

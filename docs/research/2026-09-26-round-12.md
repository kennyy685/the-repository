# Research round 12 (2026-09-26): bilingual edge + building a sellable product

## Bottom line
1. **The Spanish opportunity is real and undersold in the industry.** Latinos are ~63% of roofers nationally and over
   70% of new labor-force growth in roofing since 2020 (BLS-sourced figures) - yet the round found **no evidence any
   named competitor app (SalesRabbit, JobNimbus, AccuLynx, Roofr, RepCard, CompanyCam) markets strong bilingual/Spanish
   features**. That's a real gap HMP can own, not just a nice-to-have.
2. **AI translation is safe for live conversation help and everyday copy, dangerous for legal text.** Every source on
   legal/court translation says the same thing: AI drafts, a human (or a fixed, pre-verified translation) finalizes -
   never let a live AI re-translate a legal notice on the fly.
3. **Nebraska law already requires this** (69-1604(3), confirmed 2026-09-26 in CLAUDE.md): since HMP sells in Spanish,
   the cancellation notice must be given in **both** languages. That's a floor, not the ceiling - the whole app should be
   built the same way: fixed, verified Spanish for anything legal; AI help for everything conversational.
4. **Vertical SaaS sold-later companies (ServiceTitan) started exactly like this** - built by/for one family's trade
   business, sold once it worked, then made generic (multi-company, configurable) for other contractors. The lesson for
   HMP: keep building for real jobs first, but stop hardcoding HMP-only facts once the app works.

## (a) Bilingual / translation
**Live conversation help, at the table or on the phone:**
- Use AI translation as a **draft or an aid**, never the final word on anything binding - confirmed across legal/court
  translation guidance (medical/legal AI translation risk pieces, National Center for State Courts guidance on court
  interpretation). Courts specifically say AI should not replace a human interpreter in real time for anything binding.
- A **glossary of fixed terms** (deductible, adjuster, depreciation, ACV, RCV, supplement, registered - not "licensed")
  should be forced into any AI translation feature so it never invents a different word for the same legal concept
  mid-conversation. This is standard practice in legal-translation QA (a "termbase" is exactly this).
- **Reading documents aloud in Spanish**: fine for anything already pre-translated and verified (the contract, the
  notices) - the app reads the *fixed* Spanish text aloud, it does not generate new Spanish on the fly for legal pages.
- **Multi-layer check for anything legal**: draft -> a second read against the source -> a check against the actual
  intent/statute -> a formatting check. HMP's version of this: any legal Spanish text gets FilthE's read-back check
  (he's bilingual) before it's used, same as the English side already waits on the lawyer.

**Market**: Hispanic workers are 31.9% of the whole construction labor force (up from 23.6% in 2010) and specifically
63% of roofers in 2024, with over 70% of new labor-force growth in roofing since 2020 being Latino (BLS-sourced,
multiple outlets citing the same figures). No hard number was found for Spanish-speaking *homeowners* specifically in
Nebraska/Fremont's roofing customer base - that's a genuine unknown, not proven.

**Competitors**: search found integrations and reviews for SalesRabbit/JobNimbus/AccuLynx but **no page claiming Spanish
UI or bilingual selling tools** for any of them. Unverified as an absence (absence of evidence isn't proof), but it lines
up with FilthE's own instinct that this is HMP's edge - worth being loud about it once the app has it solid.

## (b) Building it to sell later
**ServiceTitan's origin (confirmed, opened multiple sources)**: two software engineers, sons of a contractor and a
plumber, built the tool for their own fathers' businesses first (2007-2012), then generalized it into a platform for
HVAC/plumbing/electrical and beyond. The pattern: **solve your own real problem first, generalize the software second.**
That already matches HMP's plan - keep it.

**What to build now, before selling later, so the app doesn't have to be rebuilt:**
1. **Settings instead of hardcoded HMP facts.** Company name, logo colors, phone numbers, service area radius (250mi
   storms / 120mi doors), price sheet, glossary terms, and legal notice text should live in one config/settings object
   the app reads - not typed into templates in a dozen places. (Confirmed as standard multi-tenant SaaS advice: "hardcoded
   tenant limits are what create the rework bills six months into growth.")
2. **One company = one row, not baked into the schema.** Even with only HMP as a user today, design the database so a
   `company_id` (or similar) could exist later without a rewrite - doesn't mean building multi-company support now, just
   not painting into a corner.
3. **The bilingual glossary and legal-text system (above) is itself a sellable feature** - if it's built as fixed,
   swappable text tied to config rather than hand-typed per page, a second roofing company (English-only, or Spanish +
   another language) could reuse the same system.
4. **Pricing models for later**: vertical SaaS (ServiceTitan and peers) commonly charge per-seat or per-location
   subscription, sometimes with a % of transaction volume add-on for payments. Not a decision for now - just don't
   architect anything that assumes "there is exactly one company, forever."

## Watch out
- Never let translation "help" touch anything that changes a legal obligation - the deductible notice, the cancel right,
  the contract price. Those stay fixed, verified text in both languages, matching this round's #2 finding.
- Nothing here suggests changing what HMP tells homeowners about deductibles or claims - the bilingual push is about
  clarity and trust, never about persuasion tactics that cross 44-8604 or public-adjuster lines.
- The "sellable product" angle is long-term (FilthE's own words, 2026-09-26) - don't let it slow down O0/O7 work now.

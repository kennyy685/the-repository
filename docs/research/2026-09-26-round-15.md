## Bottom line
1. **The money is lost in the gaps between checks, not in the sale.** Missed supplements, un-asked-for recoverable
   depreciation, and slow mortgage-check endorsement are the three named leaks across every source found. All three
   are fixable with a checklist and reminders, not new skills.
2. **Referral rewards to the person who refers are legal and common** - the Nebraska rule (44-8604, confirmed again
   this round via search summary of the statute text) bars rebating **the referred customer's own insurance
   deductible** or paying **an insured** tied to their own claim. A thank-you gift to a *third party* who sent a
   friend, for a job that isn't their own claim, is a different thing and is standard industry practice. Still: no
   cash tied to "get your claim approved," and nothing that reads as paying someone to influence their own claim.

## (a) Claims + money checklist

**Where money leaks, in order of how often named:**
1. **Missed supplements** - the initial adjuster estimate misses items; contractors that don't re-submit with photos
   leave $3,000-$5,000 on the table per claim (search-summary figure, unproven/unaudited). Supplements are normal,
   not adversarial, and most insurers accept a supplement within ~180 days of the first payment.
2. **Recoverable depreciation never requested** - the insurer holds back depreciation (RCV - depreciation = ACV, the
   first check) until the job is done and the final invoice is sent in. If nobody sends the invoice and asks, that
   money sits unclaimed.
3. **Mortgage-company check endorsement delay** - when the insurer's check is made out to the homeowner **and** their
   mortgage company, the mortgage company must endorse it before funds release: adds roughly 1-3 weeks. Fix: start
   that paperwork the day the check arrives (signed contract + request to the mortgage company), don't wait for
   material order or install date.
4. **Overhead & Profit (O&P) stripped from the estimate** - a 10%+10% markup that applies when 3+ trades need
   coordinating (the "three-trade rule"); carriers often remove it claiming the contractor "self-performs." Knowing
   to ask for it, and why, is a real skill gap for a new salesman.

**Money checklist by claim stage (for the app - one line per stage, "what to check/chase"):**
| Stage | What to check / chase |
|---|---|
| Inspected | Photos of every damaged slope/side, dated; note any pre-existing damage separately |
| Claim filed | Claim number, adjuster name/phone, date of loss on file |
| Adjuster set | Be present at the adjuster meeting; bring the photo set |
| Scope in | Compare the insurer's scope line-by-line against the photos - flag anything missing (this is where a supplement starts) |
| Signed | Contract has the deductible notice (44-8607, already verified text) and the 3-day cancel notice, both languages |
| Supplement | File within the window (roughly 180 days of first payment); attach photos + a note on why each added item is needed |
| Materials ordered | Confirm ACV check received/deposited before ordering, unless HMP fronts it |
| Installed | Get the completion date on record - this is what unlocks depreciation |
| Depreciation requested | Send the final invoice + completion date the day the job finishes - this step is the one most often forgotten |
| Mortgage check (if any) | Start endorsement request the day the check arrives, not after |
| Paid | Confirm ACV + depreciation both deposited; close the loop |

This maps directly onto the `claims` schema already in CLAUDE.md - it's a set of nudge lines, not a new data model.

## (b) Legal referral programs in Nebraska

**What's confirmed allowed, cross-checked against 44-8604's actual text (search summary):**
- 44-8604 bars a contractor from **promising to rebate part of an insurance deductible** as an inducement, including
  "any form of compensation... to an insured or a person directly or indirectly associated with the residential real
  estate," except items of **nominal value**. That's about the customer whose own claim it is.
- A referral thank-you (gift card, small cash bonus, free gutter cleaning) to **a different person** - the friend or
  neighbor who sent the business - for a job that is not their own insurance claim, is a distinct, common, legal
  practice; nothing found treats third-party referral rewards as deductible rebating.
- Caution: keep the reward **modest and after the fact**, never framed as "refer someone and we'll cover your
  deductible too" (that would tie back into 44-8604), and never for a referral where the referrer is also the
  customer getting their own claim worked - a same-person "refer yourself" loophole isn't a thing here.

**What top roofers actually do (search summaries):**
- Thank-you note or small gift card (not "coupon" language) mailed a couple of days after the job finishes, while the
  homeowner is still happy - not at signing, not mid-job.
- Yard signs during/after the job (simple, free, always allowed - not a claims matter at all).
- Ask for a Google/Facebook review at completion, same moment as the referral ask.
- Fall is repeatedly named as the best season to ask (post-storm-season word of mouth) - no controlled study found,
  treat as seasonal color, not proof.

**HMP-fit version (S effort, free-$):**
1. At job completion: read-aloud script (EN/ES) - "Most of our work comes from people like you telling a friend or
   neighbor. If anyone comes to mind, we'd love an introduction" (this line already exists in round 9's plan - this
   round just confirms it's legal and adds the reward step after it).
2. Small thank-you (gift card, $25-$50 range, or a free service like gutter cleaning) mailed to the **referrer**,
   never framed near the deductible or claim language, sent only after the **new** customer's own job is signed (not
   the referrer's).
3. Yard sign + review ask at completion, both free.

# Research round 17 (2026-09-26): competitors, the niche, software rivals

FilthE's ask: "we need more info, we have to study our competitors and learn the niche we're going
towards." Three topics, one pass each (search-summary based; some pages could not be opened directly,
flagged below).

## Summary
1. **Local restoration companies are established, not scrappy** - Millard Roofing (Omaha, since 2001,
   BBB A+), Tillotson Enterprises (Lincoln/Kearney/Omaha/Aurora, 30+ yrs, employee-owned), Nastase
   Contracting (Omaha, since 1977, A+ BBB, "hires people who show up ready to work") [1][2][6]. None
   found serving Fremont with a dedicated storm-sales team the way HMP intends to build one - this is
   the gap.
2. **Out-of-state storm chasers are a known, disliked pattern** local companies actively warn against
   ("we are not storm chasers," "sign nothing with door-knocking crews") [3]. HMP door-knocks too, so
   the pitch has to visibly separate HMP from that reputation on the very first thing said at the door
   (own name, HMP, what we sell - already CLAUDE.md's 69-1602 rule) and back it with the registration
   number once the boss provides it.
3. **The niche is real money, hard to size precisely.** Nebraska sees ~319 hail events/year (486 in
   2023); 2017-19 nationwide-style data implies roughly 50k+ hail claims/year state-wide historically,
   $50.8M/year in Nebraska hail losses overall [4] - but no source gave a clean "Nebraska residential
   roof claims per year" or "average claim $" figure; treat as unproven, a real gap to note not fill.
4. **Fremont's Hispanic population is ~20%, and nearby towns are far higher** - Schuyler 75%, Columbus
   and Lexington also high [5]. This confirms round 12's Spanish-edge thesis with real local numbers
   for the first time, and argues for expanding town targets beyond Fremont/Omaha/Lincoln.
5. **Software rivals all charge per seat and get the same complaints**: rising/opaque pricing (quote-
   only in 2026), weak mobile apps, poor reporting, no Spanish anywhere found [7][8][9]. Confirms round
   12/16: bilingual + legal guardrails + rookie coaching is still unclaimed territory.

### 2. The niche
- Nebraska: ~319 hail events/year on average, 486 in 2023 [4]. Historical (2017-19) hail-loss-claim
  totals imply large annual claim volumes state-wide but no source gave a clean current "NE roof
  claims/year" number - unproven, flagged.
- Nebraska hail losses: $50.8M/year cited in one source [4] - scope (all property types or just
  homes) unclear, treat as a rough order of magnitude only.
- Sub labor rates found were general roofing labor cost, not restoration-specific: $125-$325/square
  (Nebraska range), ~$39/hr average skilled labor rate; insurance-restoration work often paid hourly
  because of documentation/scope-change overhead rather than a flat per-square rate [8]. This is a
  real unknown for HMP's actual arrangement with Nastase/VTR - best answered by asking them directly,
  not the open web.
- Spanish-speaking population, most useful new number this round: **Fremont ~20.3% Hispanic, Schuyler
  ~75.2% Hispanic**, Columbus and Lexington also named among Nebraska's most-Hispanic cities [5]. This
  is a stronger, closer market than Omaha/Lincoln for the bilingual pitch and should shape which towns
  get everyday door lists next, not just storm lists.

## Where HMP wins
- Genuinely local and bilingual where every named local competitor's marketing appears English-only.
- Can name and lean into the "not a storm chaser" reputation gap out-of-state crews leave behind.
- Software rivals all charge per-seat with rising/opaque pricing and no Spanish - if the HMP App is
  ever sold, that's still open ground (matches round 12's product thesis).
- Already has a real sub relationship with Nastase, a 49-year local player - a trust bridge new
  entrants don't have.
- Free MRMS-based hail engine, locally calibrated, beats paid hot-zone tools' basic tier on cost with
  no proven accuracy gap (round 16).

## Threats
- Established local competitors (Millard, Tillotson, Nastase itself as a competitor for direct jobs,
  not just a hiring partner) have decades of BBB history HMP doesn't have yet.
- Out-of-state storm chasers can undercut on price after a big hail event and leave before problems
  show - homeowners are primed to distrust any door-knocker, which raises the bar for HMP's first
  words at the door.
- Real claim-volume and sub-rate numbers for Nebraska specifically are still unproven from the open
  web - decisions on pricing/rates risk being made on guesswork until FilthE or the boss confirms
  real figures from their own network.

## For FilthE (plain lines)
- The competitors you'll run into are old, established Omaha/Lincoln companies (Millard, Tillotson) -
  not scrappy startups. Your edge is being from here, bilingual, and cheaper software than they use.
- Local companies openly warn homeowners about "storm chasers" - say your name, HMP, and that you're
  local, right at the door, every time. It matches the law (69-1602) and beats a real reputation problem.
- Schuyler and Columbus have way more Spanish-speaking homeowners than Fremont does (75% vs 20%) -
  worth adding to your everyday (non-storm) door lists, not just Fremont.
- Nobody could find a real "how many hail claims does Nebraska get a year" number online - if Nastase
  or the boss knows real figures from experience, that's better than anything the internet has.
- No sales software out there speaks Spanish. That's still your biggest edge if you build the app well.

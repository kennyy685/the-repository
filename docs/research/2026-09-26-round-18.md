## 2. Efficiency: how small roofing companies run lean

- **Batch by geography.** Grouping similar jobs by location can cut travel time roughly 30% [3]. HMP's turf
  lists already batch *door-knocking* by area (the engine's Hot Zones) - the same idea should extend to
  **crew job scheduling** once there are 2+ signed jobs running at once: don't send a crew across town and
  back for a small repair when there's a job two streets over.
- **Aerial measurement tools cost real money at HMP's volume.** At ~20 jobs/month: EagleView ~$1,100/mo,
  Hover ~$1,300/mo, Roofr ~$300/mo (per-report: EagleView $60-87, Roofr $13-19) [4]. At HMP's current volume
  (pre-first-job), none of these pay for themselves yet - a tape measure and the free satellite view in
  Google Maps/Earth is the right tool until there's steady job flow. Revisit Roofr (cheapest, has a free
  plan) once HMP is running 5+ jobs/month.
- **Supplier credit terms free up cash.** ABC Supply, Beacon, and SRS all offer net-30/net-60 credit accounts
  and job-specific accounts for a single project, letting a contractor order materials, install, collect from
  the customer, and pay the supplier after - without fronting cash [5]. **Action: apply for one supplier
  credit account before the first signed job** (a job-account for that one project is the easiest first
  approval for a young business with no payment history yet).
- **Checklists/SOPs before each job starts** and **tracking a few numbers weekly** (close rate, margin by job
  type, revenue per crew) are named repeatedly as what separates growing small shops from stalled ones [3] -
  this is exactly what `hh.py weekly` + the app's Money tab already aim to give FilthE; keep pointing him at
  it once he has real numbers.

## 3. Profit math (1-minute table)

Figures are national industry benchmarks from search-summary sources [6][7][8] - **not audited, not
Nebraska-specific**, flagged unproven like round 15's figures. Use for rough sizing only.

| | Insurance roof replacement | Siding job | Sub labor only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical job revenue | $8,000-$15,000 (2,000 sq ft) | similar range, material-driven | HMP is paid a labor rate, not the full job price |
| Materials | 22-28% of revenue | Higher % (materials are a bigger share of siding cost) | Not HMP's cost - the hiring contractor supplies materials |
| Labor | 32-38% of revenue | Similar | This IS HMP's revenue line |
| O&P (when 3+ trades coordinate) | +10-20% on top, often stripped by carriers unless asked for [round 15] | Same rule applies if multiple trades involved | N/A - no O&P as a pure sub |
| Gross profit per job | $2,000-$8,000 | Similar range | Lower revenue per job, but almost no material risk or cash float needed |
| Net margin (industry) | 5-15% typical, 15-20% top performers, NRCA average is only 2.8% | Similar band | Subcontractors average slightly *higher* net margin (2.2-3.5%) than general contractors (1.4-2.4%) because they carry less overhead [7] |
| Cash timing | ACV check first, depreciation held until job done + invoice sent (round 15); mortgage-endorsement can add 1-3 weeks | Same claim mechanics if insurance-funded; cash-pay siding has no wait | Paid faster and simpler - no insurer, no depreciation chase |

**Where small contractors lose money (repeated across sources):** missed supplements (round 15, still the
#1 leak), O&P stripped and never asked for, starting material orders before the ACV check clears (cash-flow
risk on a young business), and undertracked labor hours eating the 32-38% labor budget on inefficient jobs.

**5 levers that raise profit most, in order:**
1. Always ask for O&P on 3+ trade jobs (free - just requires knowing to ask, round 15's checklist covers this).
2. Chase every supplement within the ~180-day window (round 15).
3. Get a supplier credit account so materials don't tie up cash before the ACV check arrives.
4. Sub-labor jobs (like Nastase/VTR work) as a lower-risk, faster-cash complement to insurance retail while
   HMP builds its own claim volume and cash cushion.
5. Batch crew routing by geography once 2+ jobs run at once, to cut wasted drive time.

## For FilthE (plain lines)
- Best free leads right now: ask The Edge's property manager for one more introduction, and ask Nastase
  about steady backup sub-labor work. Both are one phone call.

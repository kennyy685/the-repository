# Research round 19 (2026-09-26): verifying round 18's 4 unverified leads

Round 18 flagged 4 ideas as "promising but unverified" (D17): Hispanic community channels, mobile
home parks, property manager/HOA vendor status, and insurance agent referrals. This round checked
each against real sources. Search egress was blocked for most individual pages this round (parish,
chamber, NARPM, radio-group sites all refused direct fetch) - findings below are search-summary
based unless marked [opened]; treat unconfirmed numbers as leads to verify by phone, not facts.

## Summary
1. **Mobile home parks are a real, mostly-untouched segment, and the buyer is the resident, not the
   park.** Nebraska's Mobile Home Landlord and Tenant Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1494) confirms park
   owners hold the land; residents own the home structure and normally carry its insurance - so a
   hail-damaged mobile-home roof is the resident's claim to file, same sales motion as a house.
   Nebraska Manufactured Housing Association's site (nemanufacturedhomes.com) has a "find a
   community" directory to locate parks in range. This is the clearest new win this round.
2. **Hispanic community channels are real but scattered, not one big list.** Confirmed: a Spanish
   Sunday Mass at St. Patrick Fremont (noon), the Nebraska Hispanic Chamber of Commerce (Omaha,
   founded 2005, South Omaha roots), the Mexican Consulate in Omaha (holds public community events),
   and the ~50-team, ~1,000-player Omaha Latino soccer league (Interligas). No confirmed Spanish
   radio station serves Fremont/Schuyler/Columbus itself - the nearest confirmed ones are Flood
   Communications' Fiesta 94.5 FM (Kearney/Grand Island/Lexington) and Omaha's Spanish-format
   stations. Church bulletin ad rates, chamber membership cost, and soccer sponsorship cost are
   all unverified - phone calls, not more web search, will answer them.
3. **Property manager "preferred vendor" status has a clear, doable checklist**: certificate of
   insurance (roofing trades commonly asked for $1-2M general liability, sometimes $2M/$4M with an
   umbrella), a W-9, and often a vendor-portal submission (AppFolio/Buildium both have one). This is
   paperwork HMP can prepare once and reuse for every property manager, including The Edge's.
4. **Insurance agent referrals: Nebraska law itself bars paying for referrals**, not just company
   policy. Nebraska Dept. of Insurance rules say a producer (agent) can't be paid anything beyond
   commission/salary for referring business, and can't split commission with an unlicensed party.
   That makes the legal version of "get on an agent's recommend list" a zero-dollar, word-of-mouth
   relationship only - confirms CLAUDE.md's no-kickback rule is state law, not caution.

## Details

### Mobile home parks
- Nebraska's Mobile Home Landlord and Tenant Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1494, 76-1495): park owners
  set written rules (must meet 6 statutory tests: safety/convenience purpose, applied fairly to all
  tenants, clear enough to follow, not a way to dodge the landlord's own duties, given to tenants in
  advance); any rule change needs 60 days' notice to all tenants [1][2]. Park owners **cannot**
  require a tenant to install a "permanent improvement" that can't be removed without damaging the
  home or lot when the tenant leaves [1] - relevant if a full re-roof is ever framed as such.
- Ownership split, confirmed by general insurance-industry sources (not Nebraska-specific, but
  consistent across sources): the resident typically owns the home structure and carries its own
  homeowner/mobile-home insurance (which covers hail/wind/fire damage to the structure); the park
  owner separately insures the land and shared infrastructure (roads, utilities) [3][4]. **This means
  the sales conversation is with the resident who owns the home, the same as any house - not the
  park office** - though courtesy/notice to park management for site access (trucks, dumpsters,
  crew parking) is still good practice.
- "Roof-over" (adding a new roof system, e.g. TPO membrane, over an existing manufactured-home roof)
  is an established product in the industry [5] - worth having as a service line/talking point
  specifically for older manufactured homes with worn metal roofs, alongside a full tear-off option.
- Nebraska Manufactured Housing Association (nemanufacturedhomes.com) has "find a community" and
  "find a retailer" directories and lists individual park owners (e.g. Heartford Homes, Columbus NE)
  [6] - a fast way to build a target list of parks within range, instead of guessing locations.
- **Not verified:** exact park locations/counts near Fremont/Schuyler/Columbus (egress-blocked from
  opening the directory page directly - needs a follow-up fetch or a phone call to the association),
  and whether HMP's current general liability policy needs any manufactured-housing-specific
  endorsement.

### Property managers and HOAs
- Standard onboarding ask across property-management guidance: a copy of the contractor's license,
  a certificate of insurance (COI), and a bond certificate if the contractor has one [16]. For
  higher-risk trades like roofing specifically, property managers commonly ask for $1-2M general
  liability, sometimes $2M/$4M with an umbrella policy layered on top [16]. A W-9 is standard for
  any paid vendor. Property managers commonly verify a vendor's standing via the Secretary of State
  business-registration lookup and any state licensing board, rather than taking the vendor's word
  [16].
- Both AppFolio and Buildium (the two platforms named in round 18's question) have vendor portals
  for submitting these documents directly [17] - **first step is simply asking The Edge's property
  manager which system they use**, then submitting once.
- **Not verified this round:** whether a Nebraska/Omaha NARPM chapter exists with its own vendor
  list (the national chapter-finder page could not be opened this round; general NARPM guidance
  confirms local chapters do maintain member/vendor networks, but Omaha-specific details are still
  unconfirmed) [18].

### Insurance agents
- Nebraska Department of Insurance rules confirm: a licensed producer (agent) **cannot be paid**
  anything beyond commission or salary for selling, soliciting, or referring insurance business, and
  commission-splitting is allowed only between two parties who are both licensed in that line [19].
  An unlicensed party (a contractor) cannot legally be paid a commission or fee tied to an insurance
  referral either way.
- Practical read: the only legal version of "get on an agent's recommend list" is a **free, informal,
  word-of-mouth relationship** - no payment, no gift tied to referral volume, in either direction.
  This matches round 18's caution and CLAUDE.md's 44-8604 spirit even though that specific statute is
  about deductibles, not agent referrals - the state's insurance-producer rules independently forbid
  paying for the referral itself [19].
- RESPA (federal) came up in search results but applies to mortgage/real-estate settlement services,
  not insurance-agent-to-contractor referrals directly [20] - Nebraska's own producer-licensing rule
  above is the one that actually governs this.

- Every phone/rate figure in this round (chamber dues, bulletin ad cost, soccer sponsorship,
  radio ad rates) is unverified - the web would not give a number; a two-minute phone call will.
  Don't act on a dollar figure that isn't confirmed by the org itself.
- Keep any insurance-agent relationship at zero dollars, no gifts, no referral-volume-linked
  anything - Nebraska law bars paying for the referral itself, not just deductible-related conduct.
- At mobile home parks, knock the resident who owns the home, not just the park office - but still
  give the park office a heads-up as a courtesy for site access.

## For FilthE (plain lines)
- Mobile home parks are a real, mostly-open lead source - the resident who owns the home is who you
  talk to, same as any house, and their insurance covers hail damage to the roof just like a house.
- Get your insurance certificate and W-9 ready as a one-page packet - property managers all ask for
  the same two things, so prepare it once and use it everywhere, starting with The Edge's manager.
- Church, chamber, soccer league, consulate events - all real and legal, but the actual costs need a
  phone call, not more searching. Five quick calls (parish, chamber, soccer league, consulate,
  property manager) will tell you more than another research round would.
- Insurance agents legally cannot be paid for referring you business, and you legally can't pay them
  - so keep any agent relationship free and word-of-mouth only. That's the law, not just caution.
- Spanish radio doesn't reach Fremont/Schuyler/Columbus directly yet - the closest one covers
  Kearney/Lexington. Lowest priority of this round's ideas, and the only one that costs real money.

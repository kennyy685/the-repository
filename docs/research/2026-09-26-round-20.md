# Research round 20 (2026-09-26): the contractor-side money playbook - supplements, missed line items, O&P

Topic: what a legitimate roofing/siding contractor (not a public adjuster) can do to make sure Nebraska hail/wind
claims are scoped and paid correctly - the missed line items, the supplement process, and the documents adjusters
want. Builds on round 15's money checklist (supplement window, mortgage-check delay, O&P) and round 18's profit
math (O&P as the #1 lever) - this round supplies the actual line-item list neither round had, plus the exact legal
line between "document and cite code" (contractor, allowed) and "negotiate the settlement" (public adjuster,
licensed, Neb. Rev. Stat. 44-9201 to 44-9219). Network egress to nebraskalegislature.gov, up.codes, and most
trade-press sites was blocked this round (same limitation as rounds 9/15/18) - everything below is search-result
summary unless marked otherwise, and every Nebraska-specific legal point still needs a direct-source re-check.

## Bottom line

1. **A specific, named list of missed line items beats a vague "ask for a supplement" reminder.** 18 items are in
   `data/supplement_items.json`, each with what proof photo/measurement to grab and (where found) the code section
   to cite. Drip edge, ice-and-water shield, step/counter flashing, ridge vent, O&P, and siding trim/matching are
   named across multiple independent sources as the most consistently missed.
2. **Nebraska has its own matching rule, stronger than the generic "ask nicely" supplement pitch**: Neb. Admin.
   Code Title 210, Ch. 60, Sec 010.01(B) requires the insurer to make damaged siding "reasonably uniform" in
   appearance at no cost to the insured beyond the deductible, when an exact match isn't available. This is
   Nebraska-specific leverage FilthE can cite by name - unverified at the primary source this round, but multiple
   independent summaries agree on the same section number and language, so it's likely correct.
3. **The legal line is simple and already matches CLAUDE.md's rule**: document, measure, photograph, cite code, and
   hand the insurer a written supplement request. Never negotiate the settlement amount as the homeowner's agent,
   never promise an outcome, never touch the deductible. That crosses into public-adjuster work (license required
   under 44-9201 to 44-9219) or straight into 44-8604.
4. **Xactimate line-item codes themselves are a proprietary catalog** (Verisk/Xactware) - this round used only
   category names that appear openly in public trade-press writing (e.g. "detach and reset," "steep/high charge"),
   never the proprietary code numbers, per the request to name categories only if public.

## Supplement process (what's allowed for a contractor)

**Contractor may:** photograph and measure every damaged area; cite the specific code section that requires an
item; meet the adjuster on-site and point out damage in person; submit a written supplement (photos + measurements
+ code citation per added item); ask in writing for O&P when 3+ trades are involved, noting Xactimate's own default
calculation includes it (a factual point, not a negotiation).

**Contractor may NOT (without a public adjuster license, Neb. Rev. Stat. 44-9201-44-9219):** negotiate the
settlement amount on the homeowner's behalf; represent to the insurer that you're acting FOR the insured to effect
a settlement; promise a specific dollar outcome. **Never** (44-8604, already in CLAUDE.md): offer, waive or rebate
any part of the deductible.

**Timeline:** most carriers accept a supplement within roughly 180 days of the first payment (round 15's figure,
repeated by multiple sources this round, still unaudited).

## Documents adjusters want (checklist)

Full dated photo set per slope/elevation - measurements (roof report or manual: squares, linear ft eaves/rakes/
ridge/valleys/flashing) - line-by-line scope comparison (insurer's estimate vs. photos/measurements) - manufacturer
install instructions for warranty-required items - code citation per added item - discontinuation letter for
siding-matching claims - claim number/date of loss/adjuster contact on everything sent.

## 1-screen checklist for the app's claim screen (per stage)

- **Inspected:** photo every slope/side + every siding elevation, dated. Note pitch/story height. Check for
  satellite/solar/fixtures that need detach-reset.
- **Claim filed:** claim number, adjuster name/phone, date of loss on file.
- **Adjuster set:** be there in person with the photo set; walk every intersection (flashing) and trim piece.
- **Scope in:** compare line-by-line against `data/supplement_items.json`'s 18 items - flag anything missing
  BEFORE the meeting ends if possible.
- **Signed:** contract has deductible notice (44-8607) + 3-day cancel notice, both languages (already verified).
- **Supplement:** file within ~180 days of first payment; one item per line, each with photo + measurement + code
  citation where one applies; note O&P separately if 3+ trades.
- **Materials ordered:** confirm ACV check received/deposited first (round 15).
- **Installed:** get completion date on record.
- **Depreciation requested:** send final invoice + completion date same day (round 15's most-forgotten step).

## For FilthE (plain lines)

- Adjusters miss the same items over and over: drip edge, ice-and-water shield, flashing where the roof meets a
  wall or chimney, ridge vent, O&P, and siding trim/matching. There's now a full checklist (18 items) - it'll show
  up on your claim screen so you don't have to remember it.
- Nebraska has its own rule that says if your exact siding color/style isn't sold anymore, the insurance company
  has to make the whole wall (or house) look uniform, not just patch one spot - worth learning the name of that
  rule before your first siding claim.
- What you CAN do: photograph everything, measure everything, point to the code, ask in writing for the missing
  items. What you CANNOT do: negotiate the dollar amount for the homeowner or promise them what they'll get - that
  needs a public-adjuster license you don't have. Stick to documenting and let the homeowner and adjuster settle
  the number.
- One item this round found is Nebraska-specific and strong (the matching rule) but needs a direct-source
  double-check before you quote it to an adjuster - flagged, not confirmed yet.
- Next small job: one phone call to Fremont's building department to confirm what code edition they use for
  ice-and-water shield and drip edge.

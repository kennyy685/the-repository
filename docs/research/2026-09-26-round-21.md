# Research round 21 (2026-09-26): Hispanic community outreach + homeowner financing

Two backlog topics: (1) Spanish Facebook groups / tiendas-panaderías bulletin boards / WhatsApp groups in
Fremont/Schuyler/Columbus (legal outreach only - no cold texts, no bought lists, no joining groups under
false pretenses); (2) homeowner financing (GreenSky/Service Finance/Hearth/Wisetack) - dealer requirements,
fees, and how financing changes close rates on cash siding/roofing jobs.

**Search egress was blocked for almost every individual page this round** (greensky-online.com, gethearth.com,
wisetack.com, fremonttribune.com, holanebraska.org, centrohispanone.org, contractortoolstack.com all refused
direct fetch, same pattern as round 19) - everything below is search-summary based unless marked [opened].
Treat unconfirmed numbers as leads to verify by phone/call, not settled facts.

## Summary
1. **Financing is the single highest-payoff idea in this round.** Search-summary sources agree financing
   lifts close rate roughly 10-20 percentage points (one source: 38% -> 49%; another: 25% -> 44% with
   multiple options) and average job size too - and **HMP is currently offering none.** This applies to
   both insurance-gap/upgrade money and cash-pay siding/roofing jobs. [vendor claims marked]
2. **HMP's size steers the choice.** GreenSky and Service Finance both lean toward established, higher-
   volume contractors (GreenSky: ~2+ years in business and roughly $1M lifetime revenue in most summaries;
   Service Finance: best fit named as $2M+ annual revenue, 2-4 week approval). **Hearth and Wisetack have no
   stated revenue/volume minimum** and fit a brand-new, pre-first-job shop like HMP much better right now.
3. **Hispanic outreach in Fremont has one confirmed, ready-made door: the Chamber's Spanish Networking
   Group** (100-150+ bilingual professionals, monthly meetings, already exists, free to attend). Schuyler
   and Columbus have real organizations (PODER Latino Schuyler, Centro Hispano Comunitario in Columbus) but
   this round could not open their pages to confirm bulletin-board/event details - a phone call or in-person
   visit is the next step, same conclusion as round 19 for this topic.
4. **Nebraska's Installment Sales Act licenses the *lender*, not the contractor** - HMP referring a customer
   to a third-party financing company (GreenSky, Hearth, Wisetack, Service Finance) does not itself require
   HMP to hold a Nebraska installment-sales/consumer-lending license, because HMP would not be the one
   extending credit. **Not fully verified** - confirm with whoever helps with the boss's business paperwork,
   or a quick call to NE Dept. of Banking & Finance, before signing any dealer agreement.

## Details

### Financing
- **Close-rate lift (vendor/industry claims, marked):** one source: contractors offering financing close
  49% vs. 38% without (+11 points); another (Regions Bank study, vendor-cited): 25% baseline -> 44% when
  multiple financing options are offered; a third: financing lifts close rates 18% and average project size
  30%; a fourth: 20-40% lift on jobs over $5,000 per Synchrony/Service Finance benchmark data. All four are
  vendor or vendor-adjacent sources - directionally consistent (financing clearly helps) but treat the exact
  percentages as marketing claims, not audited numbers [1][2][3][4].
- **GreenSky:** requires an EIN/business license, Federal Tax ID, bank statements showing financial
  stability, a synced POS, and dealer training. Search summaries disagree slightly on the volume bar - one
  says "if you make less than $1M/yr or have been open <2 years, GreenSky is the best option"; another says
  GreenSky's own merchant requirements are "roughly 2+ years in business and ~$1M lifetime revenue." **This
  is a real contradiction found in the sources** - don't rely on it either way without asking GreenSky
  directly. Dealer fees run 3-15% (lower for standard-rate loans, higher for 0%-promo financing). Approval
  ~2-3 business days [5][6][7].
- **Service Finance Company (SFC):** an FHA Title I lender, 50+ loan products, "stage funding" (no consumer
  payments due until the job is done - contractor-friendly cash timing). Fees run 0% (standard 9.99% APR
  plans) up to 7-15% (0% promo plans), no hidden merchant/ACH/activation fees. Deeply embedded in GAF Master
  Elite and other manufacturer dealer programs. Best-fit description in sources: $2M+ annual revenue roofing/
  HVAC/remodeling contractors; approval takes 2-4 weeks - **too slow and too high a bar for HMP right now**
  [8][9].
- **Hearth:** flat annual subscription ($2,000-6,000/yr by tier + $99 setup, per one comparison site), no
  per-loan dealer fee, no underwriting/revenue requirement to sign up. Loans $1,000-$250,000, credit scores
  as low as 550 accepted by some lenders in its network, funding in as little as 24 hours. The subscription
  cost is fixed whether HMP closes 1 job or 20 that month - a real risk for a shop with no jobs booked yet
  [10][11].
- **Wisetack:** no signup fee, no subscription, no revenue minimum - pure pay-per-use: **3.9% fee only when
  a customer actually uses Wisetack financing**, paid out after the job completes, minus the fee. Loans
  $500-$25,000, 0-35.9% APR depending on the customer's credit, terms 3-120 months. This is the lowest fixed
  cost and lowest risk of the four for a business with zero financing history [12][13].
- **Nebraska licensing angle:** Nebraska's Installment Sales Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. 45-334 to 45-380) requires
  a license (via NMLS) for a business that *acts as a sales finance company* - i.e., the entity extending or
  buying the credit contract. In a GreenSky/Hearth/Wisetack/SFC arrangement, that entity is the finance
  company, not HMP; HMP is only referring the customer and (for GreenSky/SFC) paying a dealer fee out of the
  job price. **This reading is not confirmed by a primary source this round** (the Dept. of Banking & Finance
  page and statute PDF came up in search but couldn't be opened) - worth one phone call to NE Dept. of
  Banking & Finance before signing any dealer agreement, just to be safe [14][15].

### Hispanic community outreach
- **Fremont:** the Chamber of Commerce's **Spanish Networking Group** is real, active, and free - started
  for bilingual professionals in northeast Nebraska, grown to 100-150+ participants, monthly meetings
  conducted in both Spanish and English, now drawing people from across the state and into Iowa/South
  Dakota. Contact is through the Fremont Area Chamber of Commerce's Facebook page [16][17][18]. This is
  the most concrete, walk-in-able channel found in this round - better fit for HMP than round 19's
  church/chamber/consulate leads because it's networking-focused, not just a bulletin.
- **Schuyler:** Hispanic/Latino residents are 75.2% of the town's population, the highest share of any NE
  place measured, with 74.4% of households reporting Spanish-only at home [19] - confirms Schuyler is worth
  real effort. A Facebook group/page "**PODER Latino Schuyler Nebraska**" exists [20] but its content,
  membership size, and whether it allows local-business posts could not be confirmed this round (page not
  opened) - visit or message it directly.
- **Columbus:** **Centro Hispano Comunitario de Nebraska** is a real, established nonprofit (400+ immigration
  cases opened in 2023, GED/computer-skills classes for 670+ adults) with its own building/office and an
  active Facebook page [21][22][23]. A physical office like this is exactly the kind of place with a real
  bulletin board or community-events calendar - a phone call or visit is the fastest way to find out. Also
  found: **Tienda y Panadería Suárez**, a newly-rebranded grocery/bakery at 1709 23rd St, Columbus NE [24] -
  a concrete, named tienda to ask about a flyer, confirming this kind of local business exists to approach
  (matches the backlog ask exactly).
- **WhatsApp groups:** by design these are private and not indexed by search - no legitimate/ethical way to
  find or join one exists except through a real personal or community connection. Not a research gap to
  solve with more searching; it's a "FilthE asks around" task, and joining one to pitch without belonging
  to the community naturally would be the "false pretenses" case the backlog explicitly rules out.
- **Not found this round:** any specific tienda/panadería bulletin-board policy or cost (none publish this
  online, as round 19 also found) - the only path is asking in person.

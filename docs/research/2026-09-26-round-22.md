## GAF Master Elite - what was found (unverified at the source)
- Top 2% of roofing contractors nationally hold this. Unlocks the 50-year Golden Pledge warranty (workmanship +
  manufacturing defects), the strongest sales pitch in this market (confirmed by round 17b: no local competitor
  combines it with Hardie certification).
- Business requirements (multiple trade-press sources agree): state registration, $1M+ general liability
  insurance, workers' comp, ~7 years roofing experience, 99% local customer satisfaction, satisfactory BBB rating,
  good business credit.
- Volume to requalify yearly (one source, not GAF's own page): ~1,000 squares of GAF shingles + 500 enhanced
  warranties registered per year. Annual fee guessed at $100-$300/yr by one blog - **unconfirmed**, GAF does not
  publish a cash price for the tier itself; the real cost is the product volume and insurance/training overhead.
- Steps (composite from trade press, not GAF's own page): apply through a GAF rep/distributor, pass a business
  and insurance review, complete GAF certified installation training, install/register the volume for a
  qualifying period, then requalify every year.

## James Hardie Elite Preferred - what was found (unverified at the source)
- The James Hardie ALLIANCE has tiers: Select -> Preferred -> Elite (Elite Preferred is the top, "Elite" label
  varies by source). Contractors move up by **volume, tenure and customer-satisfaction score, not by paying a
  fee**.
- Elite Preferred bar (one source): Homeowner "Likely to Recommend" score of 90%+ on GuildQuality (an independent
  third-party survey company, unrelated to Hardie) + proper license + $1M liability + workers' comp + ongoing
  installation audits + BBB record.
- **Select tier (entry level)** looked like the realistic first step for HMP: it exists mainly to certify that a
  contractor installs Hardie products to spec so the homeowner's product warranty is backed - no volume/tenure
  bar found in the sources this round. Worth a direct call to a Hardie rep to confirm Select's actual requirements
  and whether HMP already qualifies from The Edge job's Hardie lap siding work.
- No application fee was found in the search-result summaries for any tier - the program appears to be
  performance-based, not pay-to-join.

## The adjuster meeting - what top reps do (composite, not a single source)
**Before:** confirm the appointment time; have the inspection report, homeowner's claim number, date of loss, and
photos ready; arrive early and have the ladder already set up.
**Bring:** a labeled roof/elevation diagram (numbered slopes, N/S/E/W), a tape measure/roof-measurement report,
flashlight, phone camera, business card, and (per round 20) the 18-item supplement checklist.
**During:** walk every elevation with the adjuster - don't let them shortcut to one "representative slope"; point
out every penetration (pipe boots, vents, skylights, chimney flashing) and every damage pattern; answer technical
questions on the spot; mark damage on the diagram together so both sides leave with the same shared record; ask
what's included in the written scope and when it arrives; ask how to submit anything found later (this feeds
directly into round 20's supplement process).
**Don't:** don't chalk the roof (one source says insurers reject chalk-marked photos - flagged, confirms with a
grease pencil/flag tape instead until this is checked further); don't promise the homeowner an outcome; don't
speak or negotiate AS the homeowner's representative (that's public-adjuster work, Neb. Rev. Stat. 44-9201 to
44-9219, already in CLAUDE.md); stick to documenting and answering technical questions.
**After:** get a copy of the adjuster's inspection notes/scope before the adjuster leaves if possible; compare
line-by-line against round 20's 18-item list before the visit ends, so a missing item gets flagged in person
instead of by a later phone call.

## Watch out
- The "don't chalk" vs. the backlog's own "chalk" wording is a real conflict in the sources - use the diagram +
  tape/pencil approach until this is settled, and don't have FilthE chalk roofs.
- Every fee/volume number above is a trade-press guess, not the manufacturer's own published number. Don't quote
  a dollar figure to the boss as fact - call a GAF rep and a Hardie rep once HMP is closer to qualifying.
- Neither certification changes what HMP can legally say about deductibles or claims - the hard rules in
  CLAUDE.md (44-8604, no deductible waiving; no claims negotiation without a public-adjuster license) apply the
  same with or without a badge.

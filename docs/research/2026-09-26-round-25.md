  year in the US [1]. Season ramps up **mid-April**, peaks **late May through July**, with **June**
  the single worst month for roofing damage in the Omaha area and May close behind [1][2].

## Nebraska claim deadlines (plain language for FilthE)

- **No short deadline to worry about.** Nebraska statute 44-357 blocks insurers from writing a
  suit-limitation period shorter than the state's regular statute of limitations; the general
  written-contract limitations period (Neb. Rev. Stat. 25-205, referenced from insurance-law
  commentary as controlling here) is **5 years** from the date of loss for filing a lawsuit over a
  claim dispute [10][11]. **[unverified-fetch]** - the search summary itself flagged this
  connection as inferred, not quoted directly from statute text; worth a follow-up round to open
  nebraskalegislature.gov directly (it was blocked this round) and confirm 25-205's exact wording
  applies to insurance-contract disputes specifically.
- **Proof of loss** (the paperwork step after filing, separate from the lawsuit deadline): typical
  policies require it "as soon as reasonably possible" or within a set window (90-180 days) *only
  after* the insurer makes a written demand for it - it's a policy term, not a hard statutory
  clock, and 180 days is common but policy-specific [10].
- **Insurer's deadline to act**, once a claim is filed: acknowledge receipt within 15 days; pay,
  deny or settle a "clean claim" within 30 days (electronic) / 45 days (other) [10].
- **Practical takeaway for FilthE:** there is no legal reason to rush a homeowner into signing
  same-day off a claims-deadline scare - the real reason to move fast is competition (another
  contractor knocks first) and material/insurance-check timing, not a statute. Never say "you only
  have X days to file" as a pressure line; it isn't accurate and it isn't a good look.

## Details: AI tools other contractors use

- **Photo-to-quote/estimate tools** (XBuild, QuoteIQ, Roofr, Hover): turn job-site photos + a
  satellite roof measurement into a proposal in minutes instead of hours [5]. QuoteIQ's AI
  Estimator starts around $29.99/mo [5]. HMP's own `hh.py estimate`/`takeoff` already do the math
  side of this for free - the piece HMP is missing is the photo-in / proposal-out UI polish, which
  is really an O7 sale-guide app feature, not a reason to buy a subscription tool.
- **CompanyCam-style photo organization**: auto-organizes before/after photos by property with
  AI-written captions/reports, ~$63-79/mo for 1-2 users [5][6]. Worth copying the *idea* (auto-tag
  photos to the address, generate a one-line AI caption) into the app's claim/job photo storage
  rather than buying a second subscription - HMP already has a claims database to hang photos off.
- **Missed-call text-back**: automated SMS sent within 1-2 minutes of an unanswered call, before
  the caller tries the next name on their list; 98% of texts get read [7][8]. This is the clearest
  buy-or-build decision this round - see ranked item 1.
- **Full AI voice answering/lead-qualification agents**: real, and several support Spanish natively
  [9], but priced and built for call volumes well above HMP's current one-salesman scale
  ($0.10-0.33/min plus a $100-300/mo platform in some cases). Not a fit yet.

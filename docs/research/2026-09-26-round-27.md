## Summary
1. **The first 3 seconds are mostly nonverbal.** Stance angled (not squared on the mat), hands open/visible,
   smiling, friendly-curious tone beats "authoritative" — homeowners decide whether to keep listening almost
   instantly. This is trainable with drills, not just talked about.
2. **"Not interested" is not the end** — it just means the porch pitch didn't earn 30 more seconds. Top reps
   ask for one small next step (a free look, a specific day) instead of trying to close the whole job at the door.
3. **Top-1% reps run identical daily structures**: same wake time, pitch spoken out loud once before the first
   knock, yesterday's numbers reviewed, one skill focus for the day, 50-70 doors/day in focused blocks, phone away.
4. **Rejection is handled by ratio-tracking and time-boxing dwelling on it**, not by "thick skin" alone — reps
   log every door and read it as data, which fits HMP's existing Today's knock taps exactly.
5. **A contingency agreement is a real, binding contract** — once the claim is approved, the homeowner is
   committed to that contractor, price often pegged to the settlement. This is legally different from (a) a
   plain inspection/photo authorization or (b) HMP's actual signed contract. HMP should be clear with itself and
   the homeowner about exactly which one is being signed and when — signing anything that locks a homeowner in
   before they've seen a scope or price is the "storm chaser" pattern homeowners are warned against (round 17).
6. **The 3-day cancel right and the deductible rule are the two things every source agrees must be said out
   loud, not just printed** — CLAUDE.md already has the exact Nebraska text (69-1601, 44-8604); this round found
   no new legal wording, only confirms the sales-craft point that reps should narrate these rather than hand over
   a page silently.

## Useful details

### Door-to-door craft (topic 1)
- **Positioning:** don't stand square on the welcome mat facing the door head-on (reads as confrontational);
  angle shoulders slightly, keep hands open/visible (not in pockets or crossed), smile before the door opens.
- **Tonality:** friendly and inquisitive beats stern/authoritative; the first three seconds set the psychological
  frame more than the words chosen.
- **Pitch shape:** short, doesn't try to sell the whole job on the porch — states who you are, why you're there,
  makes one clear, small offer, and asks for one specific next step (a look, a day/time), matching CLAUDE.md's
  69-1602 rule (say name, HMP, and what you sell, first thing) and round 5's "specific next date, never 'I'll
  call you'" finding.
- **"Not interested" handling:** treat each no as specific to that door/moment, not a verdict on the rep;
  persistence + a clear next step turns more no's into inspections than a harder pitch does.
- **Daily routine (top reps):** consistent wake time, say the pitch out loud once before knocking, review
  yesterday's numbers, pick one skill focus, 50-70 doors/day worked in phone-free blocks (4-hour blocks cited);
  weekly team-style debrief on which objections beat people yesterday (HMP is solo/small, so this becomes
  FilthE's own end-of-day note, already partly built per round 9).
- **Rejection mindset:** top performers don't get rejected less than others — they spend less time dwelling on
  it, track their ratio as data, and move to the next door. This is exactly what Today's knock's tap-per-door
  logging already supports; the missing piece is showing the ratio back to FilthE as encouragement, not just a count.

### Kitchen-table / inspection-to-signature (topic 2)
- **Contingency agreement ≠ the final contract.** A contingency agreement authorizes inspection + insurer
  contact and commits the homeowner to that contractor once the claim is approved (price often set equal to the
  settlement); a standard contract is signed after scope/price are known. Several sources warn homeowners
  against signing a binding contingency agreement under time pressure before they've seen a real, itemized
  estimate — this is the storm-chaser pattern. HMP should be explicit at the door about which document is being
  signed and when, and should prefer showing photos + a real scope before any signature that locks the
  homeowner in.
- **What good inspection-to-signature process looks like (general sources, not Nebraska-specific):** documented
  inspection with photos, a clear written estimate, explaining repair-vs-replace options, supporting the
  insurance process without taking control of the claim (matches CLAUDE.md's "we meet the adjuster and document
  damage" rule and the public-adjuster-license line, 44-9204/44-9201+ already in Practice Door).
- **3-day cancel + deductible:** every general source agrees these must be disclosed in the contract; none
  offered anything Nebraska-specific beyond what CLAUDE.md already has verified (69-1601, 44-8607, 44-8604).
  No new legal text found this round — reinforces round 25's flagged need to re-verify 25-205/44-357 directly
  once network access allows it, and confirms saying these out loud (not just printing them) is standard advice
  everywhere it's discussed.
- **EN/ES:** no source specifically addressed bilingual kitchen-table delivery; this remains HMP's own edge
  (round 17) and an unfilled gap in the sales-training literature — worth a future scout pass once other topics
  are covered, if practitioner content in Spanish surfaces.

## Details

### Pricing psychology for cash jobs
- **Good/Better/Best:** offering three clear tiers (not one price) increases average ticket size and close rate
  in home-services sales generally - customers "self-select" instead of deciding yes/no on one number. [1][2]
- **Anchoring:** a high "Best" tier makes the middle tier look reasonably priced by comparison; a meta-analysis
  of 53 pricing studies found people are more likely to buy when a higher anchor price is shown first. Broader
  pricing research (McKinsey, cited via [3]) ties good anchoring to measurable margin gains generally, though
  that number is from a general retail context, not roofing specifically - treat it as directional, not a roofing
  benchmark. [1][3]
- **Same-day close:** same-day proposals (especially with financing offered on the spot) convert roughly 2x
  versus quotes followed up on later; big jobs still take longer to decide than emergency repairs. [1]
- **3-day cancel right is not a sales obstacle to work around.** Federal (FTC Cooling-Off Rule) and Nebraska
  (69-1601, already in CLAUDE.md) both require it on every door-to-door sale over $25, cancelable through
  midnight of the third business day. Consumer-protection guidance specifically calls out sellers who pressure
  people to waive it or bury it in fine print as a violation - so the safe move is saying it clearly, not
  rushing past it, which costs nothing and keeps HMP clean. [4][5]
- Unverified: I could not open a roofing-specific source with a hard "X% picked the Better tier" number - the
  general home-services figures above are the best evidence found this round; flag as directional until HMP has
  its own data.

### Hiring and paying a second salesman
- **Nebraska's control test:** Nebraska courts use a multi-factor common-law test (degree of control, method of
  payment, independence, etc.) to decide employee vs. contractor, regardless of what the paperwork or 1099 says.
  Construction and delivery work specifically carries a legal presumption of employee status unless the worker
  meets the Employee Classification Act's specific criteria (own business, own equipment, other clients, etc.).
  [6][7] (Primary source `dol.nebraska.gov` was blocked by egress this round for both WebFetch and curl - relying
  on a secondary legal-directory summary; re-verify with the state site or an employment lawyer before acting.)
- **Penalties:** first-offense fine of $500 per misclassified worker, $5,000 per worker on repeat offenses, plus
  back taxes/interest and possible referral for prosecution. [7]
- **Roofing-industry-specific risk:** trade sources call 1099 commission-only salespeople one of the most
  common and most litigated misclassification setups in roofing - the test used in practice: does the salesman
  represent only HMP, present himself as HMP to the homeowner, and use HMP's contract? If yes, that looks like a
  W-2 employee no matter what he's called. [8]
- **Standard fix used in the industry:** pay a "draw against commission" for a 60-90 day ramp (a guaranteed
  minimum paid against future commission, recoverable or forgiven depending on the agreement) instead of pure
  commission-only from day one - protects the new hire's cash flow and leans the relationship toward looking like
  paid work, not a side contractor. Typical commission-only ranges run 15-30% for pure door-to-door reps, 8-15% of
  gross for a base+commission structure. [9][10][11]

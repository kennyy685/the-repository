Runs alongside round 29 (backlog track) — no topic overlap. Two topics: (1) bilingual (Spanish)
kitchen-table sales for the insurance-restoration pitch and a cash siding/roof quote; (2) follow-up
scripts for touch 1/2/3 (days 2/5/10, `hailhunter/followups.py`) after a homeowner says "let me think
about it." WebFetch was blocked for individual pages again this round (same pattern as rounds 25-29);
findings below are search-summary based unless noted, and money/financing claims are flagged unverified.

## Summary
1. **Financing acceptance without an SSN is real but not confirmed for any specific lender.** ITIN-based
   home-improvement loans exist in the market generally, but this round could not confirm whether Hearth
   or Wisetack (round 21's picks for HMP's size) themselves accept ITIN-only applicants — their own pages
   stayed blocked. Don't promise a homeowner "no SSN needed" until FilthE calls and confirms one lender by
   name.
2. **The "both spouses/owners must be present" ask has a legitimate legal reason for HMP, separate from
   the manipulative sales-trick version some sources warn about.** Nebraska's 3-day cancellation notice
   and the contract itself need to go to whoever will actually sign; asking "who's on the deed / who'll be
   signing" up front avoids a contract that's easy to unwind later. Say it plainly as a paperwork reason,
   never as "we need to talk you both into it together."
3. **Nothing HMP-specific exists on immigration/paperwork fears at the door** — general sources only cover
   ITINs for taxes/lending, not sales scripts. HMP's own safe answer, consistent with existing rules: never
   ask immigration or citizenship status; the only ID HMP ever needs is the homeowner's name (for the
   contract) and, only if they choose financing, whatever the *lender* requires — that's the lender's
   question, not HMP's, and HMP should say so out loud if it comes up.
4. **The follow-up cadence HMP already built (2/5/10 days) matches general sales-training advice well.**
   Industry sources (NSEA-style stat, widely repeated) say 80% of sales take 5 touches and 44% of reps quit
   after 1 — HMP's 3 planned touches already beats the typical single-follow-up habit rounds 9/27 flagged.
   A same-day touch (day 0, already covered by round 27/9's day-of-inspection habits) plus 2/5/10 covers the
   shape general advice recommends (immediate, mid-week check, later new-angle push) without adding a 4th
   config touch.
5. **"Let me think about it" is a stall, not a no** — the recommended move is to acknowledge it, then ask
   ONE isolating question (price? timing? comparing another bid? talking to a spouse?) instead of
   re-pitching. This slots directly into HMP's existing "one small ask" rule from round 27.
6. **Channel rules matter more than script wording for compliance**, and CLAUDE.md's rules already answer
   this: door revisit or a call to a number the homeowner gave (and who asked to be followed up with) for
   residential; business-line calls only for commercial; never a cold text. `data/followup_scripts.json`
   is built with a `channel` field per script so the app can only surface the door/phone script the lead
   actually allows.

## Useful details

### Topic 1: bilingual kitchen-table sales
- **Trust signal #1 is genuinely speaking Spanish, not a translated flyer** — sources agree culturally-
  adapted communication (understanding the family's actual customs/preferences) builds more trust than a
  literal translation of English sales material. HMP already has this (Alex Mendez, bilingual FilthE) —
  the gap is making sure every printed/app script is written natively in Spanish, not translated word-for-
  word from the English version.
- **Home and family pride is a stronger frame than "storm damage repair"** for the everyday (non-storm)
  siding/roof pitch — Hispanic households are described as investing specifically in making the home
  reflect family identity and be welcoming for gatherings. Suggests leading with "keep your home looking
  its best for your family" rather than a purely transactional pitch, for the everyday door list.
- **Deciding who signs:** legitimate reason for HMP to ask who's on the deed/mortgage and who will sign —
  Nebraska's cancellation notice and the written contract need to reach the actual signer(s), and a
  contract signed by only one owner is easier to unwind later. This is a different, honest reason for the
  same question that some general sales sources warn is used manipulatively ("insist both spouses are
  present") to create pressure and remove the "I need to ask my spouse" exit — HMP should ask it as a
  paperwork fact, not a pressure tactic, and should never use it to block someone from taking time to
  decide.
- **Financing/paperwork worry:** no direct source on immigration fears at the door; the closest hard fact
  is that ITIN-based financing exists in the market broadly (mortgage-specific sources), but neither
  Hearth nor Wisetack's own acceptance of ITIN-only borrowers could be confirmed this round (pages
  blocked). HMP's safe default: never ask status; state that cash/check works with no paperwork beyond the
  contract, and that financing (if the homeowner wants it) is between them and the lender.
- **EN/ES kitchen-table lines** (add to Practice Door / Sale Guide):
  - Opening the paperwork step — EN: "Before we go further, can I ask whose name is on the house? That's
    who needs to sign, and it's who gets the 3-day cancellation notice — just how Nebraska law works, not
    a sales thing." / ES: "Antes de seguir, ¿me dice a nombre de quién está la casa? Esa persona es quien
    firma, y a quien le damos el aviso de cancelación de 3 días — así es la ley de Nebraska, no es nada
    más."
  - No-status reassurance — EN: "We never ask about immigration status — the only thing we need for the
    contract is your name. If you want financing later, that's between you and the lender, not us." / ES:
    "Nunca preguntamos sobre estatus migratorio — lo único que necesitamos para el contrato es su nombre.
    Si más adelante quiere financiamiento, eso es entre usted y el prestamista, no con nosotros."
  - Everyday (non-storm) pride frame — EN: "A lot of families on this street have been fixing up their
    homes — just want to make sure yours is looking its best too, and check if there's anything worth
    fixing before it gets worse." / ES: "Varias familias en esta calle han estado arreglando sus casas —
    quería asegurarme que la suya también se vea bien, y revisar si hay algo que arreglar antes de que
    empeore."

### Topic 2: follow-up mastery
- **Stat (widely repeated, not independently verified this round):** 80% of sales need about 5 follow-up
  touches; ~44% of salespeople stop after one. Supports keeping/expanding HMP's 2/5/10-day cadence rather
  than a single "checking in" text.
- **General cadence shape found (search-summary, roofing-specific blogs):** same-day text with
  photos/next step -> day 2-3 call to answer questions -> day 5-7 "have you compared bids, anything
  unclear?" -> day 10+ a new angle (schedule filling up, weather, a neighbor's finished job). This maps
  cleanly onto HMP's touch 1 (day 2) / touch 2 (day 5) / touch 3 (day 10), so no config change is needed —
  just script content per touch.
- **"I need to think about it" handling:** acknowledge without arguing, then ask ONE isolating question
  (price, timing, comparing another bid, wants to talk to a spouse) rather than repeating the pitch;
  pressing immediately makes prospects manage the rep toward the door instead of giving the real reason.
  This is the model for touch 1's script.
- **Best call times cited:** mid-to-late morning and late afternoon — informational only, no HMP-specific
  change needed (calltoday/app scheduling already leaves call timing to FilthE).
- **Channel rules (from CLAUDE.md, restated here for the scripts):** door revisit any time; phone call only
  to a number the homeowner gave AND who asked to be followed up with (residential); business-line call
  only for commercial leasing/PM offices; never a cold text — texting is fine only as a reply channel once
  the homeowner has already texted in (matches `docs/research/2026-09-26-followup-texts.md`, which this
  round doesn't duplicate — that file covers the day-of/booking texts; this round's file covers the later
  2/5/10-day stall-recovery touches).

`data/followup_scripts.json` has 9 entries: touch 1/2/3 x door / phone / business_phone channel, each with
EN + ES lines and a `when` field (matches the touch's actual trigger — "let me think about it" for touch 1,
no reply since for touch 2/3).

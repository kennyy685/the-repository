## Details

### 1. Employee Classification Act - verified against `nebraskalegislature.gov` (primary source, not a summary)

Fetched directly (Nebraska Revised Statutes, Chapter 48, §§ 48-2901 to 48-2912, "Employee Classification Act"):

- **§48-2903 (the actual test):** a construction-labor worker is *presumed an employee*, not a contractor, unless
  ALL three are true: (a) he meets the independent-contractor criteria in §48-604(5) - free from control/direction
  in fact, work is outside the usual course of the hiring business or done outside all its places of business, AND
  customarily engaged in his own independently established trade/business; (b) he was already **registered as a
  contractor** under Nebraska's Contractor Registration Act *before* starting the work; and (c) he has his own
  combined unemployment-tax rate or is otherwise exempt. Calling someone "1099 commission-only" satisfies none of
  these by itself - a second salesman who works only for HMP, under HMP's name, with no other clients and no
  contractor registration of his own, is presumed an employee no matter what the paperwork says.
- **§48-2907 (the penalty, confirmed exact and current):** administrative penalty **"not more than five hundred
  dollars per misclassified individual for the first offense and not more than five thousand dollars per
  misclassified individual for each second or subsequent offense,"** plus 15 working days to contest, plus (per
  §48-2908) the state can pursue unpaid unemployment tax, income tax withholding, and refer criminal violations
  to a prosecutor. This confirms round 29's $500/$5,000 figures were correct, from the statute itself.
- **New wrinkle round 29 missed:** §48-2906 and §48-2909 were **repealed in 2026** (Laws 2026, LB847/LB1048) - those
  sections existed when round 29's secondary source was written, so worth knowing the act was recently amended even
  though the core presumption and penalty sections (48-2903, 48-2907) are untouched and still in force.
- **§48-2910:** contractors must post a bilingual (English/Spanish) notice at the job site or place of business
  telling workers they have the right to be classified correctly and how to report it to the Department of Labor -
  a real, cheap compliance item once HMP has any crew/salesman on site, worth a one-page laminated posting.
- Bottom line for HMP: today's subcontracted crews are a different question (HMP is the sub, not necessarily the
  one classifying them); the risk this flags is specifically **if/when HMP itself hires a second salesman** on a
  commission-only basis. No new action needed until that hire is real - just don't structure it as pure 1099
  without the boss making that call knowingly.

### 2. Roof-chalking vs. adjuster-photo-acceptance (partially resolved, still not a government primary source)

Search results (WebFetch/curl both blocked for nachi.org, iroofing.org, griffithroofing.com - so this stays a
search-summary, not a fetched primary source; flagged unverified accordingly) converge on one practical answer that
resolves round 22's conflict enough to act on:

- The industry's actual method is the **"test square"**: pick a 10'x10' area on a slope that took hail, count hail
  bruises inside it (8+ bruises per 100 sq ft is a commonly cited threshold for "functional damage" worth claiming),
  and chalk **that one square** to keep count while inspecting.
- The "don't chalk" warning is really about **not handing the adjuster (or a photo record) a roof covered in chalk
  marks everywhere** - some carriers push back on heavily chalk-marked roofs because it can look like damage was
  manufactured or exaggerated, and it makes it hard to tell what's real impact vs. mechanical/manufacturing marks.
- **Practical rule to put in the print kit and the inspection checklist:** chalk one test square at a time for your
  own counting, take the photo of that square, then move on - don't leave the whole roof marked up, and never
  chalk over a spot before photographing the un-marked damage first.
- This is still not a government or manufacturer primary source (GAF's own guidance and a direct adjuster
  confirmation are still the ask in the backlog) - keep that half of the backlog item open, but the practical
  contradiction from round 22 is resolved enough to write into HMP's own materials now.

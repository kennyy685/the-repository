## Summary
1. **HOA unit-owner deductible questions have one correct move: redirect to the association, never
   discuss dollar amounts.** The roof is common property under the HOA's master policy; the individual
   owner's own claim path doesn't apply. Round 34 flagged this boundary; this round writes the actual
   word-for-word line (`hoa-unit-owner-deductible` in the new file) - closes that backlog item.
2. **A signed contract with another company (including a storm chaser) is never something to talk
   someone out of.** The only safe move is stating the 3-day cancel right as a fact they already have
   (not coaching them to use it) and offering a free second opinion - actively persuading someone to
   break a signed contract is a real civil-liability line (tortious interference), separate from the
   3-day right itself.
3. **"No damage found" is a trust asset, not a lost visit.** Say it plainly, show what was checked, and
   ask for the referral/anniversary follow-up anyway - manufacturing damage or implying "an adjuster
   will always find something" is the fraud line, not a sales technique.
4. Confirms round 29/30's existing "never say we can eat the deductible" rule extends naturally into the
   HOA context - same statute (44-8604), same public-adjuster-license boundary, just aimed at a board/
   management company instead of a single homeowner.

## Useful details

### Already-signed / storm-chaser competitor
- The framing that works across contractor-training sources: **listen, ask what's in their contract, and
  compare scope - never criticize the other company by name or claim they're a scam without proof.**
- If they signed in the last 3 days, Nebraska's 3-day right (69-1601) is theirs to use regardless of who
  they signed with - state it as a fact, don't coach the cancellation.
- Actively persuading someone to breach an already-signed, binding contract is tortious interference risk
  - the line is state-the-facts-and-offer-a-second-opinion, not push-them-to-cancel.
- "Storm chaser" concerns (crew leaves town, no local warranty support) are legitimate and fair to
  mention in general terms (being local, sticking around for warranty calls) without naming or
  disparaging a specific competitor.

### No damage found -> trust
- Honest-contractor guidance converges on: **document what was checked with photos, say plainly there's
  no damage, and don't manufacture a claim.** A contractor who "always finds something" is a red flag
  homeowners are trained to notice (BBB, Angi "free inspection scam" articles).
- The ask-anyway move (referral or "call us after the next storm") turns a no-sale visit into a future
  lead without any dishonesty - matches CLAUDE.md's referral thank-you rule (handwritten note only).

### HOA unit-owner deductible/claim boundary
- HOA master policies typically carry large deductibles (often 2-5% of building value per search
  summaries); when a claim doesn't clear that, or does but the deductible itself is billed back, the cost
  comes to unit owners as a **special assessment**, decided by the board, not something a contractor
  discusses dollar-for-dollar with an individual owner.
- A unit owner's own HO-6 policy loss-assessment coverage (commonly $1,000-$2,000 default limit) is the
  owner's own insurance question, not something HMP should quote or advise on - redirect to their agent.
- The one thing HMP can offer a concerned unit owner: get them the management company's contact and offer
  the board a written roof-condition report - a legitimate value-add that never touches deductible/claim
  dollars.

## Watch out
- Every source in this round is a contractor-marketing blog or consumer-advice site (ghostrep.ai,
  hookagency.com, theroofstrategist.com via search summary, honestaberoofing.com, various HOA-insurance
  explainer sites) - none of it is a Nebraska statute or a named court case. The legal_flag lines above
  rest on CLAUDE.md's already-verified statutes (44-8604, 69-1601) applied to these new situations by
  this round's own reasoning, not a new primary legal source.
- Don't let "second opinion" offers to an already-signed homeowner slide into contacting them repeatedly
  uninvited - one offer, then drop it, consistent with the no-cold-text/no-bought-list rule.

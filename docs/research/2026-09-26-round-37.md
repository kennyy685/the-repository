## Summary
1. **Contractor Registration Act (48-2101 to 48-2117):** confirmed the whole act. The one open question
   from round T38/contract-rules ("does the reg# have to print on contracts/ads?") is answered: **no** -
   nothing in the Act requires it. Printing it (current practice, T64) is good practice, not a legal
   must. Penalty structure matches the Employee Classification Act: $500 first violation / $5,000
   second+. One real risk found: the 60-working-day penalty-free grace period to register after a first
   citation expired in 2009 - so once HMP's boss's registration is active and current, keep it renewed
   every year (48-2107); a lapse isn't a slow-motion problem anymore.
2. **Home Solicitation Sales Act, sections 69-1606/69-1607** (the two sections of that Act nobody had
   fetched yet): sharpens the 3-day cancel rule already in CLAUDE.md. **69-1606(5): if HMP performs ANY
   service before the buyer cancels, HMP is entitled to ZERO compensation** for that work - not a
   reduced amount, none. This is stricter than the separate insurance-specific carve-out in 44-8603/8607
   (which lets a contract deduct the reasonable value of homeowner-approved emergency work like
   tarping) - that carve-out lives in the Insured Homeowners Protection Act, not here, so don't assume
   it covers a plain (non-insurance) home solicitation sale. 69-1607: a violation lets the buyer recover
   everything paid, plus damages, plus attorney's fees - real money at stake if the cancel notice is ever
   skipped or buried.

## Details

### Contractor Registration Act - who counts, what's required
- "Contractor" (48-2103) covers any firm doing construction (built explicitly to include roofing/siding-
  type work), AND any subcontractor, AND anyone providing/arranging labor for it. HMP's own subs (VTR,
  Nastase) and any second salesman doing contractor-type work would each need their own registration if
  they're separately contracting, not just working under HMP's registration as employees/subs of HMP.
- Registration application (48-2105) requires proof of workers' comp coverage (or a self-insurance
  certificate, or a signed statement it isn't legally required) - tying back to round 35's Employee
  Classification Act and round 29's misclassification-risk findings: worker classification keeps showing
  up as the recurring real risk area, not a one-off.
- Registration number format is 5 digits + 2-digit year (48-2108) - so a registration issued this year
  looks like `12345-26`.
- Public database (48-2117): the Department of Labor publishes contractor registration + workers' comp
  status online. Worth a quick look someday to see HMP's own listing and to check a subcontractor's
  status before using them, but not urgent.

### Home Solicitation Sales Act - the two missing sections
- 69-1606(1)-(4): ordinary logistics for returning goods/materials after a cancellation (buyer must take
  reasonable care of anything left with them, tender it back within 20 days, seller has 20 days to pick
  it up or it becomes the buyer's free and clear). Not urgent for HMP's own script since jobs rarely
  leave materials behind before day 3.
- 69-1606(5) is the one that matters: **no compensation for any service performed before cancellation.**
  This is a blanket rule under the general home-solicitation-sale statute (applies to any plain,
  non-insurance door sale, e.g. an "old house, worn siding" everyday sale) - separate from the
  insurance-specific 44-8603 language that permits deducting the value of homeowner-approved emergency
  repairs from a refund. Don't rely on the insurance carve-out language for a cash/non-insurance job.
- 69-1607: violating any part of 69-1601 to 69-1607 (the caption, the cancel form, the timing, this
  no-compensation rule) lets the buyer sue for everything paid back, plus damages, plus a reasonable
  attorney's fee. Another reason the cancel notice must be given every time, per CLAUDE.md's existing
  rule, with no exceptions for "but he asked us to come."

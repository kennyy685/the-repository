## Weekly self-review: the 5 numbers to check every Monday
Framed for a solo salesman with ADHD - a short checklist, not a spreadsheet. All 5 already come out of
`hh.py weekly` (`hailhunter/weekly.py: report()`), no new tool needed:

1. **Doors knocked** (`totals.doors`) - the base activity number. If everything else is low, check this first:
   most weeks that miss are a volume problem, not a skill problem (search-summary source: door-to-door teams
   check knocks as the "leading indicator" before anything else).
2. **Contact rate** (`totals.answered / totals.doors`, i.e. `totals` fields feed `by_kind`/`by_walk` `contact_rate`)
   - % of doors where someone actually answered. Low contact rate with good doors-knocked = wrong time of day or
   wrong neighborhood (renters/empty-nesters during work hours), not a pitch problem.
3. **Leads per 100 doors** (`totals` -> `leads_per_100` in `by_kind`/`by_walk`) - interested + booked, out of 100
   doors. This is the number that separates a volume problem from a pitch problem: good contact rate but low
   leads-per-100 means the doorstep conversation needs work, not more knocking.
4. **Inspections booked** (`totals.booked` / `inspection_rate_per_100`) - the real commitment, not just interest.
   Compare against `industry` (round 15's `data/benchmarks.json` ranges) to see if this week is normal-low or a
   real dip.
5. **Follow-ups overdue** (`follow_ups.overdue`, `follow_ups.due_today`) - open leads with a missed next-step date.
   This is the number most likely to quietly kill deals; check it every Monday even in a slow week, since a
   forgotten follow-up loses a lead that already said yes once.

Bonus, not one of the 5 but worth a glance: `areas.best`/`areas.worst` (which street/zone worked, which didn't)
tells you where to knock again vs. skip next week.

## Details

### Site-unseen-quote script (data/price_objections.json id: site-unseen-quote)
Multiple roofing-sales sources agree: never give a firm number without seeing the roof (attic, decking, layers,
ventilation aren't visible from the street or a photo) - a wrong phone guess either loses money or has to be
revised up later, which costs trust either way. If pressed, a wide range is okay, but say out loud it's a range,
not a quote, and make the free inspection the actual ask.

### Wants-three-bids script (data/price_objections.json id: wants-three-bids)
Same validate -> diagnostic-question -> reframe pattern already used for "cheaper bid elsewhere." Ask whether
they're the first bid or already have others (if others, ask what they liked/didn't like - turns the objection
into information). Reframe from lowest-price to scope/warranty comparison. Legal line: never talk them out of
getting other bids, never imply a competitor is dishonest without proof (tortious-interference risk, round 36),
never offer to "beat any price" (looks like disguised deductible-covering on an insurance job).

### Commercial decision-maker calling (data/commercial_calls.json)
- **Gatekeeper**: be specific and brief ("I noticed [property] took hail last [month], who handles roof/exterior
  maintenance") rather than vague ("I wanted to discuss our services") - vague asks get screened.
- **PM vs. asset manager**: PM identifies needs and gathers bids; asset manager (often off-site, works for
  ownership) approves capital spend and signs off the vendor. Ask the PM directly whether this needs
  asset-manager sign-off rather than guessing or trying to skip past them.
- **First-call goal**: a scheduled walk, a name/number, or permission to drop a one-pager - never a sale.
- **Cadence**: general B2B research says 5-12 touches over 2-3 weeks, 3-5 days apart, reply rates drop hard past
  a 7-day gap. Adapted to HMP's phone + in-person-only rule (no cold email/text): 5 touches over ~18 days
  (call, call, drop-by, call, call), stop on a clear no or "we have a vendor," re-approach only after the next
  storm.

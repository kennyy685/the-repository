# Research round 5 (2026-09-26): siding claims + cash siding sales

**Bottom line:** siding is where HMP can win claims that roof-only companies miss. Three things matter:
1. **Nebraska has a matching rule.** When new siding can't reasonably match the old (color, quality, size), the insurer
   must replace everything in the area so it looks uniform: 210 NAC 60-010, based on the NAIC model. The homeowner asks
   for it; HMP only documents the mismatch.
2. **Missed scope items are real money.** Trim, J-channel, house wrap and detach/reset often go missing from the first
   estimate ($1.5-3.5k per house for trim alone, one source). HMP documents what's on the wall; the homeowner's insurer decides.
3. **Cash siding on old houses** sells on an honest Vinyl vs Hardie comparison, a clear visit structure, and a
   cash-job version of the 3-day cancel notice.

## Useful details
**Signs of hail by material**
- **Aluminum** (siding, gutters, wraps): dents show easily. Look for spatter: bright spots where oxidation was knocked off; these fade over weeks.
- **Vinyl:** curved or star cracks and holes where the panel isn't supported; chips on bottom edges. Look on the storm-facing side.
- **Hardie / fiber cement:** needs a hard or wind-driven hit. Sharp-edged cracks or chips show raw material; age cracks look gradual, not sharp.
- **Soffit/fascia:** dented or pulled panels, loose fascia wrap. **Screens:** tears or dimples (often the first visible sign, since hail hits at an angle).
- Put a coin or tape in close-ups for scale. Circle hits before photographing. Photograph all 4 elevations: the pattern proves the storm direction.

**Matching (Nebraska):** 210 NAC 60-010 (claims settlement standards): when replacement items "do not reasonably match
in quality, color or size, the insurer shall replace all items in the area so as to conform to a reasonably uniform appearance."
The DOI enforces it; whether a homeowner can sue over it is unclear. **44-9204:** negotiating a first-party claim needs a
public-adjuster license. There is an exception for people who only gather facts or give technical help, which is HMP's role.

**Vinyl vs Hardie (honest talking points):** vinyl costs about $2-5/sq ft installed; Hardie about $4.70-8.50 (70-100% more).
Hardie is Class A fire-rated and lasts 30-50+ years (vinyl 20-40). Resale recoup is roughly 88% for Hardie vs 80% for vinyl
(cost-vs-value style data). Energy: only insulated vinyl adds R-value (about R-2 to R-4, ASTM D7793, printed on the label).
Say "up to, depends on your home"; never give a flat savings number.

**Objections:** "Too expensive": ask what number they had in mind, then show the comparison; don't just cut the price.
"Getting other bids": "Good, you should." Offer to compare the bids line by line. "Maybe next year": lock a specific
callback date now.

**Timing:** fall is a good window for cash siding (crews are free between storms). Spring suits owners planning to sell.

**Don'ts:** "We're doing your neighbor's house" only when it's literally true. Don't claim you can see damage from the
street. No energy-savings guarantees. No financing talk until a lender and its disclosures are set up (TILA/Reg Z; the
CFPB flags hidden dealer markups). Never mention deductibles (44-8604). Never promise insurance pays or negotiate the claim.

## Bottom line
1. **HMP's existing shot list (round 26) is already close to the trade standard** - elevations, soft metals,
   test squares, close-ups with scale, safety rules. This round didn't need to reinvent it, just fill real
   gaps and fix one live contradiction (see #2).
2. **There's an unresolved contradiction sitting in the repo right now.**
   `docs/research/2026-09-26-inspection-checklist.md` still says "chalk-circle every hit BEFORE the photo,"
   but `data/inspection_walk.json` (written after round 26 caught this) already corrected it to flag
   tape/grease pencil, citing round 26's own finding that InterNACHI warns chalk-circling "will create the
   illusion of larger damage than actually exists." **The old file was never fixed.** `data/photo_checklist.json`
   below follows the corrected (no-chalk) version; the old checklist doc should be updated to match so nobody
   copies the outdated line again.
3. **New, sourced additions this round, not in round 26:**
   - **Timestamps aren't as bulletproof as they look.** A phone's photo timestamp (EXIF metadata) is recorded
     automatically, but both iPhone and Android let a user edit the recorded date/time/location afterward
     [1]. That means the metadata alone isn't ironclad proof - a **visible** timestamp (burned into the image,
     not just metadata) is what survives being forwarded, screenshotted, or pulled into a report [1]. Worth
     a note in the app, not a build item: HMP doesn't need special hardware, but shouldn't rely on "the phone
     already dates it" as the whole story.
   - **"Context, not just the damage" is the single thing a working roofer/vendor source flagged as the
     photo mistake that costs jobs.** A TAMKO Building Products contractor-engagement director, in a trade
     podcast, named it directly: "the proper process for taking photos includes thinking about where the
     person on the receiving end of the photo can identify where it's located on the property, and why it's
     relevant to the roofing discussion" [2]. That's exactly what a house-number photo + elevation-labeled
     wide shots (already in round 26's plan) are for - this round adds it as the *why*, not just the *what*.
   - **You don't have to climb every roof to document it credibly.** Hover's own field guidance for
     restoration pros: eight ground-level smartphone photos, taken at the right angles, produce complete
     measurements for roof, siding, openings and interiors - "skip the ladder, avoid unsafe conditions" [3][4].
     This doesn't replace round 26's test-square-on-the-roof method where it's safe to climb, but it's a real,
     named fallback for a wet/steep/unsafe roof, which round 26 didn't have an alternative for beyond "skip
     it."
   - **A backup size reference beyond the coin/tape trick**: note one already-known measurement (like the
     front door's width) in your written notes as a fallback in case a close-up's scale object isn't clear
     later [3]. Cheap, no build needed - already added to the checklist as an optional step.
   - **"Documentation debt" as a plain-English argument for why this matters at all**, if FilthE or the boss
     ever needs to explain it to a new hire: inconsistent job-site documentation shows up later as disputes
     (no proof of pre-existing conditions), rework (no proof the work was done right), and slower/lower
     claim payouts - all three framed by CompanyCam as real, compounding costs, not just an annoyance [5].
   - **A real curriculum exists in the storm-restoration training world built specifically around this** -
     Balance Academy (a storm-restoration training channel) runs a "Parts Of A File" series that treats photo
     documentation as the *first*, foundational piece of a claim file (before the measurement report,
     insurance estimate, or contract) [6], and a separate 3-part series titled "Common Mistakes Contractors
     Make When Documenting and Reporting" [7][8][9]. **Flagged, not verified in detail**: individual YouTube
     watch pages were blocked this round (bot-detection wall, same as reddit), so only the titles, channel,
     and one-line search snippets were confirmed - not the actual 3 mistakes named inside. Worth a future
     round if a way to reach transcripts opens up.
4. **What stayed unreachable/unverified this round** (flag, don't build on): NRCA's technical/hail-specific
   pages (nrca.net homepage loaded fine, but every guessed hail/technical URL 404'd - NRCA's real technical

## The shot list (`data/photo_checklist.json`)
25 steps, English + Spanish, each tagged `when` (`roof` / `siding` / `gutters` / `wind` / `hail` / `any`) and
`required` (a must-shoot for that category, not a global requirement - a wind-only job skips the hail-only
steps entirely). Order matches round 26's walking order (ground → elevations → soft metals → siding →
ladder/roof → test squares → wind-specific → wrap-up), with this round's additions folded in at the point
they belong: GPS/timestamp check right after the address photo, the no-chalk correction on the test-square
steps, the backup reference-measurement as an optional step, and "show the homeowner" + "after photos" at the
end. Spanish follows the existing fixed pairs in `data/glossary_en_es.json` (ventila, tapajuntas, canaleta,
granizo, daño por granizo, siding, ajustador, etc.) - no new terms invented.

Documentation only, per CLAUDE.md: nothing here states a damage verdict, promises insurance will pay, or
suggests negotiating a claim. "Why" fields explain what a photo proves, never what an insurer will do with it.

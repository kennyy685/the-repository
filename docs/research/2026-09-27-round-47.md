## Summary
1. **The compliant opener has to be restructured, not just softened.** Every real trainer script found
   (SPOTIO, Roofr, Roofing Contractor) leads with a reason/hook *before* the rep's name and company - the
   opposite order from Nebraska's 69-1602 requirement (name, HMP, what you sell, first thing). HMP's
   version keeps their "get to the point fast, one clear reason, one open-ended question" shape but
   front-loads the legally-required opener first.
2. **The single scariest line found this round is one HMP must never say**: The Roof Strategist's and
   Aged Lead Store's "I don't want to file a claim / my rate will go up" objection scripts (search-summary
   only, not opened) tell reps to tell homeowners their insurance rate legally can't rise for an
   "Act of God" claim. That's an unverified, state-by-state legal claim dressed up as a sales line -
   it reads as promising an insurance-company outcome, which CLAUDE.md already forbids. HMP's rewrite
   below drops the promise entirely and just documents the damage, no pressure on if/when to file.
3. **Real, reusable mechanics, not just tone**: knock three times, never ring the bell (a sleeping baby is
   the stated reason); take two steps back and turn sideways after knocking (less intimidating, gives
   the homeowner room to size you up); ask "did I catch you at a good time?" before pitching. These are
   concrete adds to round 27's body-language checklist, not a repeat of it.
4. **Appointment-setting is a direct, specific-day ask, every source agrees** - "Can we get an inspection
   on the calendar this week? What day works best?" - matching round 27's existing "one small ask" rule
   with real word-for-word closing lines behind it.
5. **A real 2026 vendor homeowner survey (Roofr) says price is a weak objection to fear**: only 11% of
   respondents named price a top consideration; most cared about service, communication and trust. It
   also flagged a generational split - older homeowners (boomers) find door-knocking helpful, younger
   ones find it intrusive - worth knowing when picking which blocks to walk, not acted on this round.
6. **Nothing new on Spanish-speaking neighborhoods, for the third round running** (rounds 30/38 already
   found this gap). The one real, opened, on-topic signal: Roofing Contractor magazine itself launched a
   Spanish-language "Techos y Más" newsletter for Latino roofing contractors in 2024 - confirms the trade
   itself sees this as underserved, but still no actual bilingual sales-script training exists to pull
   from. Recommend not re-assigning this exact sub-angle again without new evidence.

## Useful details

### 1. First 10 seconds (opened sources)
- **Roofr**: "The first 15 seconds at the door WILL make or break the conversation... Lead with specifics
  and get to the point fast: 'Hi, I'm [name] with [company] — we're working down the street and noticed
  your roof has some missing shingles along the ridge line. I'd be happy to do a free inspection for
  you.'" Basics: dress cleanly, visible company logo, smile, eye contact.
- **SPOTIO (Trey Gibson)**: "field-tested storm-damage script": *"We help [City] homeowners replace
  storm-damaged roofs within about 10 days using a price-lock guarantee. About 90% of the homeowners
  we've been serving have had underpaid claims before we worked for them. How has the insurance process
  been for you?"* - **not usable as-is**: "price-lock guarantee" and "underpaid claims" both imply an
  insurance outcome HMP can't promise. Kept: ending on an open-ended question instead of a yes/no one
  (SPOTIO's own examples of what NOT to end on: "Would you like a free estimate?", "Is your roof okay
  after the storm?" - both invite a one-word "no").
- **Roofing Contractor magazine (Jill Bloom)**: "The Knock: always knock three times — never ring the
  doorbell, especially if a baby might be sleeping. The Stance: take two steps back and turn sideways
  after knocking... The Introduction: 'You must be Judy! I'm Jill with Bloom Roofing. I'm here to talk
  to families in [City] to make sure they're taken care of after the storm.'" Also: if you think someone's
  home but they're not answering, wave down the street like greeting a neighbor - reads as familiar with
  the area (a real tactic, use judgment - not deceptive, just don't fake having a specific reason to wave).
- **HMP's compliant version** keeps the same "short, one reason, one open-ended question" shape but
  starts with 69-1602's required order (name, HMP, what we sell) before the storm/neighbor hook -
  see `door_lines_pro.json` openers.

### 2. Top 5 objections (rewritten to HMP's rules)
1. **"I need to talk to my spouse"** - Roofr: "Of course. Can I email you an estimate right now so you
   have something to look at together later?" Kept close to verbatim - already legal.
2. **"I want a few more quotes"** - Roofr: "Of course. Here's mine. Happy to answer questions once you've
   had a chance to compare." Kept close to verbatim.
3. **"Not sure I can afford it"** - Roofr: "No pressure. Let me give you the estimate just so you have
   it. If you want, we can talk through payment options from there." Added HMP's existing rule (round 30):
   financing is between the homeowner and the lender, HMP never promises approval or terms.
4. **"Not interested" / "just had it done"** - Roofr: "No problem at all — if you need roofing work down
   the road, call us anytime for a free estimate. Here's my card." Kept close to verbatim.
5. **"I don't want to file a claim" / "my rate will go up"** - the one this round had to rewrite from
   scratch. Search-summary sources (Roof Strategist, Aged Lead Store) coach reps to claim insurance rates
   legally can't rise on an "Act of God" claim, and to push urgency because the roof's "lifespan is
   compromised." **Neither claim is used.** HMP's line instead: whether/when/how to file is the
   homeowner's call and their insurer's question, not HMP's; HMP will document the damage now regardless,
   no obligation to file anything. This keeps CLAUDE.md's "never promise insurance will pay" and
   "document damage, don't control the claim" rules intact for a real objection HMP will hear often given
   its insurance-restoration focus.

### 3. Setting the inspection appointment (opened sources)
- Roofr: "Asking for the business directly is something some folks are afraid to do. That will cost you
  jobs... Ask 'Can we schedule in an inspection this week? What days work best for you?'"
- SPOTIO: end every pitch on an open-ended question, then move to a named day/time; matches round 27's
  "one small ask, never a vague 'I'll call you'" rule with real closing wording behind it now.
- SPOTIO's own follow-up cadence (same-day text with photos, day 2-3 call, day 5-7 check-in, day 10+ new
  angle) matches `data/followup_scripts.json` (round 30) touch-for-touch - no change needed there.
- Roofr's 2026 homeowner survey: best answer rates weekday late afternoon (roughly 4-7pm) and weekend
  mornings; weekday mornings mostly miss people at work. Vendor-run survey, not independently verified,
  but a concrete, actionable number where round 30 only had general phone-call timing.

### 4. Spanish-speaking neighborhoods
- Still no real bilingual door-to-door sales-script content found anywhere reachable (third round
  confirming this gap - rounds 30, 38, now 47). Recommend treating this as answered-empty rather than
  reassigning the same narrow angle again.
- The one real signal: Roofing Contractor magazine (a trade publication, not a training vendor) launched
  a Spanish-language "Techos y Más" newsletter for Latino roofing contractors in Aug 2024 - the industry
  agrees this audience matters, it just hasn't produced sales-script training for it yet.
- Search-summary only (IKO, Roofing Contractor's IRE coverage, both blocked): younger Latino homeowners
  tend to be bilingual and comfortable in English; older-generation homeowners keep closer roots to their
  birth culture and prefer Spanish; relationship-first (not transaction-first) framing matters more than
  with English-speaking prospects; one good job in a Latino neighborhood can lead to several more through
  word of mouth. This matches round 30's finding and HMP's existing handwritten-thank-you referral rule
  (D17) - `door_lines_pro.json` adds a direct, no-pressure referral ask to say right after a good visit.
- "Latinos are not one-size-fits-all - over 20 Spanish-speaking countries" (search-summary) - reinforces
  why `glossary_en_es.json` already tracks regional word variants (e.g. MX-specific terms) rather than
  treating "Spanish" as one script.

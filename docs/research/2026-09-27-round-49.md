# Research round 49 (2026-09-27): sales track - the Spanish-speaking homeowner sale

Done directly by the Research Lead (no agent-launch tool available this session, same as rounds 38/45/47).
Brief: round 47 confirmed nobody in the industry has published real bilingual sales-script content for
this exact angle. This round tried the four specific source types the brief named - Nebraska/state DOI
Spanish consumer pages, Spanish contractor YouTube channels, Hispanic chambers, and bilingual sales
trainers - instead of another pass over general roofing-sales blogs.

**Network note:** same tight allowlist as every round since 25 - almost every domain tried was
proxy-blocked (`CONNECT tunnel failed, 403`): iroofing.org, kleberandassociates.com, iko.com, naic.org,
tdi.texas.gov, nomasfraudecolorado.gov, tn.gov, nj.gov, unidosus.org, espanol.nationwide.com,
nebraskahispanicchamber.org, es.certainteed.com, gaf.com (403). Two domains were fully open this round:
**doi.nebraska.gov** (a real, government-primary source, opened directly - new for this project) and
**roofingcontractor.com** (confirmed reachable again, per round 47 - this is what broke the topic open).

## Summary
1. **Real evidence finally surfaced, from the one reachable trade domain.** `roofingcontractor.com`
   hosts **Techos y Más**, Roofing Contractor magazine's Spanish-language section (round 47 only knew it
   existed; this round opened it). Its own **advisory board includes GAF, OC, Carlisle, SRS
   Distribution**, and its columns are written by named people, not anonymous marketing copy. Two
   columns by **Ricardo González, founder/CEO of Bilingual America** (a real bilingual-sales/culture
   training company - past clients include Coca-Cola, National Roofing Partners, Kimberly-Clark; he
   teaches SpanishPower/SuccessWithHispanics courses) gave the round its best material: **"sé su asesor
   de confianza, no un vendedor astuto"** (be their trusted advisor, not a clever salesman) and **"no les
   des falsas esperanzas"** (don't give false hope) - both a genuine bilingual trade-source restating of
   rules CLAUDE.md already requires (no promising insurance outcomes), now in real, natural Spanish
   instead of a rewrite of an English blog.
2. **Nebraska's own Department of Insurance brochure is a real, primary, verified source** - "After the
   Storm - The Disaster Claims Process" (PDF, opened directly). It tells homeowners to **"be present and
   watch when contractors inspect your home"** and gives a free consumer hotline
   (1-877-564-7323). Both are now grounded, checkable trust signals HMP can hand a homeowner, not just a
   sales tactic borrowed from a blog. The DOI's own consumer-tips page only offers a Google Translate
   widget - **no native Spanish version exists**, so HMP's own Spanish framing of this state material is
   genuinely additive, not a translation of something official already available.
3. **Family decision-making is collective and extends past the spouse.** Search-summary sources
   (UnidosUS, NAHREP-style reporting) describe Latino home-related decisions as often involving extended
   family (an uncle, siblings, grandparents) financially and as advisors, not just "talk to my spouse."
   This broadens round 30's spouse-only framing and gives the referral ask a wider, more accurate target
   than "a neighbor."
4. **"Usted" is the safe default, confirmed from general language sources** - in Latin American Spanish,
   business/professional interactions default to formal address; "when in doubt, use usted" is the
   universal advice, especially relevant since HMP's own research (round 47) already found older
   homeowners are more likely Spanish-dominant. This is a genuinely new addition: no prior round set an
   explicit register rule for HMP's Spanish scripts.
5. **A real, useful legal-vocabulary trap was found**: Spanish-language state consumer pages from
   licensing states (Texas DOI) tell homeowners to "hire only a **licensed** contractor" - accurate advice
   there, but it means a Nebraska homeowner may ask HMP for a "licencia" using exactly that word. HMP's
   scripts now include the direct, honest correction (Nebraska registers, doesn't license) rather than
   letting the moment pass unaddressed.
6. **Fear-of-scam vocabulary now has a real source**: Texas DOI's and Colorado's own Spanish
   consumer-scam pages (search-summary only, both proxy-blocked) name the two concrete red flags
   regulators tell Spanish-speaking homeowners to watch for - demanding full payment upfront, and
   high-pressure same-day signing. Stating plainly that HMP does neither answers the fear with the same
   specificity the warning itself uses, instead of a vague "we're trustworthy" line.

## Useful details

### Trust-building source: Ricardo González / Bilingual America (Techos y Más, Roofing Contractor)
Two columns opened directly via curl (roofingcontractor.com is reachable, confirming round 47's finding):
- **"Construyendo un Legado: Cómo Crear una Marca de Techos Extraordinaria"** (Oct 2024) - three-part
  framework (consciencia/conexión/consistencia). Key lines pulled into the playbook: "sé su asesor de
  confianza, no un vendedor astuto"; "no les des falsas esperanzas... dales un presupuesto claro, honesto
  y detallado"; "la última impresión es la que más importa" (the last impression matters more than the
  first - argues for consistent follow-through, not just a strong opener); "cariño" as the specific
  Spanish-language warmth concept to aim for, distinct from informality.
- **"SUCCESS WITH HISPANICS: How to Gain Trust in the Hispanic Community"** (2007, still live) - workforce/
  hiring-focused (Latino employees and supervisors), not homeowner sales, but its core mechanism is
  directly transferable: trust in Hispanic culture is earned through small, consistently-kept promises,
  not one big gesture, and is *not* assumed the way it often is in mainstream US business culture ("people
  will not trust you until you prove you can be trusted" - the reverse of the US default). This is the
  reasoning behind playbook entries 2 and 16.
- Also confirmed (Jill Bloom / Amparo Sancen articles): Techos y Más itself launched in 2024 with GAF, OC,
  Carlisle and SRS Distribution as partners, holds a full-day Spanish-language track ("Techos y Más EN
  VIVO," Dec 3, 2025, Arlington TX) at the industry's Best of Success conference - confirms the trade
  itself now invests real resources here, beyond just a newsletter (round 47 only found the newsletter).

### Nebraska DOI: a real primary state source, opened directly
- **"After the Storm - The Disaster Claims Process"** (PDF, doi.nebraska.gov, Rev. 03/18) - opened and
  read in full. Directly relevant lines: "Be sure you are present and watch when contractors inspect your
  home. Some unscrupulous companies will cause damage to drive up the repair cost..."; "Do not feel
  rushed or pushed to agree on a settlement"; "get estimates from contractors... known to you or
  recommended by someone you trust"; public adjusters "must be licensed as a consultant with the
  Department" (confirms round 27/36's public-adjuster-license line from the state's own consumer side,
  not just CLAUDE.md's existing statute citation); toll-free consumer hotline 1-877-564-7323.
- The DOI's "Consumer Tips for Weather-Related Disasters" page (opened) has a Google Translate widget
  embedded (`google_translate_element`) - confirms no native Spanish version of this specific page exists,
  so there is no official Nebraska-Spanish text to quote or contradict; HMP's own Spanish framing of the
  state's advice (playbook entries 12-13, 21) doesn't compete with or duplicate anything official.

### Family decision dynamics and register (search-summary only, consistent across several sources)
- Latino home-related decisions are frequently described as collective/extended-family rather than a
  single decision-maker; family co-borrowers (parents, grandparents) on financing are common. Source:
  UnidosUS and NAHREP-style Hispanic-homeownership reporting (pages themselves proxy-blocked; summarized
  by WebSearch only).
- "Usted" is the default in Latin American professional/business Spanish; "when in doubt, use usted" is
  the consistent advice across language-reference sources checked. No single source specific to
  door-to-door contractor sales addressed this directly - it's a general-Spanish-register finding applied
  to HMP's situation, not a sales-trainer's own rule.

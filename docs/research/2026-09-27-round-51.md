# Research round 51 (sales track): the commercial/apartment MEETING after the call gets booked

Single topic, done directly by the Research Lead (no `improvement-scout` subagent-launch tool available
in this session, same as rounds 38/45/48). Round 38 already covers *getting* the call booked (gatekeeper
script, PM-vs-asset-manager split, pre-call cadence); rounds 23/34 already cover commercial/apartment
background (TPO/EPDM, PM budget cycle, HOA/landlord contacts). This round starts where those leave off:
**the property walk-through itself, the bid package, how PMs compare bids, and how to follow up after
without pestering** - tied to HMP's live job, The Edge Apartments (5 buildings).

## Summary
1. **A multi-building property needs a per-building walk, not a whole-property blur.** Trade sources
   agree: photograph and log each building separately (elevations, especially the storm-facing side),
   check flat-roof drainage for standing water over ~48 hours (a real red flag, not cosmetic), and don't
   assume all buildings are the same age or condition - The Edge's 5 buildings are exactly this case.
2. **The bid package property managers actually want is a real, named checklist, not guesswork** - and
   it comes straight from a trade-association primary source (NRCA, direct fetch, verified): permanent
   business address, insurance + workers-comp certificates, references/completed jobs, a *written*
   proposal with complete scope, specific start/completion dates and payment procedures, and warranty
   terms explained plainly. HMP's existing vendor packet (`docs/print/vendor-packet/`, COI/W-9/lien
   waiver) already covers most of this - the gap this round found is the **scope-per-building, phasing/
   tenant-notice and safety-plan pieces**, which don't exist yet.
3. **A real 2006 trade-magazine consumer survey (direct fetch, verified) puts a number on two habits
   that are free to copy:** the contractor who won the job mentioned their manufacturer certification
   77% of the time (vs. 34% for a typical bid) and offered an enhanced warranty 71% of the time (vs.
   30% typical). Bids are still delivered in person 80% of the time, but only 40% come in an actual
   presentation folder. This is a residential survey, dated, but the two habits transfer directly and
   cost nothing to adopt: **use a real folder/PDF, say the warranty and registration out loud.**
4. **Occupied multi-building work needs a phasing order and a tenant notice**, not just a schedule -
   general practice (search-summary sourced, not Nebraska-specific) is a building-by-building sequence,
   notice a few days ahead of each building's start, and keeping work to daytime hours. No Nebraska
   landlord-tenant statute was checked this round - flagged below as unverified before this becomes a
   printed, legally-worded template.
5. **Follow-up after a bid is a different rhythm than the pre-call cadence round 38 already set.**
   General sales-practitioner sources agree: one check-in 1-2 days after delivery reads as attentive,
   not pushy, especially if it offers to explain something rather than just asking for a decision; space
   out further touches over weeks, not days; stop hard on any "no" or "we picked someone else."
6. **Safety plan**: OSHA requires a written, site-specific fall-protection plan by a qualified person
   for any work at 6 feet or higher - low-slope roofs allow guardrails, nets, personal fall-arrest
   systems or warning lines. `osha.gov` itself was blocked to direct fetch this session (network
   egress), so this is stated as the well-known federal standard, not quoted from the primary page -
   flagged below to re-verify once osha.gov is reachable.

## Useful details

### The walk-through (multi-building)
- Walk and photograph **each building separately** and label photos by building - don't treat a
  5-building property as one property. [1][2]
- Check each flat/low-slope roof's **drains for standing water**; water sitting more than ~48 hours is
  treated as a real red flag for membrane stress, not just an eyesore. [1]
- Look for the same soft-metal/creased-siding/spun-turbine signs already in HMP's inspection checklist
  (round 26), just repeated per building rather than once for the whole property. [1][2]
- Ask about **interior water stains, tenant-reported leaks, and any active roof warranty** - a live
  manufacturer warranty on one building can change the scope from replace to repair, and buildings on
  the same property are often different ages. [1][2]
- Get logistics answers before leaving: parking/dumpster placement, tenant occupancy hours, gate/key
  access, day-of contact - these become the phasing and tenant-notice plan in the bid, and naming them
  unprompted is a small thing that reads as thorough. [3][4]

### What property managers actually check before picking a contractor (NRCA, direct-fetched primary
source - nrca.net/roofing-guidelines/selecting-a-contractor)
Quoted directly from NRCA's own consumer-facing "Selecting a Contractor" guidance: check for a
permanent place of business, phone number, tax ID and business license; ask for copies of liability
insurance and workers' compensation certificates in effect through the job; look for a proven track
record and client references; **"insist on a written proposal and look for complete descriptions of
the work and specifications, as well as start and completion dates and payment procedures"**; check
industry-association membership; call the local BBB; ask who will supervise the project and how many
workers will be on-site; **"carefully read the warranty and make sure you understand the provisions
that will void it"**; and be skeptical of the lowest bid - "price is only one criterion." [5]
This is essentially HMP's existing vendor packet plus two things not yet built: **who supervises this
specific job and how many people will be on site**, and a plainly-stated warranty explanation at
delivery time, not just a printed clause.

### The bid package structure trade sources describe
A commercial roofing proposal should answer the same scope questions for every bidder so a PM can
compare apples-to-apples: what comes off, what goes back, insulation type/thickness, fastening method,
seam spec, edge-metal scope, and detail treatment at every penetration - broken out by area/building,
with a drain map and a defined process for what happens if the deck needs replacing once opened up. [6][7]

### The two habits that separate a winning bid (Roofing Contractor magazine's 2006 Consumer Survey,
direct-fetched primary source - roofingcontractor.com/articles/85553)
This is a residential survey (94% single-family respondents, data from 2006) but the two findings
transfer directly and cost nothing: **the contractor who won the job mentioned manufacturer
certification 77% of the time versus 34% for a typical bid, and offered an enhanced warranty 71% of
the time versus 30% typical.** Bid presentations are given in person 80% of the time, but only 40% are
in a real presentation folder and 29% are just mailed. [8]

### Phasing and tenant notice for occupied buildings
General practice (not opened at a primary source this round): phase the job building by building so
the property stays occupied, publish the building order up front, and give notice through more than
one channel (a letter/notice per unit plus email if the PM has one) a few days before work starts on
that building - typical notice windows cited are 24-48 hours minimum, work kept to daytime hours
(commonly 7am-6pm weekdays). [9][10]

### Safety plan
Federal OSHA requires a written, site-specific fall-protection plan (developed by a "qualified
person") for work at 6 feet or higher; low-slope roofs may use guardrails, safety nets, personal
fall-arrest systems, or a warning-line system combined with another protection method. Multi-story
occupied buildings with tenants/cars moving below raise the stakes on site communication (barricades,
posted warnings) beyond a vacant single-family roof. [11] (osha.gov itself blocked to direct fetch this
session - see Watch out.)

### Follow-up without pestering (post-bid, not pre-call)
Distinct from round 38's pre-call cadence (which is about getting the walk scheduled in the first
place): once a written bid is delivered, one follow-up **1-2 days later** reads as attentive rather
than aggressive, especially framed as "does anything need explaining" rather than "have you
decided." [12] Space any further touches out over weeks rather than days, and stop immediately on a
clear no or "we picked someone else" - log it and only re-approach after the next storm or the
property's next budget cycle, matching the stop-rule HMP already uses for the pre-call cadence
(`data/commercial_calls.json`).

## Watch out
- Everything under "Phasing and tenant notice" and "Follow-up without pestering" is WebSearch-summary
  sourced (the specific vendor/blog domains stayed blocked to WebFetch and curl this session) - treat
  the 24-48hr notice figure and the exact follow-up day-counts as directional, not verified.
- No Nebraska-specific landlord-tenant statute was checked this round for construction-entry notice -
  don't print the tenant-notice template as a legally-required notice until that's confirmed (item #5
  above). This is separate from the already-verified 3-day cancel right (69-1601/69-1606) and
  deductible-notice statutes (44-8607), which stay unchanged.
- The 2006 consumer-survey numbers (item 3) are residential, not commercial/apartment-specific, and are
  20 years old - used here only for the two low-risk, low-cost habits (folder, saying the warranty/
  certification out loud), not as a claim about commercial buyer behavior specifically.
- Hard rules unchanged and baked into `commercial_bid_kit.json`'s wording: never promise insurance
  pays, never negotiate a claim on the property's behalf, business lines only (leasing office/PM/HOA
  board contact, never a resident's personal cell), "registered" - never "licensed" - and nothing about
  covering, waiving or rebating a deductible (44-8604) if any building's damage becomes an insurance
  claim.

## Plain lines for FilthE
- For The Edge or any multi-building property: walk and photograph each building on its own, not the
  whole property at once - they're probably not all the same age or condition.
- Bring the vendor packet (COI, W-9, lien waiver, registration #) to every commercial walk even if
  nobody asks for it yet - property managers are trained to expect exactly that packet.
- Two free things that a real survey found the winning bid usually does: hand over the proposal in an
  actual folder or clean PDF (not just texted or emailed loose), and say your warranty and registration
  out loud when you present it.
- After you drop off a bid, one follow-up call 1-2 days later is normal and expected - just make it
  "does anything need explaining," not "did you decide yet."
- If it's a no, or they picked someone else, stop calling that property - only try again after the next
  storm or when their budget year turns over.
- Standard reminders stay: "registered," never "licensed"; never say insurance will pay for anything;
  never talk them out of a competitor's bid; business lines only, never a tenant's personal number.

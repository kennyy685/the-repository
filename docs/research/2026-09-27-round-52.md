## The details

### 1. Fall wind, fallen-tree and ice-dam claims

**Ice dams - what's covered, straight from Nebraska's own regulator** (doi.nebraska.gov, two Consumer
Alert PDFs, fetched directly this round):
- Ice dams form when heavy snow melts off a warm roof and refreezes at the cold eave; the ice works
  under the shingles and causes interior water damage once it melts again - a maintenance/insulation
  problem, but the resulting water damage is generally treated like other covered water damage.
- **What's covered:** roof collapse from the weight of snow/ice; water that gets inside through a hole
  or broken window caused by a *covered* event (wind, falling tree limbs); frozen/burst pipes.
- **What's NOT covered:** the trees/shrubs themselves (loss of a tree to wind/ice/snow is excluded);
  yard debris cleanup, *unless* the debris actually damaged a covered structure (a branch that missed
  the house doesn't get a paid cleanup).
- **Prevention angle HMP can sell:** clear gutters + attic insulation/ventilation reduce the
  freeze-thaw cycle that causes ice dams in the first place - a legitimate maintenance/upsell
  conversation, not a claims pitch.
- **How to file:** contact the insurer, take photos before disposing of anything, keep records; the
  DOI's consumer hotline is **1-877-564-7323** (already wired into print via T177).

**Claim-deadline rule, Nebraska-specific (re-verified this round):** the national "1-2 years from
date of loss" line people hear elsewhere is a floor, not the real Nebraska limit. **Neb. Rev. Stat.
44-357** (read in full this round) bars an insurer from writing *any* policy provision that limits the
time to sue to less than "the regular period of time prescribed by the statutes of limitations of this
state" - and **25-205** sets that at **5 years** for a written contract (which an insurance policy is).
So a Nebraska homeowner almost always has far longer than the "hurry, it's been a year" line some
contractors use. This doesn't change HMP's rule (`claims-101.html` already says "years, not days,
check your policy, we never rush you with a deadline" - keep saying exactly that, never a number) -
it just confirms the rule is right and gives FilthE the "why" if a homeowner pushes back. Practical
advice to homeowners stays the same regardless of the legal floor: document damage and file soon,
because evidence fades and adjuster/contractor schedules get tight in winter - never state it as a
legal deadline.

**Fallen tree, quick recap (already in the wind-playbook, unchanged):** safety first, no roof access
with a tree still on it, photograph before anything moves, tarp once safe, tree removal is a tree
service's job, whether the tree is "covered" is the insurer's call.

### 2. "Pre-winter inspection" offers - the legal-safe version

Real contractors run offers like "Pre-Winter Roof Check - Book Before October 15th" and "Is Your Roof
Ready for Snow?" through September-November, while asphalt shingles still seal at 40°F+ [3]. That
pattern is fine for HMP to copy - **with these wording rules, non-negotiable:**
- Say **"free inspection,"** never **"free roof."** A "free roof" hook implies the homeowner pays
  nothing, which edges toward covering/waiving the deductible - banned under **44-8604** (verified
  text: a residential contractor "shall not promise to rebate any portion of an insurance deductible
  ... includes granting any allowance or offering any discount ... or paying an insured ... any form
  of compensation, except for any item of nominal value").
- No deductible talk at all, in the offer or at the door - that's the homeowner's and the insurer's
  business.
- Never say insurance will pay, or push urgency using a false deadline - say "your insurance company
  decides," same as the existing wind-playbook script.
- Open with name + HMP + what we sell + "registered Fremont company" (69-1602), before the seasonal
  hook - not the other way around.

### 3. Winter siding installs + financing

**Temperature limits (cross-checked against `off-season-plan.md`'s numbers, both close, refined):**
| Material | Floor | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Asphalt shingles | ~40°F+ to self-seal | Hand-seal (dab of roofing cement) below that [3] |
| Vinyl siding | ~35-40°F is where it starts getting brittle; most makers won't warrant it below ~20°F | Cold vinyl needs wider expansion gaps and slower, sharper cuts [4] |
| James Hardie ColorPlus/pre-finished | **Not below 30°F** | Caulk and touch-up paint won't bond right at or below 30°F [5] |
| James Hardie plain fiber cement | Can go colder (~20°F) with the right cold-rated caulk and an experienced crew | Siding stored outside overnight can be colder than the air temp even on a "warm enough" day - check the material, not just the forecast [5] |

**"Book now, build in spring" deposits:** contractors nationally do lock in fall pricing/scheduling
with a deposit for a spring build slot, and there's a real reason to: shingle/siding manufacturer
price hikes often land in early spring [6]. Evidence here is thinner (small contractor blogs, no big
named company), so treat this as a reasonable, low-risk idea to try, not a proven tactic.

**Financing promos - watch the "0% financing" trap:** many roofers' 0% offers are actually *deferred
interest*, not truly free - if the balance isn't paid off by the promo deadline, the deferred interest
(commonly 18-26% APR) gets charged all at once, and the contractor typically pays the lender 6-8% of
the job cost as a fee, built into the price [7]. HMP's already-picked lender, **Wisetack** (T109), is
cleaner: confirmed straight from wisetack.com in round 43, its terms are $500-25,000, 0-35.9% APR,
3-120 months, no participation fee. Keep pushing Wisetack in the off-season rather than a
deferred-interest retail card - it's honest with a Spanish-speaking homeowner and there's no gotcha to
explain later.

### 4. Winter door-knocking

- **Daylight is the hard limit, not a preference.** Darkness by ~4:30-5 PM Nov-Feb [8]. Practitioner
  advice: don't stop working once it's dark - **switch to setting an appointment** for a daylight
  return instead of cold-knocking blind after dusk [8]. This matches (and sharpens) round 47's T175
  and `off-season-plan.md`'s Nov/Dec windows.
- **Winter is for referrals and pipeline, not just fresh knocks.** Reps use the slower season to work
  the CRM/pipeline - follow up on stalled estimates, ask happy customers for referrals, revisit old
  storm leads that never closed [1][8]. Matches HMP's existing Dec-Feb plan (call Nastase/VTR, work
  estimates) - just confirms it's the industry-standard move, not just an HMP guess.
- **Holidays: knock, but read the room.** More people are home around Thanksgiving/Christmas, which
  can mean more doors answered - but also more people who are busy, stressed, or annoyed at an
  unscheduled visit. Be brief, polite, and take no for an answer fast; skip late evenings and the
  holiday's actual dinner hour [9].

## Unverified / flag for later
- The "book now, build in spring" deposit model (item 3 above) - only small-contractor blog evidence,
  no named regional or national company confirmed doing it. Try it small before building it into a
  print offer.
- Whether Nebraska courts still let an insurer deny a claim for "unreasonably late notice" under a
  policy's general notice condition, separate from the 44-357/25-205 suit-deadline floor above - this
  round found the statute (clear), not case law on notice timing. Don't use 44-357 to tell a homeowner
  "you have 5 years, no rush" - keep the existing "document and file soon" advice.
- jameshardie.com's own installation-instructions library page loaded but didn't expose the
  temperature specs directly in this session (its Best Practices PDF is behind a document picker);
  the 30°F ColorPlus figure and 20°F fiber-cement figure came from WebSearch's summary of that guide
  plus third-party contractor sites repeating it, not a PDF opened directly this round.

## D26: Build through winter, or hold non-emergency leads for spring?

**Outside view.** Standard-form (ISO HO-3) homeowner policies - the kind used in Nebraska too -
require "prompt" notice of loss with no fixed day count, but a formal proof-of-loss only once the
insurer asks for one (typically a 60-day window from that request) [1]. Practitioner guidance is
consistent: waiting to *file* until spring risks the claim being disputed or getting bigger (small
damage becomes a bigger, costlier claim, which can even trigger non-renewal), and many manufacturer
warranties expect prompt repair once damage is found [2]. Separately, temperature floors are real and
already documented (round 52, unchanged): asphalt shingles self-seal at ~40°F+ (hand-seal below),
vinyl gets brittle under ~35-40°F, James Hardie ColorPlus needs ≥30°F for caulk/paint to bond, plain
fiber cement can go colder with the right cold-weather caulk [3]. Nebraska's actual legal deadline to
sue an insurer is 5 years (44-357/25-205, verified in round 52) - far longer than homeowners usually
assume, so there's no legal reason to rush **filing**, only a practical one (evidence freshness,
warranty timing, and not losing the lead to a competitor who inspects sooner).

**Around the table (one line each):**
- *Homeowner:* wants the damage looked at before it gets worse, but is wary of being rushed by
  another "storm chaser."
- *Adjuster:* doesn't care when the physical repair happens, but wants damage documented while it's
  fresh and unambiguous.
- *Competing roofer:* most local competitors slow way down once it's cold - a real opening for HMP to
  keep inspecting/filing (not necessarily building) all winter.
- *The boss:* wants crews paid and busy, but not committed to a job they can't finish to spec in a
  cold snap.
- *FilthE at the door, 5 PM, tired:* needs one honest sentence, not a seasonal policy memo.
- *A Nebraska AG investigator:* is watching for a false deadline ("file now or lose your claim") -
  there isn't one, and HMP must not imply there is.

**Pre-mortem ("it's spring and this failed"):** Two ways this goes wrong - (a) HMP told leads to
"wait for spring," and by spring several had already signed with a competitor who inspected and filed
immediately even though the repair came later; or (b) HMP pushed full installs through a hard cold
snap, shingles never sealed right, and spring brought callbacks/warranty disputes. Both are avoided by
splitting the decision in two.

**One-way or two-way door:** "Inspect and file every lead now, year-round" is reversible and safe to
adopt immediately - a two-way door. "Run a full install below a material's temperature floor on a
specific job" is closer to one-way (a warranty/liability commitment once shingles are hand-sealed
cold) - that one call stays with FilthE/the boss, per job, not a blanket policy.

**Answer:** Inspect and file every lead right away, all winter - never tell a homeowner to wait.
Schedule the actual **build** for the next stretch of days above the material's floor (often just a
couple of weeks out in eastern Nebraska, not necessarily spring); only push a real spring date if a
long, hard cold snap is forecast, and even then get the paperwork/claim moving today.

**One-line door offer -**
EN: *"We can start your free inspection and get your claim moving today - and do the work as soon as
the weather allows, no need to wait for spring."*
ES: *"Podemos empezar su inspección gratis y su reclamo hoy mismo - hacemos el trabajo en cuanto el
clima lo permita, no hay que esperar hasta la primavera."*

**Default we use now:** none written down yet (round 52 raised it as D25, unanswered until now).

**Still needs a person?** No decision needed - this is the policy. FilthE/boss still make the
per-job call on whether a specific cold-weather install date is safe to commit to (normal judgment,
not a new question).

## D20: What paper before the adjuster comes - inspection permission, contingency agreement, or the full contract?

**Outside view.** Nationally, insurance-restoration contractors commonly use a "contingency
agreement": a conditional contract that authorizes inspection/documentation and commits the homeowner
to that contractor *if and when* the insurer approves the claim, with price tied to the approved scope
rather than fixed at signing [4][5]. Consumer-side sources warn this same document is exactly how
predatory "storm chasers" lock homeowners in before they've seen a real price - the difference is
entirely in the wording [4]. Nebraska's own Insured Homeowners Protection Act already anticipates this
exact document: 44-8603 defines and gives cancellation rights to "a written contract... to provide
goods or services to be paid from the proceeds of a property and casualty insurance policy" - Nebraska
already has a legal shape for a contingency agreement, it isn't a gray area [6].

**Around the table:**
- *Homeowner:* doesn't want to feel locked in before a real price exists; wants to know they can still
  say no.
- *Adjuster:* fine with HMP being present and documenting; not fine with HMP "negotiating for" the
  homeowner - that needs a public-adjuster license (44-9204), already confirmed in round 43-verify [7].
- *Competing roofer:* locking a homeowner in early, before the adjuster even shows up, is the
  storm-chaser move - a legit local shop should make the paper feel different, not just call it
  something nicer.
- *The boss:* wants the document to actually hold HMP's spot as contractor once approved - not just a
  photo-permission slip a homeowner can take to a cheaper bidder after HMP did the legwork.
- *FilthE, tired, at the door:* needs one document, in both languages, he can explain in one sentence.
- *A Nebraska AG investigator:* checks that the 3-day cancel notice, the deductible notice, and "we
  don't negotiate your claim" are all in there - not quietly dropped because it's "just" a contingency
  paper.

**Pre-mortem:** Three ways this fails - (a) HMP uses a bare inspection-permission slip, nothing
commits the homeowner once the claim is approved, and a competitor's cheaper bid wins the job HMP
found and documented; (b) HMP uses a locked-price contingency agreement before a real scope exists,
the insurer's approved number differs, and the homeowner feels tricked - the exact pattern regulators
warn about (round 27); (c) the contingency paperwork skips the 3-day cancel notice or the deductible
notice because "it's not the real contract yet," and the whole thing is void under 44-8608.

**One-way or two-way door:** One-way - it's a signed document a homeowner relies on. The wording goes
through Designer/Code now, but needs FilthE + the boss's yes before first use, and the lawyer review
CLAUDE.md already deferred is the one thing that must still happen before this goes to a homeowner.

**Answer:** Use a **contingency agreement**, not a bare inspection slip and not the full priced
contract. It must:
- Authorize inspection, documentation, and meeting the adjuster - never "negotiating" (keep the
  44-9204 line already in `docs/guides/adjuster-meeting.md`).
- State plainly that it only becomes a real job if/when the insurer approves, with price tied to the
  approved scope, not fixed today - this is what keeps it from being the storm-chaser pattern.
- Include, because it's "paid from insurance proceeds" under 44-8601-8608: the 44-8607 five-sentence
  deductible notice (EN+ES, already drafted), 44-8603's insurance-specific cancellation right, AND the
  general 69-1601 three-day cancellation form (both apply - already settled in CLAUDE.md), plus HMP's
  business mailing address.
- Charge nothing for the inspection/paperwork itself before the cancel period ends (69-1606(5)).
- Never include an assignment of benefits / co-payee clause (44-8605) - HMP doesn't do that today, so
  leave it out entirely; simpler and safer.

**Default we use now:** unconfirmed before this round - rounds 27/31 both flagged "which document HMP
actually uses at the door" as an open question; `data/inspection_walk.json` step 5 assumed
authorization-only, not confirmed practice.

**Still needs a person?** Yes - the exact contingency wording needs the lawyer pass CLAUDE.md already
deferred, before it's used on a real homeowner (a signature document, not a fact question).

## D18: Does HMP need Nebraska registration to offer third-party financing (Wisetack, GreenSky, Hearth)?

**Answer: No.** Neb. Rev. Stat. § 45-336(4)-(5) (Nebraska Installment Loan and Sales Act, fetched
directly this round) requires an installment *sales* license only for someone who "acts as a sales
finance company" - defined at § 45-335 as a person who *purchases installment sales contracts* or
*acquires rights of ownership/servicing* in them. A "seller who does not otherwise act as a sales
finance company" needs no license, "but such seller shall comply with all of the other provisions...
in order to charge the time-price differential" (i.e., only if the seller itself marks up the price
for letting someone pay over time) [8][9]. HMP referring a homeowner to Wisetack's own application -
where Wisetack is the actual lender, does its own underwriting, and HMP never extends credit or holds
the paper - doesn't make HMP a "seller charging a time-price differential" or a "sales finance
company." This matches how these platforms work everywhere: Wisetack/GreenSky/Hearth are built so
unlicensed contractors nationwide can offer them; the lender carries the licensing.

**Default we use now:** Wisetack is already the picked lender (T109, confirmed directly against
wisetack.com in round 43) - no change needed.

**Still needs a person?** No.

**Sources:** [8][9] below.

## D16: Spanish glossary - Mexican vs. Central American usage for deductible, adjuster, claim, supplement, depreciation, roof, siding, gutters, hail

**Checked first:** `data/glossary_en_es.json` already exists (v2026-09-26, from round 13) with 47
terms, safest-default wording, an explicit "never pair deducible with cubrir/eliminar/rebajar/pagar/
regalar" rule, and MX region-tags on a handful of construction words (shingle, metal roofing,
flashing, siding) [10].

**Answer:** The insurance-specific words in this question - *deducible, ajustador, reclamo,
depreciación, suplemento* - are standardized industry/legal terms and this round found no real
Mexican-vs-Central-American split for them; what regional variation exists is mostly in everyday
construction words, which the glossary already flags (region: MX) where it matters. One real nuance
confirmed: "perito" for adjuster is heard in Spain/South America, not Mexico/Central America - the
glossary already says "don't use it," which this round's search corroborates rather than contradicts
[11]. The one gap worth closing: "suplemento" is correct insurance vocabulary everywhere, but it's not
an everyday word for most homeowners regardless of dialect - it needs a plain-language explanation at
the door ("cosas extra que el seguro aprueba después"), not a regional swap.

**Default we use now:** `data/glossary_en_es.json` as-is; add one `note` field to the existing
"supplement/suplemento" entry with that plain-language line (Code, effort S).

**Still needs a person?** No. Flag as unverified: this round's search did not turn up

## D14 (research only): Class 4 (UL 2218) shingles - install requirements, price, Nebraska insurer discounts

**Answer:** Physically, Class 4 shingles nail down the same way as any architectural shingle - no
special license is needed to install them. But getting the *enhanced* manufacturer warranty (and, per
several roofing-industry sources, sometimes the insurance discount) commonly expects a
manufacturer-certified installer and matching manufacturer accessories (starter strip, ridge cap) -
that's a manufacturer/warranty condition, not a Nebraska legal requirement [14]. Price premium:
commonly cited at roughly 10-25% more than standard architectural shingles, or about $1,200-3,000 more
material cost on a typical house, though figures vary a lot by brand and were only found in
contractor-blog sources, not a manufacturer price sheet [15]. Nebraska insurer discounts: confirmed
directly from the Nebraska Department of Insurance's own page that discounts for "impact-resistant
roofing and siding" (Class 3 or 4) are real and worth asking about, but the DOI does **not** publish a
fixed percentage or an insurer list - it tells homeowners to ask their own insurer [16]. Named insurers
elsewhere (State Farm, USAA, West Bend) reportedly offer such discounts, but that was a
lower-confidence forum/blog find, not Nebraska-specific and not independently confirmed this round
[17].

**Default we use now:** none - Class 4 isn't on the price sheet yet; this is a boss decision
(`docs/orders/roadmap.md` already lists it as D14, blocking the price sheet/contract).

**Still needs a person?** Yes - whether to actually offer Class 4 is the boss's call (cost/price
premium is real money), this round only supplies the facts to decide with.

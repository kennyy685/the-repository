# Round 56: Job #1 playbook — signed contingency to final payment (T191)

Not a scout round (worked alone, per the King's brief; a builder is running in parallel). Job #1 = HMP's first
direct insurance job where **HMP is the main contractor**: its own yard sign, its own supplier account, buying its
own materials — different from today's normal pattern (subbing labor for VTR/Nastase, who supply materials).
Everything legal here reuses rounds 25/28/37/39/43-verify's verified statute text and round 53's D20 contingency
decision — nothing new was re-litigated. Web was only used for two real gaps (mortgage-endorsement mechanics,
supplier credit accounts), both search-summary only (every direct-fetch attempt this round — lincoln.ne.gov,
cityofomaha.org, abcsupply.com, beaconbp.com, theroofcrafters.com — was blocked at the proxy or the site's own
CDN); flagged below.

**One blocking gap before Job #1 can even start:** the contingency agreement itself isn't built yet. Round 53
(D20) answered *what it must say*; nobody has written it as a real print piece, and CLAUDE.md's contract-lawyer
review is still deferred. Step 1 below can't happen for real until Designer builds it and it gets whatever review
FilthE/the boss decide is enough for a first real signature. Everything after Step 1 assumes it exists.

## The runbook

Each step: **Who** · **Papers** (existing pieces linked by path) · **Money** · **Typical days** (from claims-101's
own day ranges, `docs/print/claims-101.html`, unless noted) · **What can go wrong**.

### 1. Sign the contingency agreement
- **Who:** FilthE, at the door/kitchen table, after the free inspection.
- **Papers:** none built yet — this is the gap above. Content spec: round 53 (D20) — authorize inspection/
  documentation/meeting the adjuster only (never "negotiating," 44-9204); state plainly the price is tied to
  whatever the insurer approves, not fixed today; bake in the 44-8607 deductible notice (`docs/legal/44-8607.txt`,
  already 5-sentence-verified) and the 69-1601 three-day cancel notice (both EN+ES per 69-1604(3)); HMP's mailing
  address (2600 Laverna St, Apt 50, Fremont, NE 68025); no assignment-of-benefits clause (44-8605) — HMP doesn't
  do that today, leave it out.
- **Money:** $0 charged. **Must** stay $0 until the cancel window passes — see the watch-out below.
- **Typical days:** day 0 (same visit as the free inspection).
- **What can go wrong:**
  - The contingency agreement is itself a "home solicitation sale" under 69-1601 (a contract for services, signed
    at the buyer's home, over $25). **69-1606(5): "If a seller performs any services pursuant to a home
    solicitation sale prior to its cancellation, the seller shall not be entitled to compensation."** Nothing HMP
    does at this stage (inspect, document, meet the adjuster) is billed separately, so this is likely moot in
    practice — but it means the contingency paper must never quietly slip in a fee for the inspection/paperwork
    itself before day 3 passes. Flag for the lawyer pass, don't guess.
  - **44-8603(1)** gives a *second*, independent 3-day cancel clock: the *later* of (a) 3 business days after
    signing, or (b) 3 business days after the insurer's written notice that all or part of the claim isn't
    covered. If the insurer later denies the claim, the homeowner can still walk away even after the "normal" 3
    days passed — the contingency's price-tied-to-approval structure already assumes this, but FilthE should know
    it out loud, not be surprised by it.
  - Signing a priced, locked contract here instead (the "storm chaser" pattern, round 27/53) — before a real
    scope exists — is the single biggest trust-and-legal risk in the whole flow. Never do this.

### 2. The adjuster meeting
- **Who:** FilthE (present the whole walk), homeowner.
- **Papers:** `docs/guides/adjuster-meeting.md`, `docs/print/adjuster-meeting.pdf` (EN/ES one-pager),
  `data/supplement_items.json` (18-item missed-scope checklist).
- **Money:** $0.
- **Typical days:** adjuster contacted ~day 3-20 after filing; meeting ~day 7-30.
- **What can go wrong:** FilthE isn't present (insurer schedules directly with the homeowner and nobody tells
  him — worth a follow-up reminder in the app); only one elevation gets walked; chalking the whole roof instead
  of one 10×10 test square at a time (makes damage look artificially larger — InterNACHI's own correction, round
  43-verify — not "insurers reject it," the older, wrong reason); anything that sounds like negotiating the
  settlement (needs a public-adjuster license, 44-9204, confirmed round 43-verify) or promises what insurance
  will pay.

### 3. Claim approval + scope arrives
- **Who:** insurer (writes the scope), FilthE (reviews it same day against the checklist).
- **Papers:** `data/supplement_items.json` — compare line-by-line immediately, before signing anything.
- **Money:** the first (ACV) check often arrives around now too (see Step 5) — claims-101 groups them the same
  window.
- **Typical days:** ~day 14-45.
- **What can go wrong:** nobody checks the scope against the checklist right away, and a missed item (drip edge,
  starter strip, step flashing, ridge vent — all in `data/supplement_items.json`) gets discovered too late to
  fold into the same paperwork cycle, costing an extra round-trip.

### 4. Full contract signed (after approval) + the 3-day cancel, again
- **Who:** FilthE presents; homeowner (+ second buyer if any) and an HMP rep sign.
- **Papers:** `docs/print/contract-draft.html`/`.pdf` (EN), `docs/print/contract-draft-es.html`/`.pdf` (ES),
  `docs/print/cancel-notice.html`/`.pdf` (2 tear-off copies, buyer keeps one) — the 44-8607 deductible notice is
  section 12 of the contract, already built in.
- **Money in:** the contract's own payment schedule (§5): deposit at signing, materials-delivered milestone,
  work-complete milestone, final balance.
- **Typical days:** ~day 15-50 (same window the scope/first check land in, per claims-101).
- **What can go wrong:**
  - **Don't collect or deposit the signing deposit before the 3-day cancel window closes.** The contract's own
    §10 promises "HMP returns all payments within 10 days"; cashing a deposit and ordering materials inside the
    3-day window, then eating a cancellation, is a self-inflicted cash-flow hit. Treat "day 4" as the real green
    light to bank the deposit, not day 0.
  - Two boss answers block this step outright: **D14** (does HMP offer Class 4 shingles — affects §3 material
    choice) and **D15** (workmanship warranty years — §6 is blank without it; also blocks the homeowner screen
    and the completion certificate, per `docs/orders/roadmap.md`). Both already tracked; repeating here because
    they sit directly on Job #1's critical path.

### 5. The 44-8606 itemized description — to the homeowner AND the insurer
- **Who:** FilthE prepares and sends; this is a *separate* document from the priced contract.
- **Papers:** no template exists yet — build one (a one-page itemized work/materials/labor/fees/total sheet,
  reusing the contract's §2-3 scope fields). `docs/legal/44-8606.txt` is the exact statute text.
- **Money:** $0 — this is a disclosure step, not a payment.
- **Typical days:** any time before repair/replacement work *commences* — i.e., before tear-off, not before
  signing.
- **What can go wrong — this is the sharpest one-way risk in the whole runbook.** 44-8606 requires the itemized
  description go to **both** the insured and the insurer before work starts; **44-8608 voids the whole contract**
  on any violation of the Act. Round 39 already flagged this as an open, unconfirmed process gap ("confirm HMP's
  process actually sends a copy to the insurer, not just the homeowner") — as of this round it's still
  unconfirmed. This needs a hard-stop checklist item, not a habit to remember.

### 6. First (ACV) check + mortgage-company endorsement
- **Who:** insurer issues the check (to the homeowner, and to the mortgage company too if there is one — the
  policy's mortgagee clause); the *homeowner* calls their mortgage company's **loss-draft department** (a
  separate department from regular servicing) to start endorsement — not HMP directly, but FilthE should push
  the homeowner to start it same-day.
- **Papers:** claims schema's `mortgage{company, amount, check_sent, check_returned}` (already built,
  `.claude/skills/hailhunter-reference`).
- **Money in:** ACV = RCV − depreciation − deductible.
- **Typical days:** benchmarks.json's own figure: 1-3 weeks added to the timeline (round 15/18). This round's
  gap-fill (search-summary only, not independently opened — theroofcrafters.com and
  integrityroofingandpainting.com were both proxy/CDN-blocked) adds sharper detail worth treating as
  **directional, not verified**: claims under ~$15,000 reportedly endorse fast (lenders describe ~5 business days
  with an adjuster summary + photo ID); larger claims can go into an **escrow/draw** structure instead of a
  single endorsed check — released in stages (roughly a third at start, a third mid-job, the rest after a final
  inspection) — which can stretch the "1-3 week" figure to 4-8 weeks. If this holds, it changes Step 7's timing
  meaningfully on a bigger job. **Re-verify with a real lender once HMP has an actual case, not before.**
- **What can go wrong:** nobody starts the paperwork the day the check arrives (round 15's #2 named money leak);
  on a larger claim, HMP assumes a simple 1-3 week wait and gets caught by a slower escrow-draw structure instead.

### 7. Open a supplier account (ABC Supply / Beacon / SRS) + order materials
- **Who:** FilthE or the boss applies; the boss likely needs to be the one signing, since HMP itself has zero
  supplier payment history (today materials come from whoever hires HMP as a sub).
- **Papers:** none in the repo — an application task, not a print piece. Round 18 already flagged "apply for a
  supplier credit account before the first signed job" (T90) as unactioned; this round could not independently
  verify the application requirements — abcsupply.com and beaconbp.com were both blocked at the proxy this
  session, same failure mode as prior rounds. What's already known (round 18, unverified/vendor-sourced): all
  three offer **job-specific accounts** (a single-project credit line) as well as open net-30/60 company
  accounts; a job-account is described as the easiest first approval for a business with no payment history.
- **Money out:** materials, commonly cited at ~22-28% of revenue for a roof job (round 18, national/unaudited).
- **Typical days:** apply well before Job #1 is even signed, so the account exists by the time it's needed —
  this should not be a same-week scramble.
- **What can go wrong:** HMP orders materials before the ACV check clears and has no supplier credit to lean on,
  creating exactly the cash-flow risk round 18 named as the #1 profit leak (rank this above the sale itself in
  the pre-mortem below). A supplier may want a personal guarantee from the boss given HMP's clean/short payment
  history — a real ask-boss item.

### 8. Schedule a crew around current jobs
- **Who:** the boss — this is his call, not FilthE's.
- **Papers:** none; this is pure scheduling.
- **Money:** crew labor cost is unknown (ask-boss, see list below).
- **Typical days:** ideally locked before the contract is signed (§9's "estimated start/completion" shouldn't be
  a guess).
- **What can go wrong:** every crew is already committed to sub work (guaranteed pay, zero cash risk for HMP)
  and Job #1 — HMP's own capital at risk — keeps getting bumped for it. Also: cold-weather install floors matter
  now that Job #1 could land in the off-season (round 52/53, unchanged): asphalt shingles want ~40°F+ to
  self-seal, vinyl gets brittle under ~35-40°F, James Hardie ColorPlus needs ≥30°F to bond caulk/paint.

### 9. Pull the town permit
- **Who:** HMP (contractor) — Fremont's own rule requires the *contractor*, not just the homeowner, to be
  registered before pulling the permit.
- **Papers:** none printed yet (an application/registration task). Verified this round from what's already in
  the repo (round 39, `docs/legal/48-2100-contractor-registration-act.txt`):
  - **Fremont** (HMP's home base, confirmed direct from fremontne.gov): both roof-shingle and siding
    repair/replacement need a building permit + inspection. Separately, **Fremont's own city registration**
    (Council Resolution 2019-049 — $65 fee, $250,000 liability insurance naming the City as additional insured,
    proof of NE Dept. of Labor registration) is required on top of the state Contractor Registration Act, for
    anyone pulling a permit in city limits or its 2-mile zoning jurisdiction. **Unknown whether HMP/FilthE
    already holds this** — round 39 flagged it and it's still unanswered; ask-boss item below.
  - **Omaha:** commonly cited threshold of needing a permit above 2 squares (200 sq ft) — search-summary only,
    not independently opened (permits.cityofomaha.org and cityofomaha.org were both blocked again this round,
    same failure as every prior attempt).
  - **Lincoln:** has a dedicated "Re-Roof Policy" page; its content has never been opened directly in any round,
    including this one (lincoln.ne.gov returned an access-denied page to a direct fetch this round — blocked at
    the site's own CDN, not just the proxy).
  - **Columbus:** only the *door-to-door solicitation* permit/hours were ever researched (round 24); the actual
    **building-permit** requirement for roofing/siding work in Columbus has never been checked. Real gap.
  - **Verdict: Fremont is solid, the other three are not.** If Job #1 lands outside Fremont, a phone call to
    that city's building-permit desk before signing is cheaper than finding out mid-job.
- **Money out:** permit fee (amount not gathered this round — small, city-set).
- **Typical days:** pull before tear-off, ideally before or right at contract signing so §9's schedule is real.
- **What can go wrong:** skipping the permit is a straightforward code violation, and the permit number is
  itself one of the items adjusters/supplements expect to see documented (`data/supplement_items.json`'s
  code-item entries) — skipping it can cost money twice, not just once.

### 10. Dumpster, tear-off, install, photos, final inspection
- **Who:** the crew.
- **Papers:** the contract's §9 already commits HMP to daily cleanup + a nail-magnet sweep + full debris haul.
- **Money out:** dumpster rental (not separately priced in the contract sheet reviewed this round).
- **Typical days:** ~day 20-75 (claims-101's "we build the job" window).
- **What can go wrong:** missing before/during/after photos of *all four* elevations, not just the damaged
  side — round 5's finding that photographing the pattern proves the storm's direction, and it's also what
  feeds any later supplement or dispute; skipping the city's own final building-inspection sign-off (separate
  from anything insurance-side).

### 11. The completion certificate
- **Who:** FilthE, at the final walkthrough.
- **Papers:** `docs/print/completion-certificate.html`/`.pdf`.
- **Money:** none directly — but this is the event that should *trigger* Step 12.
- **Typical days:** the day the job finishes.
- **What can go wrong:** the completion date doesn't get logged anywhere durable (the claims schema has
  `completion_sent` and `install{start,done}` fields already built for exactly this — use them the same day, not
  "later").

### 12. Depreciation request + any remaining supplements
- **Who:** FilthE sends the final invoice + completion date the same day the job finishes.
- **Papers:** none built yet as a template — a one-page "job complete, please release depreciation" letter is a
  real, cheap gap to close (Designer/Code). Supplements (for anything the scope missed, caught in Step 3 or
  found during tear-off) go in writing with photos, within roughly the 180-day window most insurers accept
  (round 15/18, unaudited industry figure) — `data/supplement_items.json` is the checklist to work from.
- **Money in:** depreciation check (Check 2), the remaining RCV − ACV amount, once the insurer confirms the
  policy pays it back (claims-101: "No Check 2 if the policy doesn't pay depreciation back").
- **Typical days:** ~day 45-90 for Check 2 overall; the *request itself* should go out same-day, not wait.
- **What can go wrong:** this is round 15's single most-named money leak — "get the check, forget to ever ask
  for the rest." A job with no depreciation-request habit just quietly leaves money on the table with nobody
  noticing.

### 13. Final check received + closed out
- **Who:** FilthE confirms both checks deposited; updates the claims record's `paid` stage.
- **Papers:** claims schema, `stage: paid`.
- **Money in:** the remainder; deductible always comes from the homeowner directly, never HMP.
- **Typical days:** by day ~90 for a normal-size claim (bigger storms run slower, per claims-101's own footnote).
- **What can go wrong:** if a mortgage company is on Check 2 too, it may need its own (usually faster, since the
  job is provably done) endorsement round — don't assume it's automatic.

### 14. Yard sign, review ask, referral ask
- **Who:** FilthE, at or right after the final walkthrough.
- **Papers:** none printed as a single piece yet — reuses existing scripted lines.
- **Money out:** a yard sign (round 18, T92 — order some, cheap, still open).
- **Typical days:** same visit as Step 11/12, plus the review ask.
- **What can go wrong — a stale recommendation to correct:** rounds 15 and 18 both suggested a gift-card
  ($25-50) or free-service referral thank-you. **CLAUDE.md's current, later decision (D17, 2026-09-27) overrides
  that: a handwritten thank-you note only, no gift cards or money.** Anyone reusing round 15/18's script text
  verbatim would ship the wrong reward — flagging so it doesn't slip through on Job #1.

## Pre-mortem: "it's month 3, the job was signed but never got built or paid — why?"

Top 5 causes, ranked by how likely each is to actually happen given what's true about HMP today, with the fix:

1. **Cash got stuck on materials.** No supplier account existed yet, so HMP either fronted cash it didn't have
   or waited on a slow mortgage-escrow draw before ordering — and the job just sat. **Fix: open a job-specific
   supplier account (Step 7) before Job #1 is even signed, and make "ACV check deposited or supplier account
   approved" a hard gate before any material order — no exceptions, no fronting cash on a guess.**
2. **Crew never got a slot.** Every crew stayed on sub work (guaranteed pay, zero cash risk) and Job #1 — HMP's
   own capital on the line — kept losing the scheduling fight. **Fix: the boss names, in advance, which crew is
   "next up" for an HMP-owned job, before Job #1 is signed, not after.**
3. **The 44-8606 itemized-description-to-the-insurer step got skipped** (it's easy to send it to the homeowner
   and think that's the whole requirement) and the contract turned out void, or the omission surfaced during a
   dispute. **Fix: a hard-stop checklist item before tear-off — "itemized description sent to the insurer, proof
   kept" — not a habit to remember.**
4. **The mortgage-endorsement paperwork sat untouched for a week+** because nobody treated "the check arrived"
   as a same-day trigger, and the job's start date slipped past the crew's other commitments. **Fix: FilthE
   pushes the homeowner to call the mortgage company's loss-draft department the same day the check arrives,
   every time, no exceptions.**
5. **The depreciation ask (and the completion date it depends on) never got sent**, because nothing in the
   process forces it — the job "looks done" to everyone and the second check just never gets chased. **Fix:
   completion cert + invoice + depreciation request go out the same day the crew finishes, tied to logging
   `completion_sent`/`install.done` in the claims record right then, not "this week sometime."**

## Ask-the-boss (money facts unknown — write these as questions, not guesses)

- **Cash cushion:** how much can HMP float for materials before the ACV check (or a supplier account) is in
  hand, without straining the business?
- **Crew:** how many crews, what size, and which one is "next up" for an HMP-owned job vs. staying on sub work?
- **Supplier relationship:** does the boss already have a rep contact at ABC/Beacon/SRS from past sub jobs, or
  is Job #1 opening a cold account? Will the boss personally guarantee the first credit line?
- **Deposit terms:** what % deposit and what payment schedule does the boss want in the contract's §5, once the
  3-day-cancel timing question (Step 1/4) is settled?
- **Workmanship warranty years (D15)** and **Class 4 shingles (D14)** — both already open on the roadmap, both
  sit directly on Job #1's contract (§3, §6) and completion certificate.
- **Fremont city contractor registration** (Resolution 2019-049, $65 + $250k liability + NE Dept. of Labor proof)
  — does HMP or FilthE already hold it? Blocks Step 9 if Job #1 is in Fremont.
- **Who signs for HMP** on the contract and the contingency agreement — FilthE, or does the boss's signature/
  registration number need to be on it too?

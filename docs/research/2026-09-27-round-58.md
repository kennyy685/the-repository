## The details

### (a) Share of hail-damaged homes that never file a claim, and why

**No hard percentage found — every candidate source (Verisk, III, Kiplinger, Insurify, a COUNTRY Financial survey
cited third-hand) was policy-blocked to direct fetch this session.** What's usable:

- **VERIFIED (direct PDF fetch, doi.nebraska.gov, "Hail Damage: Does My Roof Need Repair?", OUT01121—Rev. 01/19):**
  Nebraska's own insurance regulator states plainly, in its own consumer brochure: *"If you believe repairs to your
  roof will cost less than the amount of your deductible, you may not want to file a claim. Some insurance
  companies will consider occurrences of wind and hail damage, even if no money was paid out, when they review
  whether to renew your policy."* That's two of the three reasons T192 asked about (deductible math, fear of a
  rate/non-renewal hit), confirmed straight from the state — no percentage attached, but it's a strong,
  on-point, primary source for Nebraska specifically.
- **Search-summary only, not independently opened:** a widely-repeated figure of "10% of homeowners who reported
  storm roof damage said they didn't file because their deductible was too high" turned up via WebSearch but its
  original source (a named survey) couldn't be traced or opened — treat as unverified. Several trade blogs
  (Truvo, roofmedic) describe hail as "the most under-claimed loss in homeowners insurance" and cite hail's poor
  visibility (a bruised, un-punctured shingle mat) as the "didn't notice" reason — same caveat, blocked to direct
  fetch.

### (b) How long out-of-town chasers stay, and when locals see the "second wave"

**Search-summary only — every candidate trade-blog source was blocked this session.** The consistent shape across
several independent summaries: chaser crews arrive within 24-48 hours of a big hail event, run an aggressive
door-knocking blitz for **2-4 weeks**, sign the bulk of their contracts in the **first 30-60 days**, then spend the
rest of the season actually building what they sold, and leave town **before the next hail season** (or before
warranty/workmanship complaints have time to surface). No source gave an explicit "local contractors see X% more
calls Y weeks after chasers leave" figure — that specific "second wave" claim is this round's own inference from
the dwell-time pattern, not a quoted stat: once the 30-60-day signing rush is over, the town isn't wall-to-wall
door-knockers anymore, but plenty of homeowners who hesitated, missed the first pass, or watched a neighbor's
chaser-signed job go sideways are still sitting on hail damage and an open (years-long) claim window. **This
matches HailHunter's own existing `hot_zones.compete_days: 90`** — the engine already assumes the crowded window
is roughly the first 90 days for its two watched cities, which is the right order of magnitude for what the trade
sources describe.

### (c) Nebraska's deadline to file a hail claim

**VERIFIED, reused from round 28 (direct `curl` fetch of nebraskalegislature.gov, 2026-09-26) — not re-verified
this round, per the "don't repeat verified work" rule:** Neb. Rev. Stat. **25-205(1)** gives 5 years to bring an
action on a written contract (including an insurance policy); **44-357** bars an insurer from writing a policy
that shortens that. Together: **no statutory deadline shorter than 5 years** to sue over a denied/disputed claim.
There is no separate, shorter "deadline to file" a first notice of loss in Nebraska law.

**New this round (verified, direct PDF fetch, doi.nebraska.gov, two of the Department's own consumer pieces —
"Hail Damage" brochure and "After the Storm: Before You Sign on the Dotted Line"):** neither document states any
claim-filing deadline at all. The only time figure in either is a **"usually 180 days"** window to *complete
repairs* after an adjuster's estimate, to collect the difference between actual cash value and full replacement
cost — that's a policy term about finishing repairs, not a deadline to file, and it's specific to
replacement-cost coverage. **Flag:** a WebSearch AI summary this round separately claimed "the 1-year filing
window applies to hail damage claims in Nebraska" — I could not find this in either DOI document I opened
directly, and it isn't consistent with 25-205/44-357's verified text. Treat that "1-year" claim as
**unverified and probably wrong**; it does not change round 28's verified finding above. Net for FilthE: same as
round 25/28 — there is no legal hurry, and using a false deadline as a sales pressure line would be both
inaccurate and against the spirit of 44-8604's rules against pressure tactics.

### (d) What this means for the engine — a concrete, config-only recommendation

Read first, no code touched: `hailhunter/zones.py`'s `hot_zone()` (the Hot Zones heat score used to rank which
walk/zone to drive to) and `hailhunter/config.py`'s `scoring` and `hot_zones` blocks.

**How storm age is scored today:**
- `todaywalk.storm_max_days` / `storm_max_days_by_month` (config.py ~L217-222) is a **gate**, not a score: in
  season a storm walk needs to be ≤60 days old; off-season (Oct-Mar) it can be up to 330 days old, with April/Aug
  as 120-day shoulder months (round 25's change). This decides *whether* an old storm's walk exists at all — it's
  already the right shape and this round found nothing to change here.
- Once a walk clears that gate, `hot_zone()` (zones.py) computes `fresh = interp(sc["recency_curve"], days)` —
  config.py's `scoring.recency_curve`: **`[[0, 1.0], [45, 1.0], [180, 0.7], [365, 0.4], [730, 0.1], [1095,
  0.05]]`** — a pure, monotonic decay. A 300-day-old storm (right in HailHunter's own off-season re-knock window)
  already scores well below a 45-day-old one, with no allowance for "chasers have left, so HMP has less
  competition and a still-wide-open claim window here."
- There's also `hot_zones.compete_towns: ["Omaha", "Lincoln"]`, `compete_days: 90`, `compete_factor: 0.85` — a
  flat 15% heat penalty applied only in those two towns while `days <= 90`, which **lifts to neutral (1.0) at day
  91** — it removes a penalty, it doesn't add a boost. This is the closest thing HailHunter already has to a
  "chasers are/aren't still here" signal, and it only fires for two hardcoded cities.
- Door-level scoring (`hailhunter/doorscore.py`) has **no storm-age factor at all** — a single door's score is
  identical whether its walk is 10 days or 300 days old, once the walk exists. The age effect lives entirely in
  zone-level heat (which zone to drive to), which is the right place for a "second wave" change to live.

**Recommended change (config-only, for the Engine Mechanic — not made this round):**
1. Replace `scoring.recency_curve`'s pure decay with a shape that dips through the chaser-crowded window and
   recovers through the second-wave window, before resuming a long-tail decay that isn't as steep as today's
   (since the real deadline is 5 years, not months): something like
   `[[0, 1.0], [45, 1.0], [90, 0.8], [150, 0.9], [270, 0.7], [365, 0.55], [730, 0.2], [1095, 0.1]]` — the exact
   knee points are a judgment call for the Engine Mechanic to tune (and to A/B against real door results once
   FilthE has some), but the shape should be: flat through the initial fresh window, a small dip around 45-90
   days (chasers actively signing, per (b)), a bump around 90-150 days (chasers gone, claim window still wide
   open, some homeowners still deciding), then a gentler long-tail decay than today's (365-day value raised from
   0.4 to roughly 0.5-0.55, since round 28/this round confirms there's no legal cliff at any particular month).
2. Generalize `hot_zones.compete_towns` beyond `["Omaha", "Lincoln"]` — e.g. apply the compete discount/relief to
   any town whose storm's average hail size cleared a size threshold (chasers follow big hail, not big
   population, per (b)'s sources), or at minimum add Fremont/Columbus/other towns HMP actually walks.
3. Leave `storm_max_days` / `storm_max_days_by_month` and door-level scoring alone — nothing found this round
   suggests changing the gate or adding an age factor at the per-door level; the "second wave" belongs in
   zone-level heat only.

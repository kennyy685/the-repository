## Bottom line — the door line FilthE can use now

**"How long do I have to report this?"** — say (EN): *"It depends on your policy, not on Nebraska law.
Nebraska doesn't set a legal deadline to report wind or hail damage — but your insurance company's own
policy does, and a lot of policies want it reported fairly soon after the storm. I'm not your insurance
company, so I can't quote your exact number — the safe move is to check your policy and get it documented
and reported soon, not wait."*

**(ES):** *"Depende de su póliza, no de la ley de Nebraska. Nebraska no fija un plazo legal para reportar
daño de granizo o viento — pero la póliza de su aseguranza sí puede tener uno, y muchas piden que se
reporte pronto después de la tormenta. Yo no soy su aseguranza, así que no puedo decirle el número exacto
— lo más seguro es revisar su póliza y reportarlo documentado y pronto, sin esperar."*

This is true, doesn't promise coverage, doesn't quote a number as fact, doesn't negotiate anything, and
matches Neb. Rev. Stat. Ch. 44-8601 to 44-8611 and the public-adjuster line (we describe damage, we don't
interpret the homeowner's contract for them). Same line as the cut-short draft, unchanged — nothing found
this round contradicts it.

## What's new this round (primary-sourced)

1. **Found the actual Nebraska regulation that touches notice-of-loss deadlines: 210 Neb. Admin. Code,
   Chapter 60 ("Unfair Property and Casualty Settlement Practices Rule"), §005.03.** Fetched the PDF
   directly from `doi.nebraska.gov` and read it with `pymupdf` (installed this round — `pip install
   pymupdf` worked fine, no network block on PyPI). Saved the relevant sections to
   `docs/legal/210-nac-60.txt`. Full text of §005.03: *"No insurer shall deny a claim based upon the
   failure of a first party claimant to give written notice of loss within a specific time limit unless
   the written notice requirement is a written policy condition, or the first party claimant's failure to
   give written notice after being requested to do so is so unreasonable as to constitute a breach of the
   claimant's contractual duty to cooperate with the insurer."* This is the state's actual rule on the
   subject: Nebraska doesn't set the day-count itself, but it does regulate *how* an insurer can use a
   late-notice denial — only if the policy itself has a real written time-limit clause, or the delay was
   unreasonable. This directly confirms and sharpens round 59's original correction (the deadline is a
   *policy* condition, not a state one) with an actual regulation instead of just the absence of a
   statute. §006-008 of the same chapter set the *insurer's* 15-day response clocks (acknowledge notice,
   start investigating, accept/deny after proof of loss) — those are the company's duties, not the
   homeowner's filing deadline; noted so nobody mixes the two up.
   **Caveat:** effective date on the document is 1992; Cornell LII and Justia's regulation mirrors (found
   via WebSearch, not opened directly — both blocked) show the same section numbers as currently indexed,
   which is a reasonable signal it hasn't been renumbered/repealed, but a repeal notice would only show up
   on `doi.nebraska.gov` itself, which I didn't separately search for. Treat "still in force" as likely,
   not 100% certain.
2. **NE DOI's "2024 Summary of Insurance Business in Nebraska" is now readable** (`pymupdf` fixed what
   round 59 flagged as unreadable). It has **no per-company market-share ranking for homeowners
   insurance** — it's a directory of every company licensed in NE plus one aggregate premium table. The
   one relevant number: **statewide Homeowners Multiple Peril premium written in Nebraska, 2024:
   $1,451,437,590** (total market size, not split by carrier). The "top ~8 Nebraska homeowner insurers by
   market share" question from round 59 is **not answerable from this document** and is still open — a
   real ranking would need NAIC's per-state market-share filings (`content.naic.org`, blocked all three
   rounds now) or a paid source. Not worth more research time until/unless that blocker lifts.
3. **Per-carrier notice-condition wording: still not independently verifiable, and this round found a

## What the app should show (new, this round's ask)

Don't show a countdown or a specific day number — that would look like Aldaba quoting the homeowner's
policy, which we can't verify. Instead, on the claims/notice screen, show:
- **Storm age** (days since the hail/wind date HailHunter already has), plain: "Storm was 43 days ago."
- A **neutral flag, not a number**, once storm age passes a rough threshold (recommend 60 days, since even
  the loosest carrier answers found this round start around 6 months and the tightest around 30 days — 60
  days is safely on the "say something" side of every number seen without inventing a false deadline):
  *"Many policies set a time limit to report storm damage — worth checking now."* Label it **"check your
  policy,"** never "deadline" or "you have X days left."
- Never auto-fill or guess a specific carrier's number even if the homeowner names their carrier — the
  contradictions found this round (same carrier, different sources, different answers) are the reason:
  a wrong specific number stated confidently is worse than a correct vague one.

## What's verified (carried over + this round)

- **Neb. Rev. Stat. 25-205(1)** and **44-357**: 5-year floor to sue an insurer, can't be shortened by
  policy — about the *lawsuit* deadline, not first-report. (`docs/legal/25-205.txt`, `docs/legal/44-357.txt`)
- **210 Neb. Admin. Code Ch. 60 §005.03** (new this round): the actual state rule on notice-of-loss
  denials — insurer can only use a late-notice denial if the policy itself has that written condition, or
  the delay was unreasonable. (`docs/legal/210-nac-60.txt`, direct-fetched and read this round)
- **NE DOI's consumer PDFs** (`HailDamage_0.pdf`, "After the Storm...") state no claim-filing deadline;
  the only number in either is "usually 180 days" to *finish repairs* after an RCV estimate — a different
  thing from reporting deadline.
- **NE DOI's 2024 Summary**: statewide Homeowners Multiple Peril premium = $1,451,437,590 (2024, all
  carriers combined); document has no per-carrier breakdown.
- **EraHub**: no Google Play Store listing found (searched directly), no G2 listing found, no Capterra
  listing found (WebSearch). Apple App Store unchecked (blocked). Still just a vendor blog claim about its
  own product.

## Watch out

- Nothing here touches deductibles, claims negotiation, or a promise that insurance will pay — clean
  against 44-8604 and public-adjuster rules.
- Don't let anyone quote "you have one year" (or any other specific number) as a Nebraska legal fact, or
  as a specific carrier's fact — every specific-number claim found this round or last came only from
  WebSearch AI summaries of secondary sources (law-firm blogs, comparison sites), never a primary policy
  document I could actually read, and the numbers contradict each other carrier-to-carrier and even for
  the *same* carrier across two searches. The door line above is the only thing to say out loud.
- The NE regulation (210 NAC 60 §005.03) is new and useful, but it's still a rule about how an insurer
  must behave when denying for late notice — it is not itself a homeowner-facing deadline, and shouldn't
  be paraphrased as one at the door.

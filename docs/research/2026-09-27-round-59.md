## Bottom line — the door line FilthE can use now

**"How long do I have to report this?"** — say: *"It depends on your policy, not on Nebraska law. Nebraska
doesn't set a legal deadline to report wind or hail damage — but your insurance company's own policy
does, and a lot of policies want it reported pretty soon after the storm, sometimes spelled out as
about a year. I'm not your insurance company, so I can't quote your exact number — but the safe move is
to get it documented and reported soon, not wait."* This is true, doesn't promise coverage, doesn't
quote a number as fact, and corrects round 58's mix-up (see correction note added to that file).

**Why this changes round 58's framing (the King's catch, confirmed correct):** Neb. Rev. Stat. **25-205**
(5-year floor) and **44-357** (insurers can't shorten it) govern the deadline to **sue** an insurer over a
denied/underpaid claim — a lawsuit deadline. They say nothing about the deadline to **first report** a
loss. That first-report deadline is a **policy condition** ("prompt notice," sometimes a stated window like
"within one year") set by each insurer's own contract, which Nebraska law doesn't limit or standardize.
Round 58's "no legal hurry" conclusion is still true for the *lawsuit* deadline, but was applied too
broadly — it read as "no hurry to report," which isn't accurate and needed the one-line fix below.

## What's verified this round

- **Neb. Rev. Stat. 25-205(1)**: gives 5 years to bring an action on a written contract (including an
  insurance policy). Read directly from `docs/legal/25-205.txt` (already in the repo, sourced
  nebraskalegislature.gov, round 28) — re-read and re-confirmed this round, not re-fetched.
- **Neb. Rev. Stat. 44-357**: bars an insurer from writing a NE policy that shortens "the regular period
  of time prescribed by the statutes of limitations of this state" for bringing an action. Same source,
  same re-confirmation.
- **Neither statute mentions a deadline to report/give notice of a loss** — both are about the deadline to
  sue. This is the King's correction, confirmed by reading the actual statute text just now, not inferred.
- **NE DOI's own consumer PDFs** (`HailDamage_0.pdf`, "After the Storm: Before You Sign on the Dotted
  Line," both fetched directly in round 58, re-cited not re-fetched) state no claim-filing deadline at
  all; the only number in either is "usually 180 days" to *finish repairs* after an RCV estimate, a
  different thing.
- **Reachability probe (this round, ~35 domains tried via `curl` and WebFetch):** `nebraskalegislature.gov`,
  `doi.nebraska.gov`, and `play.google.com` returned real responses. Every carrier site tried (State Farm,

  Farm Bureau Financial Services, Grinnell Mutual, EMC, Cincinnati), `content.naic.org`, `doi.nv.gov`

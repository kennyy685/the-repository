# Research round 62 (2026-09-28): who's actually insured, and where else are the leads?

FilthE's challenge, answered directly: "is this house insured" turns out to be close to the wrong
question (most homes already are), and yes, real people have built real tools for lead-finding outside
hail — most are either too expensive for HMP's stage, redundant with what the engine already does for
free, or legally the same restricted-data problem as CLUE. The useful new ground is below. No scout
subagent tool was available this session (same as rounds 38/45/48/51 — see `docs/research/backlog.md`),
so this round was done directly by the Research Lead with WebSearch/WebFetch, primary sources opened
where reachable, search-summary flagged where a domain was egress-blocked.

## Bottom line
1. **"Insured?" isn't the sharp question — "will the claim pay for a full roof?" is.** ~86-93% of US
   homes carry insurance already (Nebraska has no reason to be far off); the real, actionable gap is
   that more carriers (Nebraska's own included) now pay depreciated Actual Cash Value, not full
   replacement, on roofs over 10-15 years old — roof age (already in the engine) predicts payout size
   better than an insured/uninsured flag ever could.
2. **A recorded mortgage does mean near-certainly insured** (the lender requires and can force-place
   it) — that IS free and public per Nebraska county, but only one address at a time, never in bulk;
   there's a free AREA-LEVEL version sitting in the same Census pipeline the engine already uses for
   owner-occupied/income/age, and it isn't wired in yet.
3. **Nebraska's homestead exemption is confirmed NOT a general "owner lives here" flag** (65+/disabled/
   veterans, income-capped) — HMP's existing mailing-address-match proxy (`owners.py`) is already the
   right, broader one. No change needed; sanity check passed.
4. **Real leads beyond hail exist and are legal**: Google's pay-per-valid-call ads, Nextdoor's free
   business-reply tool, and referral relationships with independent insurance agents, solar installers,
   and realtors/inspectors are all real channels HMP isn't using yet — none of them need a data vendor.
5. **Most of the paid "roofing lead tech" HMP hasn't tried (PropStream, BatchData, ATTOM, Shovels.ai,
   BuildZoom, Angi, SalesRabbit DataGrid) is a "tested and cut" this round**: too expensive for a
   1-person start, redundant with what's already free at HMP's actual 6-county scale, or resting on the
   same FCRA-restricted data class as CLUE. Detail and reasons below, not a repeat of round 61.

## A. The honest answer: "which houses are insured?"

**Nationally, most homes already are.** LendingTree's 2024 study puts it at ~86% insured (12.2M of 86.6M
owner-occupied homes uninsured, ~14.1%); the Federal Reserve's own household survey puts uninsured lower,
~7% (so ~93% insured) — the two disagree on method but agree on direction: insured is the norm, not the
exception [1][2]. The uninsured are heavily concentrated in one group: **among homeowners with a
mortgage, only ~2% lack insurance; free-and-clear (no-mortgage) owners are about 7x more likely to go
without it** [3]. Nationally ~60.6% of owner-occupied homes carry a mortgage and ~39.4% are free and
clear (2024 ACS) — and the free-and-clear group skews old (54% of them are 65+) [4][5]. So the real,
useful split isn't "insured vs not," it's "mortgaged (near-lock insured, lead with insurance-claim
documentation) vs. free-and-clear (real chance of no coverage, lead with a cash-sale/financing pitch
instead)" — that changes which opening pitch FilthE uses, which is exactly the kind of thing this round
is testing for.

**Getting the mortgage signal, honestly:**
- **Per single address: yes, free.** Every county HMP cares about publishes a free public index showing
  the recorded instrument (Deed of Trust / Mortgage / Release / Satisfaction), grantor/grantee and date —
  no login, no fee, for the index itself. Dodge (Fremont), Washington and Saunders run through the shared
  "Nebraska Deeds Online" network; Douglas (Omaha), Sarpy and Lancaster (Lincoln) each run their own
  portal [6][7][8][9][10][11]. This confirms it's legal and free to sanity-check ONE address before a
  visit or appointment.
- **In bulk, for a whole door list: no, not free.** These are one-address search tools, not bulk
  downloads or an API. At door-list scale that means paying: PropStream ($99-199/mo, plus $5/document for
  the actual recorded deed image) [12], BatchData ($1,000+/mo API access) [13], or ATTOM ($89.99/mo+, or
  $499/yr for its Property Navigator tool) [14]. **Not worth it yet** — HMP's real footprint is 6
  counties, and the free area-level option below gets most of the value for $0.
- **Free at the AREA level, and not wired in yet:** Census ACS table B25081 ("Mortgage Status") reports
  the share of owner-occupied homes carrying a mortgage per block group [15] — the exact same free
  geography `hailhunter/nbhd.py` already pulls owner-occupied share (B25003), year built (B25034/35) and
  income (B19013, T163) from. Adding it is the same one-line pattern as the income field. This is this
  round's recommended build step (below).
- **Off-limits, already settled — not re-researched:** CLUE claims history and any credit-based "who's
  likely to have filed" data (the same data class SalesRabbit's DataGrid buyer-score leans on, see
  tested-and-cut) is FCRA-restricted to the insurer and the homeowner, no legal path for a contractor at
  any price. Round 61 section C already nailed this down; still true, still not worth a second look.
- **Homestead exemption, sanity-checked — confirmed NOT a general "lives here" flag.** It's only for
  owners 65+ (income-capped: ~$37k single / ~$43k married for full relief, sliding scale above),
  permanently-disabled non-veterans (also income-capped), and disabled veterans/surviving spouses (no
  income cap) [16][17][18]. Real, public, filed at the county — but a narrow, self-selected slice.
  **HMP's current proxy is already the right one:** the owner's mailing address on file matching the
  house's own address (`hailhunter/owners.py`) catches every owner-occupant, not just the ones old or
  disabled enough to have filed for the exemption. No change needed.
- **The more useful question: will the claim pay for a full roof?** Nebraska's own Department of
  Insurance says plainly: the adjuster calculates Actual Cash Value first and only pays the gap up to
  full replacement if the policy has that coverage; a claim can affect a future renewal decision;
  wind/hail often carries its own separate deductible; repairs typically must finish within 180 days
  [19]. Separately, more carriers nationally — and Nebraska specifically, per independent-agent sources —
  are adding **ACV-only roof endorsements once a roof passes 10-15 years old**, so the payout can shrink
  to a fraction of replacement cost right as a roof gets old enough to actually need one [20][21][22][23].
  Nebraska is also the **4th most expensive state for home insurance premiums** (Flatwater Free Press, a
  Nebraska newsroom — page itself egress-blocked this session, search-summary sourced) [24], which likely
  pushes some owners toward thinner coverage to save money. Net: **roof age (already in the engine)
  predicts a good insurance-restoration lead better than any insured/uninsured flag on its own, because
  it predicts payout size, not just coverage.** This is background/training material, not a customer
  script — never turn it into a promise about what a specific insurer will pay.

## Recommended first build step: one data layer

**Add Census ACS table B25081 (Mortgage Status) to `hailhunter/nbhd.py`'s existing ACS pipeline**, the
same way median household income (B19013, T163) was added — same geography, same join, same free source,
no new vendor, no new county integration. Output: a `share_mortgaged` field per block group, surfaced as
a plain "why" line ("~68% of homes here carry a mortgage — likely insured") next to the age/owner/value
lines door score v2 already shows, **never labeled "insured"** per the naming rule. Pair it with a small,
non-scraping helper: given one specific address the salesman is about to visit, a link that opens the
right county's free deed-index search (Nebraska Deeds Online for Dodge/Washington/Saunders, or the
county's own portal for Douglas/Sarpy/Lancaster) pre-filled where the site allows it, so a 30-second
manual check before a promising visit can confirm "mortgaged" or "paid off" for that one house — never a
bulk pull, never an owner name stored. This is the most honest, cheapest, most legally clean way to
answer FilthE's literal question inside the app.

## Tested and cut (real tools, not worth it yet — reasons, not a re-run of round 61)

- **PropStream / BatchData / ATTOM / DataTree bulk mortgage-lien data** ($99-1,000+/mo): sells, at a
  price, exactly what HMP's own `owners.py` already gets free for the counties that matter, plus a bulk
  convenience HMP doesn't need yet at 6-county scale. Revisit only if HMP expands outside eastern NE.
- **Shovels.ai / BuildZoom bulk permit data** ($500-2,000+/mo — one independent reviewer calls BuildZoom
  "overbuilt and overpriced" for a local contractor [25][26]): the useful part (who already re-roofed, so
  don't waste a knock) is available free from city permit portals (Omaha Accela, Lincoln's own site,
  already flagged round 61); Fremont's own online search is still an unconfirmed phone call, unchanged
  from round 61's backlog item — not re-solved this round.
- **Angi / HomeAdvisor paid leads**: each lead is resold to 3-8+ competing contractors, real cost-per-
  booked-job runs $500-1,250+, and HomeAdvisor paid an FTC settlement over lead-quality claims in 2023
  [27][28] — worse economics than Google LSA's pay-only-for-a-valid-contact model. Skip in favor of #5
  above.
- **SalesRabbit DataGrid** as a standalone buy ($19-31/user/month, needs a base SalesRabbit subscription
  HMP doesn't have): its "buyer score" is built on 280M individual records / 600+ data points including
  credit-capacity-adjacent signals [29] — the same restricted data class that makes CLUE off-limits, not
  a clean copy-the-idea target, and redundant with the engine's own free door score.
- **Any paid "who filed a claim" product** (CoreLogic/Verisk-style): doesn't exist at any legal price for
  a contractor — round 61 already confirmed this via the FCRA permissible-purpose rule; not re-investigated.

## Found but not verified this round — flag for next time
- **Omaha's public code-enforcement dataset explicitly tracks "deteriorated roofs and gutters"** as a
  named violation category [30][31] and a matching public GIS layer appears to exist [32] — potentially
  the single most direct, government-verified "this house needs a roof" signal found this round, ahead of
  every hail/age/owner proxy. `nebraskamap.gov` was egress-blocked to both WebFetch and curl this session,
  so the dataset's actual fields, access method and whether it's bulk-queryable are **unconfirmed** —
  worth a direct-fetch retry next round before building anything on it. Scope note: Omaha only, not
  Fremont/Dodge County, so lower priority for a 1-person Fremont start today, but worth having ready for
  when HMP's everyday-lead reach extends toward Omaha-adjacent towns (already in `everyday_towns`, T97).
- **Nebraska Deeds Online's exact free/paid boundary** (index free, but is a document image or a bulk
  export paywalled the same way as neighboring counties) is converging-but-secondary sourced — the site
  itself was egress-blocked this session; worth a direct fetch once egress allows.
